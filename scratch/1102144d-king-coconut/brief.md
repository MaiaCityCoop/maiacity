King coconut 'Thambili' · Cocos nucifera L. var. aurantiaca Liyanage (Rath thembili), Sri Lanka's orange-fruited intermediate (semi-tall) coconut palm.

THE PALM. An unbranched palm of intermediate stature: about 12 m tall when fully grown (between the 18 m tall coconut and the 10 m dwarf), slender and a little frail-looking, with a sparse crown. It is planted as a whole husked nut, and it first flowers at 5–8 years.

TRUNK. The trunk shows at about 4 years. It grows about 0.3–0.4 m a year when young and 0.1 m a year after 40 years. It is slender: 22–23 cm across at 1.5 m (71 cm round). It never thickens. At its foot it swells into a distinct root bole about 35–38 cm across, with a mat of thick orange-brown roots at the soil line. Its bark is light grey, smooth and pale, with leaf-scar rings all the way up: crescent scars 2.5–5 cm apart, closer near the foot and near the crown. The trunk rises straight or gently curved, often leaning a few degrees. Just under the crown the trunk is browner, with fibrous leaf-sheath cloth.

FRONDS. About 25–32 living fronds make a round, rather open crown about 7–8 m across. Each frond is pinnate and 4.3 m long when grown, its petiole about a quarter of that. It carries about 200 short, narrow, glossy dark-green leaflets, 60–100 cm × 2–4 cm, held in a shallow V. The midrib (rachis) is ORANGE-YELLOW on its upper side; this is the king coconut's mark. The youngest fronds stand upright in the middle. The middle ones arch out. The oldest hang down yellow-brown before they fall. About 15 new fronds come a year.

FLOWERS AND NUTS. From every leaf axil comes a boat-shaped spathe, then a branched cream flower spike, then a bunch of about 10 nuts (8–15) on a stout orange stalk. About 15–18 bunches a year means a dozen bunches hang at once at every age, below the crown. The nut is BRIGHT ORANGE (golden orange when young, orange-brown when ripe), smooth and glossy, ovoid and faintly three-sided, with a small button-like point at its tip. It is 21 × 13.5 cm and weighs about 1.2–1.6 kg as a tender nut. Cut across, it shows a cream-white husk 2–3 cm thick, a thin brown shell, a thin white kernel (12 mm) and clear water.

AGES. The palm grows from the nut as follows:
- Seed nut: an orange husked nut lying half sunk.
- Seedling: pleated undivided leaves from the nut.
- Juvenile, 3 years: a fountain of 2 m fronds, no trunk.
- Ripe, about 7 years: a 2 m trunk under a full crown with its first bunches.
- Bearing palm, 10 years: 3 m of trunk, about 7.5 m to the frond tips.
- GROWN PALM, 22 years: a slender grey ringed trunk 6–6.5 m tall, about 9.5 m to the frond tips. The tall coconut palm people know, but slimmer and less tall than a tall coconut.
- OLD PALM, 50 years: a tall bare grey trunk about 11 m, 14 m to the frond tips. Its crown is smaller, with shorter fronds; it still bears.

LAYOUT:
Row 1 — the ages standing on one ground line, soil cut away so the roots and bole show, all at one scale (metres), left to right: seed nut; seedling (1 year); juvenile (3 years); ripe (7 years); bearing palm (10 years); grown palm (22 years); old palm (50 years). The grown and old palms are tall: draw them whole from roots to frond tips, not cut off at the top.
Row 2 — the parts laid flat: one grown frond whole (4.3 m, its orange rachis seen from above); one leaflet both sides; the trunk cut across (22 cm, fibrous, no rings inside); a 30 cm swatch of grey ringed bark from the middle of the trunk and one from just under the crown; the root bole with its roots, washed.
Row 3 — a bunch of ripe orange nuts on its stalk; one opened cream flower spike with its spathe; the nut at four ages side by side (button, young golden 3 months, tender orange 7 months, ripe orange-brown 12 months); the nut cut lengthwise; a 24-patch colour checker.
