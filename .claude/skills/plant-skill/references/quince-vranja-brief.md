Cydonia oblonga Mill. 'Vranja' (Vranjska dunja, 'Vranja Nenadović', Riesenquitte von Vranja; the Serbian pear-shaped quince) — the big golden pear quince of southern Serbia, ripe in late October. A small deciduous tree of the rose family, grafted onto a quince seedling rootstock.

Pip and seedling: the pip is a glossy red-brown to dark brown seed, 9 × 5 × 2.5 mm, obovoid and wedge-shaped, flattened on its faces, pointed at one end; wet, its coat turns white and slimy. Germination is epigeal: the hypocotyl comes up as a pale green hook 3–5 cm tall and lifts two oval fleshy bright-green seed leaves, 12 × 7 mm; the first true leaves are small (1.5–3 cm), round-ovate, entire, dull green above and white-woolly beneath; the seedling stem is reddish and downy.

Wood and bark: a small, upright, dense, rounded tree, 3.5 m tall and 3 m across in its eighth year, on a SHORT trunk: it branches LOW, at 0.5–0.7 m, into three to five crooked, upright then spreading limbs, and the crown's leafy skirt comes down to about a metre from the ground — a bushy small tree, NOT a lollipop standard on a bare stem. The one-year shoots are purplish red to purple-brown, zig-zag, grey-felted toward the tip; two- to six-year wood is smooth purple-brown to grey-brown with few lenticels; the old trunk (8–10 cm across at eight years) is grey-brown, slightly twisted and fluted, and flakes in thin plates 3–10 cm across that fall and leave smooth patches of orange-brown, tan and grey-green: a mottled trunk. A small step of the bud union shows 15–20 cm above the soil.

Leaf: alternate, ovate to broadly elliptic, 9 × 6 cm (8–11 × 5–7), base rounded, tip short-pointed, margin ENTIRE (no teeth), the blade a little concave; upper side dull dark green, smooth; underside densely grey-white felted, the 6–8 pairs of side veins raised; stalk 1–1.5 cm, felted; large leafy round stipules with gland-toothed edges at the base of young leaves. Unfolding leaves silvery-white with wool. Autumn leaves clear yellow.

Flower: in late April–May, after the leaves, ONE flower alone at the tip of each short new leafy shoot (10–15 cm, 5–8 leaves), sitting almost without a stalk among the last leaves; 4.5–5 cm across, a shallow cup of five broad, overlapping, rounded petals (18 mm), dark pink in bud, opening pale pink with a pinker edge, nearly white at the centre; about 20 stamens with yellow anthers on pale filaments, five free pale styles woolly at their base; a felted green bell-shaped hypanthium; five green felted sepals with gland-toothed edges, bent back.

Fruit: a large pome, pear-shaped with a distinct neck: ripe 100 mm long and 84 mm wide (90–110 × 70–90 mm), about 400 g, widest in its lower third toward the calyx, the neck short and broad toward the stalk, the surface lumpy, with five low ribs round the calyx end; the five sepals persist, dry and brown, bent back in a medium hollow at its tip; it sits almost stalkless (5 mm) at the tip of its short shoot among the last leaves. Young it is wrapped in thick grey-white felt; the felt thins as it swells and rubs off at ripeness, leaving a smooth faintly greasy golden-yellow skin with felt left in the hollows. By age: just set (2 cm, a small grey-white felted club, its green sepals spread at the tip and bigger than it), green (5–6 cm, July, long and narrow, green under grey felt), full-sized yellow-green (September, felt thinning), ripe golden yellow (late October, about 175 days after full bloom). Flesh pale yellow, hard and gritty; five locules in a star, each with a stack of brown seeds in white mucilage.

Roots: fibrous and shallow, most in the top 40–50 cm, spreading wider than the crown; a few suckers at the stem base.
