# Juglans regia L. 'Franquette': facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for a walnut tree grown stage by stage, from the nut to a 25–40-year-old bearing tree, in a
walnut orchard of the lower Isère valley (AOP Noix de Grenoble: Grésivaudan, Chambarans, Bièvre). Built on the
template `datasheet.md` and set out like `plum-hauszwetschge.md`. Where grafting or the way the tree is grown changes a
number, the change has its own line (section 2 and "Grafted or not" under section 3).

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured or described on 'Franquette' itself, or on one of its selections ('Scharsch Franquette', 'Moyer
  Franquette', 'Graves Franquette', "S. Franquette" in the Davis tables).
- **[SP]**: said of *Juglans regia* in general, of another *J. regia* cultivar (named), or of the genus.
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with the reasoning given. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a search snippet, or a page that cites the work, not the original.

Each number has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are millimetres for small organs,
centimetres for leaves and trunks, metres for trees, grams, days and years. **Ages count from sowing the nut** (the
seedling rootstock) unless a row says otherwise. "DAFB" means days after full female bloom (stage Ff2).

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** The classic late walnut of the Isère: an old local selection of the Dauphiné (said to come from
  Notre-Dame-de-l'Osier, Isère, S33 [2nd]), one of the "three endemic varieties" with Parisienne and Mayette on which the
  appellation was recognised in 1938 (S1). It makes **about 90 %** of the Noix de Grenoble (S35 [2nd]) and is the
  variety "produced mostly in the area" (S1). It went to California in the 1870s with Felix Gillet and was the
  principal walnut of Oregon (S9, S10). "The name Franquette has been applied to a number of selections made over the
  years by different growers but they all have certain traits in common" (S9).
- **Its marks.** Very late leafing ("the standard for late leafing in California", S9; "late" in the CPVO/UPOV scale,
  S4; "très tardif" in FiBL's table, S5), protandrous (male before female, S4, S8), **terminal-bearing** (fruit buds only
  at the apex of one-year shoots, S4, S9, S11), one or two nuts per terminal (S9; infructescence "solitary", S4), a
  strong semi-upright tree (S4, S5), slow to come into bearing (7–8 years, S12). Nut medium, elongated with a long
  pointed tip, light thin firm shell, very light kernel (S3, S4, S10).
- **Why it is grown.** Its late budbreak escapes spring frost and much of the bacterial blight (S9, S10); quality and
  light kernel; it is the AOP's base. Its weak points: late and modest yields (S9, S10, S5), protandry that in warm
  springs leaves the late female flowers without pollen (S10), and nut size that drops in heavy years (S10).
- **Rules for the model.** Model one grafted orchard tree on a *J. regia* seedling rootstock, at **10 × 10 m**, in full
  sun. Fruit comes only at the tips of the strongest one-year shoots, one (rarely two) nut each; catkins only on the
  lateral buds of one-year wood. The young tree is a monopodial cone with clear apical dominance; once it bears, every
  fruiting tip stops and is relayed by one to three buds below it, so the crown forks and rounds into a broad dome
  (section 3). Keep nut size ±10 % and dates ±7 days between trees; a sown (ungrafted) seedling is a different
  genotype (section 2).

## 2. How it is planted (grafted or not)

| Way | What it is | Source |
|---|---|---|

| Planting density | **≥ 100 m² a tree, at least 8 m between trees** (Franquette, Mayette, Parisienne planted after 25 Feb 2002; Fernor 48 m² and 6 m) (S1); 10 × 10 m = 100 trees/ha is the standard, 12–15 × 12–15 m (39–69/ha) for very large crowns (S5); "about 30 feet" (9 m) in Oregon (S10) | [CV] S1, S10; [SP] S5 |

**What changes between a grafted tree and a sown seedling (for the growth driver)**

- Height and girth: the sown seedling is ahead by about a year in the first 4–6 years (no graft check, a stronger
  leader); by 15–25 years the two are within the range below. Use **+10–15 % height up to year 8** for a seedling.
  [EST]
- First nuts: grafted Franquette **7–8 years** after planting (S12; a few nuts at 5–6 years, commercial production from
  about 10 years, S10); a sown seedling **8–12 years** from the nut (S16 minimum 8). [CV] S12, S10; [SP] S16
- Catkins: grafted trees carry catkins a few years after the first female flowers (Scharsch Franquette "bears catkins at
  a younger age than regular Franquette", S9); a seedling flowers male later still. [CV] S9; [EST]
- Graft union: a slight swelling or a change of bark texture 15–25 cm above the soil on grafted trees; none on a
  seedling. [EST] from S5's planting depth
- Nut: Franquette's (section 6) only on the grafted tree. A seedling's nut is drawn from the species' range (round to
  oblong, 25–40 mm, shell thin to thick, S14, S20). [SP]

### Growth rhythm

- **One main flush a year** on grown trees, a second (summer) growth unit on young vigorous axes: annual shoots are
  made of 1–3 growth units, each one uninterrupted extension; the spring unit is preformed in the winter bud, the summer
  unit preformed in the spring bud (S22). On adult trees "monocyclic shoots are usually flowering, the bicyclic and
  tricyclic mostly vegetative" (S36 [2nd]). [SP]
- **Season (Isère):** budbreak (stage Cf) **19–26 April** (Chatte, La Buissière, Cras; 2021 and 2022) (S7); shoots
  extend May–June; in Franquette the main extension ends with the terminal bud by **late June–July**; a second flush on
  young trees in July–August. [CV] S7; end of growth [EST]
- **Annual shoot length:** young trees **~30 cm** the first year after planting, then **50–100 cm** (S5); Franquette's
  one-year shoots average **one third longer** than those of the intermediate 'Zdole-59' and lateral-bearing 'Fernor'
  (S31 [2nd]); 16–18-year Franquette, uncut control trees: **15 cm** average annual branch (S13). Use **60–100 cm**
  on the leader to year 8, **30–50 cm** on scaffold tips at 8–15 y, **10–20 cm** on bearing shoots of a grown tree.
  [CV] S13, S31; [SP] S5; use [EST]

### Branching and the year-by-year architecture

- **Habit.** Semi-upright (S4, class 2 of 3). In free growth young *J. regia* is conic with clear apical dominance;
  later the main stem forms large branches and a globular crown (S17). Light-demanding and strongly phototropic at all
  ages: branches lean toward light and gaps (S17, S19). [CV] S4; [SP] S17, S19
- **Buds.** Walnut has **supernumerary (accessory) buds** stacked one above the other in a leaf axil (S23); the lower,
  smaller one is a reserve. Franquette's bud is **triangular** (S4); terminal buds of the species 6–9 mm, downy,
  brownish (S21). Branching may be immediate (sylleptic) on vigorous shoots, with a long first internode (hypopodium)
  (S23). [CV] S4; [SP] S21, S23
- **Fruit buds.** Franquette is a **terminal bearer**: mixed buds (a leafy shoot ending in female flowers) only at the
  apex of one-year shoots (S4: "at apex of one year old shoot"; S9: "not fruitful on lateral buds"; S11). Lateral bearers
  flower three years earlier than terminal bearers (S36 [2nd]). Franquette produces "one or two nuts per terminal" and
  yields less than Hartley because it branches less and has fewer terminals (S9). [CV]
- **Year by year (for the growth driver)** [EST, from the above]:
  - **Year 1 (seedling)**: one stem, 30–50 cm, a terminal bud set in late summer.
  - **Year 2 (graft)**: a whip from the graft; at planting cut to ~1 m; 3–5 scaffold shoots from the top buds at
    **40–60° from the vertical** (the breeders' target of 30–60° insertion for semi-upright walnuts, S6), spread round
    the stem 15–30 cm apart in height; the leader trained up.
  - **Years 3–7**: leader +60–100 cm a year; scaffolds +50–80 cm; side shoots acrotonic (the top buds of each annual
    shoot grow out), so each year's growth makes a whorl-like tier near the top of the previous one; lower small shoots
    die back in the shade; the clear stem lifted to **1.5–2.0 m** by pruning (FiBL: the stake ends 10 cm below the
    future crown base, S5). No flowers yet.
  - **Years 7–10**: first female flowers at the tips of the strongest outer shoots, **1 (–2)** flowers each; that tip
    stops; **1–3 relay shoots** grow from the buds just below the spent inflorescence (S22) and carry on, so each
    fruiting tip forks. First catkins on one-year wood a year or two later.
  - **Years 10–25**: the fork-and-relay repeats on every bearing tip: the crown broadens and rounds; the outer shell of
    shoots carries the crop; inner shoots weaken and die; scaffolds thicken to 20–35 cm and bend out to 50–70° under
    their own weight and the crop.
  - **Year 25 on**: a broad, open dome; heavy low limbs; water sprouts from the top of bent limbs; dead twigs inside;
    the crop only in the sunlit outer metre of the crown.

### Phyllotaxis

- Leaves **alternate**, spiral (S28 [2nd] and every flora). Divergence not measured for *J. regia*: use **2/5
  (144°)**, with ±8° jitter. [EST]

## Gaps

These rows have no published number for 'Franquette' (nor, sometimes, for the species). Each is marked [EST] above with
how it was estimated.

- **Height, trunk diameter (at 30 cm and at 1.3 m) and crown width by age (1–40 y).** No age series for any open-grown
  *J. regia* orchard tree was found, let alone Franquette. The table is fitted between the seedling (S17, S16), the
  8-year open-grown figure (8 m, 20 cm, S17 [2nd]), the 7-year 'Gillet' (S24), forestry MAI of 1–2 cm a year (S17, S18),
  the crown/DBH ratio of Hemery et al. (S17 Table 1), the 16–18-year Franquette crowns cut at 3.5 m radius (S13) and
  the RHS mature size (20 × 15 m, S29 [2nd]). The 30-cm/1.3-m diameter ratio (1.15–1.25) is an estimate.
- **Sown seedling vs grafted tree differences** (+10–15 % height to year 8, later first catkins) are estimates; only
  the minimum seed-bearing age (8 y, S16) and Franquette's 7–8 years to bearing (S12) are published.
- **Hallé–Oldeman model.** No source read names it for *J. regia*. Rauh's for the young tree is inferred from the
  monopodial rhythmic growth (S17, S22, S23); the sympodial adult crown from terminal female flowering and relay axes
  (S22). Barthélémy, Sabatier & Pascal (1995) and Sabatier's thesis (1999) would settle it; neither was readable.
- **Phyllotaxis 2/5.** "Alternate" only; the divergence angle is an estimate.
- **Leaf sizes of Franquette.** Only the leaflet shape ("medium elliptic", S4) is published for the cultivar. Lengths
  come from the floras and 'Gillet' (S24). Flush colour and autumn colour come from nursery pages (S28 [2nd]); leaf
  colour hex from 'Gillet''s Munsell values.
- **Catkin number per shoot, winter catkin bud size, colour sequence.** Not found; estimates. Catkin length and
  diameter are of the species and other cultivars (S14, S24, S25 [2nd]).
- **Female flower size of Franquette, stigma intensity note.** Species and 'Gillet' values only (S15, S16, S24).
- **Isère male flowering dates.** Only the Davis 2006 dates are measured for Franquette's pollen (S8); the Isère window
  is shifted to fit the Isère female dates (S7).
- **Husk size and shape of Franquette, and the fruit-growth-by-week numbers.** No measured Franquette husk. Husk = nut
  + 5–6 mm a side (S24 for husk thickness); the weekly sizes scale the Californian curve (S11) to Isère dates.
- **Husk lenticel size, stalk length, fruit colours as hex.** Estimates.
- **Germination timing, cataphylls and first leaves of the seedling.** Genus-level (S16) and an abstract (S27) only.
- **Nut colour and shell texture as hex.** Words only ("light", S3, S10).
- **Yield of a single grown Franquette tree at 25–40 years.** Only per-hectare figures (S1, S5, S35 [2nd]), one 18-year

- **Bark ridge and fissure sizes, lichens, trunk clear-stem height.** Estimates.
