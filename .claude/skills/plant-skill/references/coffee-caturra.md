# Coffea arabica L. 'Caturra' (red Caturra, Caturra Vermelho): facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for a coffee bush grown stage by stage from the parchment bean through germination ("soldier",
then "butterfly"), the nursery, the young field plant, first flowering, and a bearing bush in flower, in fruit set and
in ripe cherry. Built on the template `datasheet.md` and set out like `sapodilla-alano.md`.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured on or said of Caturra itself.
- **[SP]**: said of *C. arabica* as a species, or measured on another cultivar. The cultivar is named in the row:
  Bourbon, Typica, Catuaí (Caturra × Mundo Novo, which carries Caturra's *Ct* dwarf gene), var. Colombia and
  Costa Rica 95 (both Caturra × Timor Hybrid, Caturra-type dwarfs), Castillo, Rubi.
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with how I made it. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a search snippet, a summary page or a review citing the work, not the original.

Each value has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are metres, kilograms, days and
years; small organs in mm or cm. Ages run **from sowing** unless a row says "after planting" (planting out of the
nursery bag into the field, about 7–8 months after sowing in Colombian practice) or "dpf" (days after peak
flowering/anthesis).

**What is measured on Caturra itself.** The original IAC description (Krug, Mendes & Carvalho 1949, S2, read in full
from the scan) gives Caturra's leaf (200 leaves), fruit and seed dimensions side by side with Bourbon, its height at
11 years and its branch angle. Cenicafé (Colombia) and a Costa Rican study (S8) measured Caturra's height, crown,
branch pairs, branches, nodes, fruits per branch and fruit mass by days. Days from planting to first flowering and to
first harvest, and from flower to ripe fruit, are measured on Caturra (S6). What is *not* measured on Caturra:
germination and seedling sizes, stem diameter by age (Catuaí stands in), flower size, cut-cherry layer thicknesses and
the peaberry share.

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** A natural single-gene mutation of red Bourbon. The dwarf habit is controlled by one dominant gene,
  **Ct** ("the short stature of Caturra is controlled by a single dominant factor"), and the short stature comes from
  **reduced internode length on both orthotropic and plagiotropic axes** (S3 [2nd], S2). WCR: stature
  "Dwarf/Compact", leaf tip colour "Green", bean size "Average", yield potential "Medium", ripening "Average",
  nutrition requirement "High", first production "Year 3", planting density 5,000–6,000 plants/ha single-stem (S1).
- **Origin, with dates.**
  - WCR: found in Minas Gerais, Brazil, **between 1915 and 1918**; IAC began selecting it in **1937**; never officially
    released in Brazil; to Guatemala in the 1940s, then common in Central America; a parent of the Catimors (S1).
  - The IAC description (S2): also called **"café Nanico"** (dwarf coffee). The agronomist Benvindo Novais
    (Ministry of Agriculture, Vitória, Espírito Santo) found it on the farm São João of Hildebrando Martinho de
    Carvalho, in the municipality of Siqueira Campos, **Espírito Santo**. He already saw two forms, red- and
    yellow-fruited, and sent seed of each under the local name "Caturra". The seed reached IAC in **November 1937**
    (No. 477 red, No. 476 yellow) and was planted in Campinas in 1938. It "probably originated" in the Manhumirim
    region of **Minas Gerais**, next to Espírito Santo. In 1945 the authors found two Caturra plantations there, 12
    and 7 years old. First IAC harvest of the selections was 1940 (S2).
  - Caturra Amarelo (*xanthocarpa*) "seems to differ from Caturra Vermelho only in the yellow colour of the fruits",
    the recessive *xc xc* (S2). **We model the red one.**
- **Names.** Caturra, Caturra Rojo (Spanish), Caturra Vermelho (Portuguese), café Nanico (S2). The yellow form is
  Caturra Amarelo / Caturra Amarillo.
- **Why it fits a dome.** About 2 m tall even at 11 years (S2); crown ~1.5–1.9 m (S5, S20); bred for dense planting.
  Colombia grows it in full sun at **10,000 plants/ha (1.0 × 1.0 m)**, where Valencia reached the potential yield
  (S26, S28); WCR gives 5,000–6,000/ha (S1). It was the variety that started intensive coffee growing in the Americas
  (S10).
- **Rules for the model.**
  - Main stem (orthotropic) with **opposite pairs of plagiotropic branches at every node, each pair at right angles
    to the one below** (decussate). Branches never become a new stem. Flowers and fruit only on the plagiotropic
    branches, in the leaf axils (S4, S30).
  - **A node flowers once, when about one year old, and never again** (S4, S27). So fruit sits on last year's wood, a
    band moving outward along the branch and upward on the plant year by year; old inner nodes are bare.
  - Short internodes, dense secondary branching and a **cylindrical** bush (S2): it reads as a dense green column,
    not a cone.
  - Cherries cluster tightly round each fruiting node, several ages and colours on one branch at once (S4, S8).
  - Young flush is **green** (bright light green), never bronze (S1, S2, S10). Bronze tips would make it Typica.

- **Growth tempo.** Height ~0.5–0.6 m a year in years 1–3 at 1,250–1,600 m in Colombia, slowing after
  (S5, S27). Growth rate is greatest near **21 °C** and falls to nil below ~10 °C and above ~32 °C (S6). Cool sites
  are slower: from planting to first harvest takes ~525 d at 21.5 °C and ~590 d at 19.5 °C (N = 1288 − 36 Tm) (S6).
- **Maximum growth.** The plant "reaches its maximum growth at 8–10 years" (S4).
- **Node pairs on the stem.** 43 pairs at 30–43 months (S5) → **~14 pairs a year** in years 1–3 [EST from S5,
  counting from the first branch pair at node 6–7]. Model **12–18 pairs a year**, two flushes a year in Colombia
  (S25) or one main flush with the rains elsewhere.
- **Main-stem internode.** **3.95 cm** mean in Caturra vs **5.65 cm** in Typica and 3.33 cm in San Bernardo
  (S5, Table 1). I checked that S5's column equals height ÷ branch pairs (170/43 = 3.95; 260/46 = 5.65), so it is the
  **mean internode over the whole stem**, not a measured single internode. Krug: "the internodes on the main stem
  are short and the bush is cylindrical, particularly in plants 4–5 years old" (S2). Model **3–4.5 cm**, the first
  6 seedling internodes shorter (1–2.5 cm) [EST].

## Gaps

These rows have no published number for Caturra, and most have none for the species either. Each is marked [EST] above.

- **Height before 30 months and after 43 months**: interpolated between the measured 330 d / 550 d phenology dates
  (S6), Cenicafé's 1.70 m at 30–43 months (S5) and Krug's 2.0 m at 11 years (S2).
- **Stem diameter by age**: nothing measured on Caturra. The only measured point is Catuaí (same *Ct* gene) at
  43 months: 48.6–55.2 mm at the collar (S20). Earlier values are interpolated from the seedling hypocotyl (2 mm,
  S15); later ones extrapolated at 0.5–0.8 cm a year. Ø at 30 cm taken as ~80 % of the collar value.
- **Crown width before 30 months and when mature**: interpolated or bounded by S5, S9, S20.
- **Node pairs per year on the stem**: from 43 pairs at 30–43 months (S5), not a measured rate.
- **Main-stem internode**: S5's 3.95 cm is height ÷ branch pairs (a mean), not a single internode. Seedling internodes
  (1–2.5 cm) are an estimate.
- **Plagiotropic internode**: S8's 0.94 nodes/cm cannot be the primary axis alone; 2.5–4 cm is estimated from a
  regrowth branch (S31 [2nd]) and the stem's Caturra/Typica ratio.
- **Branch angle**: three Caturra measurements disagree (66°, 88.7°, 28–40° by stratum, S2, S9, S10); their reference
  axes are unclear. The model angles are my reconciliation.
- **Branch growth per year**: from one season's 16.5 % length gain on a fruiting branch (S8).
- **Secondary branches per primary**: only "particularly abundant" is published (S2).
- **Parchment dimensions**: from the bean size plus shell and slack. Bean thickness is a species value (S4).
- **Peaberry share**: not found for Caturra; ~5 % is a round estimate.
- **Sowing orientation**: no source says flat face down; Colombian germinators scatter the seed.
- **Hypocotyl at emergence and at the first true pair, bean-cap duration**: from BBCH dates (S4, S11, S12) and the
  butterfly-stage hypocotyl of Costa Rica 95 (S15).
- **Seedling leaf size, leaf-pair rate in the nursery, size at transplant (20–30 cm, 6–8 pairs, Ø 4–5 mm)**: estimated
  from Brazilian nursery data on Catuaí (S20, S32 [2nd]) and Colombia's longer nursery period.
- **Cotyledon colour, all hex colours**: estimated from the published words (dark/intense green leaf, light-green
  flush, white corolla, green → yellow → red → purple-black fruit, beige parchment, grey-green bean, grey fissured
  bark).
- **Under-leaf colour, leaf cupping, vein count**: IAC counts 7.2 (S2), floras 9–11 pairs (S18); 8–10 chosen.
- **Branch-plane leaf twist**: the familiar flat spray, not stated in a source I read.
- **Flower size**: Caturra flowers are only "a little smaller" than Bourbon's (S2); the 18–20 mm is reduced from the
  species' ~2 cm. Pedicel length estimated.
- **Flowers per node (8–16)**: from inflorescences × flowers per axil (S4, S23) and Caturra's post-set fruit count
  per node (S8).
- **Cherry stalk (3–5 mm), the disc at the fruit tip, cut-cherry layer thicknesses**: estimated from S2's
  "short-pedunculate", the rudimentary calyx, and the fruit and bean dimensions.
- **Cherries per fruiting node (~3) and per stem (~2,400)**: computed from S8's branch counts.
- **Yield per plant in kg cherry**: computed from S5's parchment yield with a cherry : parchment ratio of ~5 : 1.
- **Root depth**: no Caturra numbers (S21 is photographic); all depths are from other cultivars and secondary sources.
- **Young stem shape and bark colours**: bark is only described for the species, in secondary sources (S33).
- **Second-hand values ([2nd])**: S3 (Carvalho et al. 1984, abstract), Suárez's Caturra fruit periods and Trojer's
  215–250 d (read only as cited in S7 and S6), Valencia 1998 and Briceño & Arias 1992 (cited in S8), S22 (roots),
  S29 (abstract), S30 (Roux's model via CIRAD GreenLab and SpringerPlus), S31, S32, S33, S34.
- **Not reachable or not read.** World Coffee Research's printed catalogue (I read the live Caturra page, S1). Wintgens
  (*Coffee: Growing, Processing, Sustainable Production*), Wrigley (*Coffee*, 1988), PROSEA 16, Cannell (1985), León &
  Fournier (1962, *Turrialba* 12: 65–74), Fournier's 1961 IICA thesis, the IPGRI/Bioversity *Coffea* descriptors and
  ICAFE (Costa Rica) guides: none could be opened. SciELO (the full Carvalho et al. 1984 paper) blocked the download.
  Page 158 of S2 is missing from the SciELO scan. Lécolier et al. 2009 (*Trees* 23: 1043–1051) was not open access.
  Salazar, Orozco & Clavijo's Caturra vs Colombia yield-component study (Cenicafé) was not found online.
