Musa acuminata Colla (AAA Group, Red subgroup) 'Red Dacca' (syn. Red, Morado, Red Jamaica, Cuban Red) — the red banana. A giant herb, not a tree: its "trunk" is a pseudostem of tightly rolled leaf sheaths on an underground corm. Sterile and seedless: planted as a sword sucker cut from its mother. A TALL, vigorous plant, much taller and heavier-stemmed than a Cavendish, with a long cycle (about 13 months to shooting, almost 6 months more to ripe).
Pseudostem: 4.5–5 m to the throat when it shoots, a thick column 0.37 m across at its foot and about 0.25 m at 1 m, tapering little. Its skin is a glossy WINE-RED to maroon-purple all the way up (greener only on the shaded side), under a faint frosty waxy bloom, with irregular black-purple blotches near the top where the petioles join; its outer dead sheaths dry red-brown and papery at the foot.
Leaves: 10–13 standing at once, held on LONG RED petioles 0.5–0.6 m long, grooved, with thin purple-brown winged margins; blades 2.4–2.9 m × 0.7–0.9 m (more than three times as long as wide), oblong with a blunt tip, dark green above, paler and glaucous grey-green below, the midrib a pale yellow-green ridge above and PINK-RED to red-purple underneath; fine parallel veins at right angles to the midrib; the blades arch and droop at their ends and many are torn into strips by wind; the new leaf comes up rolled as a green cigar on a red petiole; old leaves hang down brown and dry along the stem. The whole plant reaches 5.5–6.5 m to its leaf tips.
Inflorescence: shoots from the throat about 13 months after planting on a long, finely hairy PURPLE peduncle (over 0.6 m) that arches out of the crown, and the bunch then hangs straight down. The bud is ovoid (egg-shaped, not a long cone), 23 × 10 cm, purple-brown under a frosty bloom; its bracts lift one at a time and roll back, red to red-purple inside, fading pale at the base, then FALL OFF. Below the female hands the male rachis is BARE, knobbly, with very prominent bract scars, ending in the bud: no tail of dry bracts.
Bunch: 20–30 kg, only 5–7 hands, 15–19 fingers a hand in two rows, about 85–95 fingers. The fingers are very FAT and blunt and nearly STRAIGHT (little upward curve), round in section, 16–21 cm long and 4.5–5 cm thick, a short stalk and a dry black style at the tip. Colour by age: just set, glossy red-purple; filling, dark purple-maroon, dull with a bloom, greenish on the shaded side; full, deep maroon to purple-brown; ripe, brick red to coppery red-orange with yellow-orange in the creases, dark brown-black flecks when over-ripe. Peel thick (5 mm). Cut across: pale salmon-orange to apricot-cream flesh, three faint segments, a distinct core with tiny brown aborted seed specks.
Corm and roots: corm about 35–40 cm across, dark red-brown outside, cream inside, with white cord roots 5–8 mm thick turning brown, spreading wide in the top 40 cm; tall red suckers rise close beside the mother (a sword sucker has narrow upright sword leaves on a red cone of a stem).
