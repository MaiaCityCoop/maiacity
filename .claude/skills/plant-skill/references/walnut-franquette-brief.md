Juglans regia L. 'Franquette' (Persian or English walnut; noyer commun, the classic walnut of the Isère valley, AOP Noix de Grenoble) — a large, spreading, deciduous nut tree of the walnut family (Juglandaceae), late to leaf out, bearing its nuts at the tips of its shoots, grown in orchards on a seedling of its own species.

Nut and seedling: the nut (the stone, husk off) is elongated and egg-shaped with a pointed tip, 39 mm tall, 32 mm wide and 31 mm thick (37–40 × 30–33 mm), about 10.5 g; a strong raised pad along the upper two-thirds of the seam, the shell light straw-brown to pale tan, thin (1.2–1.4 mm), rather smooth with shallow wrinkles and a fine raised seam (the suture) right round it from base to point; the kernel inside is pale cream-gold with a thin light skin, in two brain-like wrinkled halves. Sown in autumn 5–8 cm deep, it splits along the suture in spring: a thick white taproot goes straight down, the two fleshy seed leaves stay inside the shell under the ground (hypogeal), and a stout shoot comes up bent; its first leaves are small, reddish-bronze, with only 3–5 leaflets. A seedling in its first summer is a single stout stem 20–40 cm tall with a few pinnate leaves; its taproot is already longer than the shoot.

Wood and bark: a broad, rounded, spreading tree, 12–15 m tall and as wide when grown (to 20 m), on a short, stout, straight trunk of 1.5–2.5 m clear stem that parts into a few thick, wide-angled scaffold limbs; the crown a big open dome down to 3–4 m above the ground. Shoots are thick (8–12 mm), olive-brown to greenish-grey, smooth, dotted with pale lenticels, with large heart-shaped leaf scars (each like a monkey's face, three bundles of dots) and a fat, rounded, dark grey-brown downy terminal bud; along last year's wood stand the male buds, small cone-like, scaly, grey-green. Young bark (branches and trunks up to ~15 cm) is smooth, silver-grey to ash-grey. Old trunk bark is grey to dark grey, cut by deep, long, narrow fissures into an interlacing net of flat, rough ridges (a diamond pattern); the cut trunk shows thin grey bark, pale cream sapwood and dark chocolate-brown heartwood.

Leaf: alternate, pinnate, 25–35 cm long (to 45 on vigorous shoots), with 7 (5–9) leaflets: the terminal leaflet the largest (12–15 × 6–8 cm, on its own short stalk), the pairs below smaller toward the base (the lowest 5–7 cm), elliptic to oblong-ovate, shortly pointed, the margin entire (smooth, not toothed), almost stalkless on the rachis; the stalk (petiole) 5–8 cm, thick, swollen at its base; upper side smooth, mid to dark green, a little glossy, the side veins (10–15 pairs) fine and pale; underside paler, matt grey-green with small tufts of hair in the vein angles. Young leaves unfold bronze to purplish red-brown and limp, turning green in two weeks; in autumn yellow then brown-black, falling early. Crushed, they smell resinous.

Flowers: one tree carries both sexes, the male first (protandrous). Male catkins come from lateral buds on last year's wood before the leaves: green, stiff and short at first, then pendulous, 8–12 cm long and 1.2–1.5 cm thick, of many small green flowers with yellow-green anthers, turning brown and falling after shedding pollen. Female flowers come singly or in pairs (Franquette mostly one) at the tips of the new shoots of the year, among the young bronze leaves: each a small green flask 5–7 mm long and 3–5 mm wide, finely hairy, crowned by two curled, feathery stigmas 4–6 mm long, yellow-orange when ready, browning after.

Fruit: the nut grows inside a green husk: just set (May–June) a smooth hairy green bead 1 cm; by July full size, egg-shaped 50 mm long and 42 mm wide, the husk about 6 mm thick, smooth, bright to olive green, densely dotted with small pale lenticels, its tip a little pointed with the dry stigma scar; in late September the husk yellow-browns, darkens and splits irregularly into 3–4 parts, the nut falls out, the husk turns black and stains. Ripe in early to mid October, about 135–150 days after the female flowers (late September to early October). Cut lengthwise a green fruit shows the thick green husk, the pale shell (still white and soft in July) and the white kernel with its brown skin forming.

Roots: a strong taproot from the first year, deep (2–3 m), and wide spreading lateral roots further than the crown; old roots dark brown with thick, rough bark.
