# Dacryodes edulis (G.Don) H.J.Lam var. edulis (safou): facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for a safou tree grown stage by stage from seed to ripe fruit. Built on the template `datasheet.md`
and set out like `avocado-fuerte.md`. The safou has **no named clonal cultivar**. Nearly every tree is a seedling, and
seedlings vary a great deal from tree to tree (S3, S5, S9, S10). The model is therefore "a typical cultivated
large-fruited var. *edulis* tree of the humid lowlands of Cameroon and south-east Nigeria". It may be either a seedling
tree or an ICRAF/IRAD marcott (air-layer) of a selected mother tree. Section 1 says which numbers belong to which.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured on var. *edulis* as cultivated: farm, market, ICRAF or IRAD trees in Cameroon, Nigeria or Congo.
  Each such source names its measured population: village, locality and number of trees.
- **[SP]**: said of *Dacryodes edulis* in general, in a flora or a review that does not name a population.
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with the reasoning given. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: read only in an abstract, a search snippet or a review that cites the work, not the original. A measured
  value read only in an abstract is tagged **[CV][2nd]**.

Each number has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are metres, kilograms, days and
years. Ages run **from sowing** unless a row says otherwise. "From weaning" means from cutting a rooted marcott off the
mother tree.

## 0. The tree in one paragraph

- **What it is.** An evergreen, dioecious fruit tree of the Burseraceae from the Gulf of Guinea. It is cultivated and
  naturalised from Sierra Leone to Angola and inland to Uganda (S1, S2). It is a home-garden, cocoa-farm and
  crop-field tree:
  - present on 50–100 % of farms in south-east Nigeria and on 80–94 % of farms in the humid lowlands of Cameroon (S1);
  - 99 % of 100 sampled trees at Mgbuisi, Nigeria, had been planted; 57 % of them stood in home gardens (S10 [2nd]).
- **The two varieties (Okafor 1983, S17, read via S1 and S2).**
  - **var. *edulis***: fruit "large, elongated, cylindrical, usually more than 5 × 2.5 cm", pulp "about 3.5–9 mm"
    thick. The tree "often has whorled branching, the branchlets stout and ascending". PROTA calls its branching
    "often verticillate or subverticillate".
  - **var. *parvicarpa* J.C.Okafor**: fruit "small, rounded or more or less conical, usually less than 5 × 2.5 cm",
    pulp 2–3.5 mm. Branching bifurcate or opposite, the branchlets slender and drooping.
  - [SP] S2, S1; Okafor's own paper [2nd] S17.
  - The wild forest populations have small fruits with a large stone, about the size of *Canarium schweinfurthii*
    (S3). The farmers' selection has moved the cultivated trees toward var. *edulis*. Leakey et al. (2004) put
    Cameroon's safou "between Stages 2 and 3 of domestication", with a 67 % relative gain in flesh depth (S18 [2nd]).
- **Its marks.**
  - A low-branched tree with a deep, dense, rounded crown and a short, straight, slightly fluted bole (S1, S2, S6).
  - Glossy imparipinnate leaves. The new flushes are rose-violet with anthocyanin (S4).
  - Grey, aromatic bark that weeps whitish resin drops. The genus name is from Greek *dakruon*, a tear (S2).
  - Olive-like drupes that ripen from pink to blue-violet or near black, with a thin skin and a buttery, pale green to
    cream flesh (S2, S6, S9).
- **Rules for the model.**
  - Rauh's model, grown flush by flush (section 2).
  - Trees are male, female or (rarely) hermaphrodite. Only female trees carry a full crop, so the fruiting model is a
    female tree.
  - One seed per fruit (often none at the start and end of the season, S3, S6).
  - Every tree differs (fruit length varies about 3.6-fold between trees in one village set, S9), so pick one set of
    means and vary per tree.

## 1. Seed, seedling and marcott

A safou tree in a field is either:

- **a seedling** (the traditional way: direct-sown or raised in polybags); or
- **a marcott**: a branch of a selected female mother tree, ring-barked and rooted in a sleeve of substrate while still
  on the tree, then cut off ("weaned"). It keeps the mother's fruit and sex, and it fruits ~18 months to 2 years after
  weaning.

The seed stage below is a seedling's.

**Marcott (air layer) on the mother tree.**

- **Branch.** A well-ripened branch 2–5 cm across (S5), or 1.5–2 cm (S6). A ring 4–6 cm long is barked between two
  cuts and wrapped in black earth, compost or moss in a plastic sleeve (S5, S3, S6).
- **Rooting.** Roots in **4–6 months**, with 80 % take (S1, S5); 6–8 months in Côte d'Ivoire (S6).
- **Weaning.** Cut 5 cm below the sleeve (S5). The weaned plant is a leafy branch ~0.3–0.5 m long (S3, Fig. 4: "30 à
  50 cm") [CV].
- **Nursery.** The marcott is potted, then planted out in a resting phase (S3).
- **Fruiting.** It **fruits 18 months after weaning** (Silou, photo of 18-month marcotts in fruit; Kengue's manual,
  Fig. 7d). IRAD's technique "reduces the time to production to 2 years" [CV] S5, S3.

- **Habit.** "A beautiful tree with a very straight trunk and spreading crown" (Bourdeaut; Silou). "Generally branched
  from low down, with a deep, dense crown"; "bole rather short, slightly fluted … more or less sinuous", no buttresses
  (Orwa). "Crown much-branched, dense"; bole "straight and cylindrical, often shallowly fluted and low-branching"
  (PROTA). [SP] S6, S5, S2, S1
- **Crown shape for the model.**
  - Dense, **rounded to broadly ovoid**, about as wide as tall in the open; H/W ~1–1.3 when young, falling to ~1 when
    mature [EST].
  - A clear bole of only **0.5–2 m** in cultivation (pruned at 0.5 m, or low-branched by nature) [CV] S3; [SP] S2.
- **var. *edulis* branching.** Tiers of **3–5 stout, ascending branches** (whorled or subverticillate) from the top of
  a flush [SP] S1, S2, S17 [2nd]. The angle is ~40–60° from vertical, curving up at the tips [EST: "ascending";
  orthotropic branches of Rauh's model].

### Growth rhythm, branching, phyllotaxis

| Row | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Growth rhythm | **Rhythmic: flushes separated by rests of the apical bud** (an endogenous rhythm, not synchronised between seedlings). A **growth unit** (one flush) holds 0–3 cataphylls at the base, then the assimilating leaves, often 1–2 abortive leaves at the top. A unit can be simple (leaves only), branched (with sylleptic shoots) or flowering (with inflorescences). The cataphylls fall early, leaving a **bare section with scars** at the base of each unit; beside the abortive leaves at the top of the previous unit, this marks every stop and restart on the stem. Seedlings flush vigorously; "older trees flush infrequently". The adult population **flushes and flowers in the dry season** (Dec–Feb north of the equator). Leaf-miner damage to the young leaves abolishes the rest and gives continuous growth. Use **~4–6 flushes a year on seedlings** (~7 leaves per 4 months in Kengue's controls) and **1–2 a year on bearing trees** | [SP] S4, S1; flush counts [EST] from S4 Table I |
| Shoot growth | seedlings: ~0.4–0.6 m in the first 6 months (S4). A Nigerian study reports ~0.05 m a month for nursery seedlings (S22 [2nd]). Use **0.6–1 m a year to year 3, 0.5–0.8 m a year to year 10, then 0.2–0.3 m a year** [EST], fitted to the height table | [CV] S4; [2nd] S22; [EST] |
| Leaves per flush | seedlings 5–9 leaves in 4 months, i.e. ~2–4 assimilating leaves per flush [EST: ~2–3 flushes in 4 months]. Bearing shoots ~4–8 leaves per flush [EST] | [CV] S4; [EST] |
| Branching | **Sylleptic**: shoots in the leaf axils of the flush now growing (the normal way, giving tiers). **Proleptic**: shoots from buds of old units, after defoliation or pruning. Natural branching starts late on some seedlings (at 1–2 m+), which is why the stem is topped at 0.5 m or the terminal bud or the last flush is removed; this "readily" triggers 2–4 laterals below. var. *edulis*: **whorled / subverticillate tiers of stout ascending branches**. var. *parvicarpa*: bifurcate, slender, drooping branchlets. Young branches carry a dense coat of **rust-brown (ferruginous) stellate or dendroid hairs** | [SP] S4, S3, S1, S2, S17 [2nd]; laterals per cut [EST] |
| Phyllotaxis | **alternate, spiral** (PROTA: "leaves alternate"), clustered toward the tips of each flush. The first two seedling leaves come together (S14). No angle is published: use **2/5 (144°)** | [SP] S1, S14; angle [EST] |
| Internodes | seedlings 4.9–6.1 cm (cluster means, Nigeria). Shorter at the flush base (cataphyll zone, 2–5 mm) and much shorter after leaf ablation. Bearing shoots 1–3 cm [EST] | [CV] S16; [SP] S4; [EST] |
| Roots | a **strong taproot**. It develops before the laterals: at 8–9 months in the nursery "the taproot is still almost the only root", and it breaks through the bag if left. The trees start holding soil against erosion "from 4–5 years, when the taproot system is well developed". Not prone to root rots (no *Phytophthora* recorded), unlike avocado. Use a **taproot to ~1.5–3 m** and laterals in the top 0.3–0.5 m out to ~1–1.5 × the crown radius [EST: no depth published] | [CV] S3; depths [EST] |

## 4. Flowers

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Sex | **dioecious**, with exceptions. Trees are male, female or hermaphrodite; males may bear a few hermaphrodite or female flowers and so a little fruit. Female:male ratio about **1:1** in seedling orchards (Gabon, Cameroon). An orchard needs **~5 % males** for pollination. Nigeria: female, male and hermaphrodite trees seen in a 3-year survey of 60 trees | [SP] S1, S2, S3, S5; [CV] S13 [2nd] |
| Where borne | **axillary panicles near the shoot tips**, with cymose branches, densely rust-brown (ferruginous) stellate-tomentose. Flowers often in clusters of 3 surrounded by bracts. Often the whole shoot tip becomes floriferous with reduced leaves, so it **looks terminal**, but the apical bud stays vegetative and flushes again. Bracts 3-lobed, caducous. Female trees flower "inconspicuously" but bear heavily and regularly; male trees flower showily | [SP] S1, S2, S3 |
| Panicle size | **up to 40 cm** (PROTA); axis **10–42 cm or longer, deeply grooved** (Orwa). Measured in south-east Nigeria: **inflorescence length 14.8–18.7 cm** (means by state and year), 4.2–6.6 inflorescences per panicle, **21–110 flowers per inflorescence**, the longer ones holding more flowers | [SP] S1, S2; [CV] S13 [2nd] |
| Size, stalk | **~5 mm across**, fragrant; pedicel up to 5 mm | [SP] S2, S1 |
| Every whorl: count, shape, colour | **3 sepals**, almost free to the base, 3–6 mm, **brown, ferruginous-tomentose outside**. **3 petals**, free, 5–6 mm, incurved at the tip, tomentose outside, **cream-yellow inside** ("brun foncé avec l'intérieur des pétales jaune"). **6 stamens**, white, slightly shorter than the petals, the filaments broadening toward the base (small and sterile in female flowers). **Disc annular, 6-lobed** (nectar). **Ovary** superior, ovoid, 2-celled with 2 ovules in each cell, glabrous; style very short; stigma 2–4-lobed (vestigial in male flowers) | [SP] S1, S2, S6, S5 |
| First flowering | **seedlings: juvenile period 4–6 years** (PROTA); "5 to 6 years" (Silou); "5–6 years" (Ecocrop via S19). Nigerian farmers reported first fruit from **3 to 22 years, mean 9.4 years** (100 trees). **Marcotts: 18 months after weaning** (Silou); "2 years" (IRAD) | [SP] S1, S5, S19 [2nd]; [CV] S10 [2nd], S3, S5 |
| Season | **dry-season flowering**. North of the equator the peak is in **January–February** (PROTA); "January to April" (Orwa). In Cameroon flower buds broke in **January on 93 % of trees in 2012**, and in **February on 80 % in 2013** (Foumban, Makenene, Njombe, Yaoundé). South-east Nigeria: December to March (2013–15). South of the equator: August–September. Each tree flowers for **about 1 month**; the population for about 3 months. Bimodal rainfall can trigger a second, minor bloom. 57–77 % of Nigerian trees flower every year; the rest every 2–3 years | [SP] S1, S2; [CV] S12, S13 [2nd] |
| Pollination and set | flowers open in the morning and **pollen is shed within 1–2 hours**. Insect-pollinated, **~80 % of visitors bees** (honey valued). Strong scent, sticky pollen, nectar. Pollen viability excellent; no incompatibility. Hand pollination raises set but not the harvest. Many female flowers drop before set, and many fruitlets drop early. **Embryo:** first zygote division **~day 15** after anthesis; most of the embryo forms **days 15–40** | [SP] S1, S2; [CV] S8 |

## Where it grows (for worlds)

| Row | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Climate | humid tropics, Gulf of Guinea to the Congo basin. **Altitude sea level to 1,000 m** (PROTA, Orwa), but grown in Cameroon's western highlands to **1,100–2,000 m** (Foumban). The five Cameroon sampling localities lay at 80–1,200 m. **Rain 1,400–4,000 mm** (Orwa); the Cameroon localities had 906–2,900 mm (Makenene 906, Obala 1,577, Kekem 1,832, Njombe 2,900, Boumyebel 2,809). ## Gaps

These rows have no published number for var. *edulis*, or I could not read the primary source. Each is marked [EST]
above with how it was estimated.

- **Kengue (2002), the ICUC monograph *Safou, Dacryodes edulis* (Fruits for the Future 3, 147 pp.).** It was not
  reached:
  - its URL (cropsforthefuture.org/publication/Monographs/Safou Monograph.pdf) now serves an unrelated site;
  - the Wayback Machine failed through the proxy.
  - Kengue's own extension manual of 2003 (S3) and his Fruits papers (S4, S7, S8) were read instead.
  - The monograph probably holds the measured seedling, height and phenology data (Kengue 1990 thesis) that would
    replace several estimates below.
- **Seed thickness, hypocotyl length and cotyledon size and colour on the seedling.**
  - Seed length, width and mass are measured (S11).
  - Thickness, hypocotyl length (4–8 cm) and the cotyledon size and colour above ground are estimates.
  - Cotyledon life: they are known to be present at 2 months (S14); the fall at 3–5 months is estimated.
- **The first true leaves.** Only that "the first two leaves" come together is published (S14). That they are an
  opposite pair of pinnate leaves with 1–3 leaflet pairs comes from the genus and the flush rule, not from a
  description of *D. edulis*.
- **Height and trunk diameter by age.** No paired age–height–DBH data were reached:
  - the ICRAF Minkoameyos progeny trial (S22) is behind a paywall or Cloudflare;
  - the Mbalmayo 2002–2012 dataset on the ICRAF Dataverse (doi:10.34725/DVN/ZIBQ32) has restricted files.
  - The table is built from these anchors:
    - 6-month seedlings at ~0.4–0.6 m, read off Kengue's figure axis (S4);
    - the pruning threshold of 3 cm at 0.5 m (S3);
    - crowns closing at 8–10 m after 5–8 y (S3);
    - the ≤ 12 m plantation ceiling (S2);
    - the Nigerian range of 4–22 m, DBH 9.55–63.65 cm, ages 5–64 y (S10, unpaired).
  - All the intermediate values are estimates, and so are the marcott heights.
- **Crown width by age and the branch angle.** Estimated from the spacing advice and from "ascending".
- **Flushes per year on grown trees, leaves per flush, internodes on grown shoots, phyllotaxis angle (2/5).**
  Estimated from Kengue's seedling counts (S4) and "older trees flush infrequently" (S1).
- **Root depth and spread.** Only "a taproot that develops first" is published (S3). The depths are estimates.
- **Whole leaf length (30–50 cm) and typical leaflet size (10–16 × 4–6 cm).** Only maxima are published (S1, S20).
- **Leaf life (12–24 months).** Not published; it is evergreen with "heavy and continuous" leaf fall (S5).
- **Fruit growth curve in mm by day.** S12 gives phase durations and times to maximum but its curves (Fig. 3) were not
  digitised. The mm values at 14–120 d are fitted estimates.
- **Fruit colour sequence and bloom.** "Pink when young → blue-violet or black when ripe" is published (S6, S1). The
  green-pink start, the patchy darkening and the bloom are estimates.
- **Pedicel length, fruits per infructescence, fruit count per tree.** Estimated (section 5).
- **Leaf and bark colour hex values, young bark colour.** From descriptions, not measurements.
- **Second-hand values ([2nd]).**
  - Waruhiu et al. 2004 (S9), Anegbeh et al. 2005 (S10), Njoku 2022 (S13) and Leakey et al. 2002/2004 (S18): read as
    abstracts only.
  - Okafor 1983 (S17): via S1 and S2.
  - Ecocrop (S19), the Korup flora (S20) and Tchatchoua et al. 2012 (S22): read as snippets.
