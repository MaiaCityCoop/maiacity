Prunus domestica L. subsp. domestica 'Hauszwetschge' (Hauszwetsche, Gemeine Zwetsche, Bauernzwetschge; the German damson-plum "Zwetschge") — the old blue plum of German farm orchards, ripe in September. A deciduous tree of the rose family, grown from its stone or from a root sucker, or grafted.

Stone and seedling: the stone is long and flat, pointed at both ends, 19–23 mm long, 9–11 mm wide, 6–7 mm thick, light brown, finely rough, a keel down its ventral edge; it germinates after a winter's cold; the seedling's first true leaves are small, toothed and elliptic on a reddish-green stem.

Wood and bark: an upright tree 5–7 m tall with a steep, oval-pyramidal crown on a straight trunk; limbs leave the leader steeply (30–45° from upright) and later spread under fruit. This year's shoots are smooth, hairless, dull red-brown on the sunny side and olive-brown in shade, with small pointed buds; two- to four-year wood is grey-brown with small pale lenticels and many short spurs (fruiting spurs, 1–5 cm, knobbly with buds); the old trunk is dark grey-brown to almost black, fissured into small rough scaly plates, sometimes with grey-green lichen.

Leaf: alternate, elliptic to obovate, 6–10 cm long and 3.5–5.5 cm wide, widest above the middle, tip short-pointed, base wedge-shaped, margin bluntly double-toothed (crenate-serrate); upper side dull dark green and quilted (the veins sunken, rugose), underside paler grey-green and softly hairy along the veins, which stand out; 6–9 pairs of side veins; stalk 1–2 cm, often with two small glands near the blade; young leaves fresh light green, autumn leaves yellow with orange.

Flower: in April, with the first leaves, one or two from each bud on the spurs and last year's shoots; 18–25 mm across, five rounded white petals (a hint of green at the base), five green sepals that bend back, about 25–30 stamens with yellow anthers round one green style; flower stalk 1–2 cm, green.

Fruit: a drupe hanging on a stalk 1.5–2 cm; ripe it is an elongated oval 40–45 mm long, 28–32 mm wide and 25–28 mm thick, about 20–30 g, slightly lopsided (one side straighter), a faint suture down one side, narrowed toward both ends, its tip blunt-pointed; skin dark violet-blue to blue-black under a dense pale blue-grey waxy bloom (where the bloom is rubbed off the skin is shiny deep purple); flesh golden-yellow to greenish-yellow, firm; it comes clean off the stone (freestone). By age: just set (1 cm, green, the dry calyx and stamens still round it), green and oval (2.5–3 cm, July), colouring (violet-red flushing over green, early August), ripe blue (mid-September, about 145 days after full bloom).

Roots: a taproot early, then spreading main roots in the top 60 cm, the bark of old roots dark brown; root suckers come up near the trunk.
