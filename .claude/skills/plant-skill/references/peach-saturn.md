# Prunus persica var. platycarpa 'Saturn' (flat, "doughnut" peach): facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for a flat peach grown stage by stage, from a stone or from a grafted nursery tree, open-vase
trained. Built on the template `datasheet.md` and set out like `banana-dwarf-cavendish.md`.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured on 'Saturn' itself, under any of its names: 'Stark Saturn' (Stark Bros.' trade name), N.J. F-2
  / NJF2 (its New Jersey selection number), 'Saturne', 'Donut'. Values from **'Rattray'** are marked **[CV-sport]**.
  'Rattray' is a limb sport of Saturn whose patent says it is "identical in all other vegetative and fruiting
  characteristics to the Saturn peach of commerce", apart from yellow flesh (S3).
- **[SP]**: said of the species *Prunus persica*, or of flat peaches as a group (other flat cultivars, Saturn's own
  offspring).
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with the reasoning given. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a snippet, a search summary or a nursery claim, not the measured original.

Each number has a source tag such as S1; the references are at the end. Units are metres, millimetres, grams and days.
"DAFB" means days after full bloom. Ages of a grafted tree run from planting the nursery tree. Ages of a seedling run
from sowing.

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **Origin.** 'Saturn' came from the cross 'Pallas' × selected seedling 602903, made in 1968 by L. F. Hough and
  C. H. Bailey at the New Jersey Agricultural Experiment Station (Rutgers). The seedling was selected in 1971 and
  numbered NJF2 in 1977. Stark Bros. patented it (US Plant Patent 5,123, 1983, now expired) and sold it as
  'Stark Saturn'. Its flat shape comes from 'Peento', the Chinese flat peach, by way of RT6. [CV] S1, S2
- **The release paper says.** "Trees … are large, vigorous, spreading, and very productive. Flower buds have been
  exceptionally hardy … Leaf glands are reniform and flowers are large and showy. The fruit … is highly blushed and
  approximately 6 cm in diameter. The fruit is vertically compressed and oblate … The flesh is white, melting, fine,
  and tender … Unlike most flat peaches which have holes in the flesh below the pit, 'Stark Saturn' has a small, tight
  scar and flesh covering the pit. The freestone fruit ripen approximately with 'Redhaven'." [CV] S1
- **Rules for the model.** A deciduous tree that flowers on last year's shoots before its leaves come out, with pink,
  showy flowers. Its fruit is a small flat drupe with a red blush, about twice as wide as it is tall. The tree is
  open-vase pruned every winter, so its form is the pruner's as much as its own. Saturn sets far more fruit than it
  can carry, so it is always thinned (S3).

## 1. Seed and germination (when the model starts from a stone)

| Row | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Stone (endocarp) of 'Saturn' | **19 mm long (axial) × 22 mm thick**. Oblate, a broad base, no point at the apex, sides unequal, "irregularly furrowed throughout" | [CV] S2 |
| Stone of the sport 'Rattray' | **15 mm long × 20 mm wide × 21 mm thick**. Round and oblate; the apex blunt, almost flat; ridges sharp; dry colour "tan with pink tones" (Munsell 7.5R 7/4); up to 10 % of stones split | [CV-sport] S3 |
| Stones of flat peaches as a group | height (along the fruit axis) **12.1–13.7 mm**, width **18.3–20.1 mm**, thickness **19.2–22.2 mm**, mass **2.4–3.4 g**, 2.3–4.9 % of the fruit's mass. The stone's axis is its *shortest* dimension, unlike a round peach's. The tip of the stone often pierces the skin at the eye | [SP] S8 (5 flat cultivars, Bulgaria) |
| Stone of a round peach, for contrast | 72–244 cleaned stones per kg (average 156, about **6.4 g** each) | [SP] S10 |
| Kernel (seed) | no Saturn value found. Its daughter 'Flat Delight Two' has a kernel **6–10 × 6–9 × 5–7 mm**. The seed is a flat almond-like kernel in a brown seed coat, without endosperm: two thick, white, oily cotyledons fill it | [SP] S7, S10 |
| How sown | **cold-moist stratification for about 90 days** (90 d of chilling for peach; 98–105 d with the stone intact, 70–105 d with it removed), then sow in spring **5 cm (2 in) deep**. Or sow in autumn and let the winter do it | [SP] S10 |
| Germination % | about **32 %** from intact stones and **82 %** with the stone removed (Woody Plant Seed Manual). About **96 %** after 90 d at 5 °C with the stone and seed coat removed (Szymajda et al.) | [SP] S10; S11 [2nd] |
| Germination type | **Hypogeal.** The thick cotyledons stay inside the seed coat (and stone, if not cracked) at or below the soil surface and never become green leaf-like organs. The epicotyl arches up and carries the first leaves into the light. The manual says germination "is hypogeal in many [*Prunus*] species" (shown for American plum), but epigeal in chokecherry; a gardener's report says the same for peach directly | [SP] S10; S26 [2nd]; peach-specific wording [EST] |
| Root out / emergence | the radicle splits the stone at its suture during or just after stratification (as in other *Prunus*). Emergence comes **about 2–4 weeks** after warm sowing [EST, from the manual's *Prunus* account and garden practice] | [SP] S10; [EST] |
| Hypocotyl, seed leaves | the hypocotyl is short and stays underground. The cotyledons stay in the stone, pale cream, shrinking as they feed the seedling, and rot away in the first summer [EST]. Draw nothing green below the first leaves | [EST] |
| First true leaves | the epicotyl, green to red-tinged, carries 1–3 small scale-like leaves, then normal **lanceolate, finely serrate leaves set alternately (spiral)**, about 3–6 cm long at first [EST]. A gardener's note: a peach seedling's "first real leaves are slender and sawtoothed" | [EST]; S26 [2nd] |
| Unchilled seed | embryos from seeds that were not fully stratified give **physiologically dwarfed seedlings**: short internodes, crowded leaves, leaf lesions. Gibberellin and more chilling reverse it | [SP] S27 [2nd] (Zigas & Coombe 1977) |
| Seedling's first year | **0.5–1.2 m**, a single whip, perhaps a few sylleptic side shoots [EST, from nursery seedling-rootstock practice in S10] |
| Seedling to first flower | about **3 years** ("minimum seed-bearing age 3") | [SP] S10 |
| What a Saturn stone grows into | the seedling is **not** a Saturn. Flat shape is a partly dominant allele *S*: Ss trees bear flat fruit, ss trees bear round fruit, and SS trees abort their fruit a few weeks after set. Saturn is Ss and self-fertile, so a selfed stone gives about 1 SS : 2 Ss : 1 ss | [SP] S23 [2nd]; ratio [EST] from that model |

| Fruiting wood | flowers and fruit are borne **only on one-year shoots** ("wood produced the previous year"). The ideal fruiting shoot is **30–60 cm (12–24 in) long and 5–6 mm (3/16–1/4 in) thick** at its base, without sylleptic side shoots. Kept shoots stand **10–15 cm (4–6 in)** apart along the scaffolds. | Shoot growth per year | fruiting shoots **30–60 cm**; moderate young-tree shoots **30–70 cm (12–28 in)**; water sprouts 1.2–2.1 m. Saturn's sport: about 50 cm. 'Flat Delight Two': 1.1–1.3 m of current-season growth on vigorous shoots | [SP] S16; [CV-sport] S3; [SP] S7 |

| Growth rhythm | one long flush from bud break (at or just after bloom) to midsummer; proleptic shoots stop after about 100 d, vigorous ones go on to late summer. Sylleptic shoots break from the axils of strong shoots in the same summer | [SP] S19 |

### 6d. Growth curve (double sigmoid)

| Row | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|

## 9. Gaps

- **Okie, USDA Handbook 714, and Layne & Bassi.** I did not read Saturn's entry in Okie's *Handbook of Peach and
  Nectarine Varieties* (USDA AH 714, 1998), nor Layne & Bassi's *The Peach*. The Saturn description here rests on
  its release paper (S1), its plant patent (S2) and its sport's patent (S3), which are the primary sources those
  books draw on. A GRIN accession was not found.
- **Chilling requirement.** Sources disagree. Dave Wilson says **200–300 h**, but marks it "estimated"; Burnt Ridge
  says 250 h. USDA RMA (Georgia) gives **700 flower chill hours** (Redhaven 950). Saturn's daughter 'Flat Delight
  Two' needs 450–500 chill units, but it is bred from low-chill 'Flordacrest'. Saturn was bred in New Jersey, a
  high-chill climate, and blooms there with the standard cultivars (8–11 April). **Model 600 h [EST]**: closer to the
  USDA figure than the nursery claims. It can crop in mild-winter California, so allow down to about 400 h.
- **Size and mass at harvest.** Saturn's patent measured the fruit hard and underripe (54–57 × 19–29 mm). The release
  gives "approximately 6 cm". The sport gives 60–90 × 30–40 mm and 90–100 g. The model's **62 × 30 mm, 95 g** is my
  pick between them.
- **Growth curve.** No measured Saturn curve was found. The DAFB sizes in § 6d are a double sigmoid fitted by eye
  to the harvest size, Guo et al.'s 15-DAFB stop of axial division, and stage lengths from general peach sources.
- **Stage II length** (20 d), June drop timing (40–60 DAFB), and the colour-by-stage table are estimates from general
  peach physiology.
- **Height, trunk and crown by age.** Only the sport's 5-year values (3 m, 10–14 cm, 2.5 m wide on St Julien) are
  measured on the cultivar. The year 1, 3, 10 and 15–20 values are interpolated. 'Flat Delight Two''s patent gives a
  trunk of 34.3 cm at 8 years but a 10.2 cm circumference, which contradict each other; I did not use them.
- **Internodes.** 3–4 cm comes from a daughter cultivar's vigorous wood; 1.2–2 cm on fruiting shoots is calculated
  from shoot lengths and node counts in other cultivars.
- **Phyllotaxis 2/5** and **Rauh's model**: assigned by reasoning, not from a peach source.
- **Germination type.** "Hypogeal" is stated for *Prunus* in general by the Woody Plant Seed Manual, and for peach
  only by a gardener's forum. The germination timing (2–4 weeks) and first-leaf sizes are estimates.
- **Leaf life** (200–220 d, 100–150 d for later leaves) and **autumn colour** detail are estimates. NC State gives
  "usually yellow".
- **Anther colour.** Given as yellow from Saturn's daughter (RHS 15B–16C). Not recorded for Saturn.
- **Yield per Saturn tree** (30–50 kg, 300–500 fruit) is scaled from Redhaven's measured yields by fruit mass.
- **Rootstock vigour.** The GF 677 +30 % comes from a one-season trial abstract; the St Julien A 75 % comes from a
  nursery catalogue (for apricot).
- **Roots.** The figures come from abstracts of Brazilian and Romanian peach-root studies, not from Saturn.
- **Pit cavity colour.** Saturn's is not described; the pink-red flush is from the yellow sport and is an estimate
  for the white original.

## 10. For the reference sheet: what the eye sees

Hex values are estimates for a neutral daylight plate. The Munsell and Ridgway notations are the measured anchors
from S2 and S3.

- **Trunk and old bark**: grey-brown, 10YR 5/2 (≈ `#7d7266`), smooth and faintly glossy on young trunks with **tan
  horizontal lenticel dashes** (1 × 3 mm, ≈ `#c8b8a4`). Rough, scaly and darker grey (≈ `#5e5650`) on old trunks.
  Amber gum beads (≈ `#b8741e`, drying to `#6b2a12`) at wounds.
- **One-year shoots (winter)**: reddish brown to wine red on the sun side (≈ `#7a2e22`), olive-brown below. Fat,
  grey-silver downy flower buds (≈ `#a9a39a`) in twos and threes beside a slim pointed leaf bud.
- **Growing shoots (summer)**: **red above** (5R 4/6, ≈ `#a33a30`), **light green below** (10Y 7/6, ≈ `#b9c27a`),
  bright green at the tip, sprinkled with tiny pale lenticels.
- **Leaf, upper face**: medium to dark green, glossy (5GY 4/4, ≈ `#4a6b2c`).
- **Leaf, under face**: a little paler and matt (5GY 5/4, ≈ `#6a8650`). Midrib pale yellow-green (≈ `#d6e0a8`) and
  raised below. Petiole glands are dark red dots (5R 3/8, ≈ `#7a1e1e`).
- **Young leaves**: yellow-green with a bronze-red flush at the shoot tip. **Autumn**: yellow (≈ `#d9b52a`) with
  orange-red flecks, then brown.
- **Petals**: medium rose-pink (2.5R 6/6, ≈ `#d27a86`), deepening toward the claw and the centre (≈ `#b8505e`), paling
  as the flower ages. **Flower cup and sepals**: red-brown to purplish (≈ `#7a3a30`).
- **Anthers**: yellow to orange-yellow (≈ `#e8b830`) on pale pink to white filaments; brownish after shedding pollen.
- **Fruitlet (to 30 DAFB)**: bright green, grey-velvet (≈ `#7ea84a`). **Pit-hardening fruit (30–65 DAFB)**: mid
  green (≈ `#8fb05a`).
- **Fruit ground colour**: light green (≈ `#c4d49a`) → green-white (≈ `#dfe6c4`) → **creamy white when ripe**
  (≈ `#efe8cc`), with whitish green in the stem cavity.
- **Fruit blush**: starts as a faint red flush on the sun side (≈ `#c25a4a`) at about 65–85 DAFB and spreads to
  **Nopal red** (≈ `#b0283a`) over 70–85 % when ripe, deepest on the sun side (≈ `#7e1a2e`), freckled and streaked at
  its edge. A fine, short, silvery down softens the whole skin. The sunken eye is a pale, dry, slightly cracked scar.
- **Flesh**: white (≈ `#f6f1e2`), translucent and juicy; a narrow pink-red flush (≈ `#d88a8a`) may line the stone
  cavity [EST].
- **Stone**: a small flattened disc, **tan with pink tones** (7.5R 7/4, ≈ `#c99d90`) when dry, wet-reddish when fresh,
  deeply and irregularly furrowed with sharp ridges. **Kernel**: brown-skinned (≈ `#8a5a3a`) with white inside.
