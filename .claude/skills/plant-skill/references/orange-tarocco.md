# Citrus × sinensis (L.) Osbeck 'Tarocco': facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for a blood orange grown stage by stage, from the rootstock seed and the budded nursery tree to
a fruiting tree in a Sicilian grove. Built on the template `datasheet.md` and set out like
`banana-dwarf-cavendish.md`. Where a value is from the trials in Sicily it says so; values from California, Spain or
Florida are marked by place.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured on 'Tarocco' itself or one of its clones: Tarocco Comune, Nucellare 57-1E-1, Gallo (C898),
  Scirè, Meli (C8158), Dal Muso (Dalmuso), Rosso, Sant'Alfio, Ippolito (M507), Lempso, TDV, and the Californian
  Tarocco #7 (CRC 3596) and Bream Tarocco (CRC 4032). The clone is named on each row.
- **[SP]**: said of the sweet orange (*C. sinensis*), of blood oranges in general, or of *Citrus*.
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with the reasoning given. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a search snippet, or a work that cites the original, not the original.

Each number has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are metres, millimetres,
kilograms and days. Ages run from planting the budded tree in the grove unless a row says otherwise.

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** 'Tarocco' is the Sicilian half-blood orange: anthocyanin red in the flesh (and, in some clones, a
  garnet blush on the rind) that forms only after cool winter nights. It is a bud mutation, "seems to have been derived
  from a mutation of the 'Sanguinello'", found at the end of the 19th century near **Francofonte** (Siracusa), and
  spread to Lentini, Carlentini and Palagonia. S5. It is the main variety of the PGI "Arancia Rossa di Sicilia"
  (eastern Sicily: the Catania plain and the hills round Etna). S4, S5
- **A cultivar-population.** "Con il termine Tarocco si indica una cultivar-popolazione": many clones that differ in
  ripening time, fruit shape and pigment. S6 citing La Rosa et al. 2012. CREA-OFA Acireale could fingerprint
  'Ippolito M507', 'Meli C8158', 'TDV' and 'Lempso C nuc' by SNPs, but orchards sold as 'Scirè', 'Gallo' or 'Dal Muso'
  are "heterogeneous populations of clones". S11. Model **one clone** (I suggest Tarocco Scirè, the most planted,
  S6) and use the others for variety.
- **Its name and its mark.** "The name … the English equivalent of which is the peg-top toy, is said to have come
  from the pronounced basal collar that is characteristic of fruits of the original clone." Hodgson 1967, S1. The PGI
  rules give the shape as "obovata o globosa con base più o meno prominente ('Muso' lungo o corto)": obovate or
  globose, with a more or less prominent base, the "muzzle" long or short. S4. **The neck is real, but it varies by
  clone**: strong in Dal Muso, weak or absent in Scirè and Gallo (section 5).
- **Rules for the model.** Every cultivated tree is **budded on a rootstock**; a Tarocco seedling is not the cultivar
  in practice (section 1). Sweet orange grows in **flushes** (three a year in the Mediterranean) and is evergreen. Fruit
  hangs over winter: the tree carries ripe fruit, the new bloom and the setting fruitlets of the next crop together
  in April–May.

## 1. Planting material: the rootstock seed, the nursery tree, and the Tarocco seed

**How it is really planted.** Every grove tree is a Tarocco bud on a rootstock seedling.

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rootstocks allowed in the PGI | "arancio amaro, citrange troyer, citrange carrizo, poncirus trifoliata" (sour orange, Troyer and Carrizo citrange, trifoliate orange), virus-free | [CV] S4 |
| Usual rootstock in Sicily, old groves | **sour orange** (*C. aurantium*). The spread of Citrus tristeza virus in Italy in the early 2000s "ha determinato l'esigenza di sostituire l'arancio amaro" | [SP] S6 citing Modica et al. 2022; S10 |
| Usual rootstock in Sicily, new groves | **Carrizo citrange** (also Troyer; trial rootstocks C35, Bitters, Furr, Swingle citrumelo). Tarocco Scirè and Moro trees in the Lentini trials are on Carrizo; the Tarocco trials of CREA/Catania use Carrizo and Swingle as references | [CV] S6, S9, S10 |
| **Model**: rootstock | **Carrizo citrange** for a tree planted today; sour orange for an old grove (40+ years) | [EST] from the rows above |
| Rootstock seed | sown ¼–½ in (6–13 mm) deep; "about two weeks to germinate" warm; quicker with the seed coat removed | [SP] S17 |
| Rootstock seedling | repotted at ~4 in (10 cm); trained to a single stem with no branches up to 6–8 in (15–20 cm) | [SP] S17 |
| Budding | when the stem is ¼–⅜ in (6–10 mm), "about the diameter of a pencil"; inverted-T bud **6–8 in (15–20 cm) above the soil**; wrap off after 2–3 weeks; stock cut back above the bud 6–8 weeks after budding | [SP] S17 |
| Age at budding | "after about 12–16 months in the nursery, the trees are usually large enough to bud" | [SP] S18 [2nd] |
| Age at planting out | "when the budded tops are one to two years old" | [SP] S18 [2nd] |
| Scaffold | main branches start "ad almeno 25–30 cm dalla linea di innesto" (at least 25–30 cm above the graft) | [CV] S4 |
| **Model**: nursery stages | seed 0; emergence ~14–20 d; 10 cm at ~2 months; budded at ~12 months (stock 6–10 mm thick, union 15–20 cm up); scion shoot staked to ~0.8–1.2 m by ~24 months; planted at 2–3 years from seed with a single trunk headed at 50–70 cm and 3–4 scaffolds | [EST] from S17, S18, S4 |

## 2. Growth and form

### Height, crown and trunk by age (grafted, Sicily)

- **Rootstock.** Carrizo and Troyer citrange and trifoliate hybrids give smaller trees than sour orange or Swingle;
  'Bitters' "showed a remarkable reduction of the canopy size, nevertheless being very productive". [CV] S10 [2nd].
  Troyer gave the smallest Tarocco canopy (4.7 m³), Furr the largest (10.1 m³). [CV] S9 [2nd]
- **Habit (sources disagree; see Gaps).**
  - Hodgson (1967): "Tree moderately vigorous, medium in size and irregular in form; foliage rather open … Sensitive
    to wind and neglect; only moderately productive." [CV] S1
  - Tarocco #7 (UCR): "The tree is vigorous, but only moderately productive." [CV] S2
  - Tarocco Nucellare 57-1E-1: "elevata vigoria e la notevole presenza di spine"; a grown tree yields up to 2 quintals
    "in virtù del notevole sviluppo della chioma". [CV] S6 citing La Rosa et al. 2012. A CREA slide gives its habit as
    "assurgente" (upright). [CV] [2nd: snippet of the CREA varietal deck; the deck itself was not reachable]
  - Tarocco dal Muso: "sensibile al vento e tende a fruttificare nella parte più bassa e interna della chioma". [CV] S6
  - Citrus ID: "Crown compact or dense, not weeping." [CV] S1
  - **Model:** a rounded to broadly upright crown, a little irregular and open (light comes through), wider than tall
    once grown; young Nucellare trees upright and thorny; Dal Muso fruit low and inside the crown. [EST]
- **Shoots and branching.** Young shoots "angular" (S14). The shoot tip aborts after each flush, so the next flush
  comes from the top 2–4 axillary buds, giving forks and a twiggy outer shell. [SP] S14; branching [EST]

### Growth rhythm (flushes), measured on young Tarocco

Young budded Tarocco trees in boxes in northern Sardinia (1972–73) grew in **three flushes** with three rests
[CV] S8:

| Flush | Start | End | What it was like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (spring) | late in the first half of **February**, when the mean air and soil temperatures reach ~10 °C | **mid-May** (stasis 26 April – 30 May, at bloom and fruit set) | few shoots, short |
| 2 (summer) | **early June** (after 15 days of stasis), at ~20 °C air / 22 °C soil | **mid-August** (stopped by the heat: 23.1 °C mean air, 26.1 °C soil); peak in late July | "germogli molto più lunghi": the longest shoots |
| 3 (autumn) | **late August**, when means fall to ~17–20 °C | **mid-October**, at 10.7 °C air / 15 °C soil; then winter rest to February | moderate |

- Roots grow in the pauses between the flushes, when the soil is near 20 °C. [CV] S8
- Florida sweet orange has three flushes too (Feb–Mar, May–Jun, Aug–Sep). [SP] S23 [2nd]
- **On a grown, bearing tree** the spring flush is the main one and carries the flowers; summer and autumn flushes are
  weaker and come mostly on the outside of the crown. [EST] from S8 and S16

**Phyllotaxis.** Spiral. Use a **3/8 divergence (135°)** on vigorous shoots and 2/5 (144°) on weak ones. [EST]: no
*Citrus* value was read; the 2/5–3/8 pair is the common spiral of woody dicots.

**Internodes.** No measured value. Use **1–2.5 cm** on fruiting spring twigs, **3–6 cm** on summer shoots and up to
**8–10 cm** on water sprouts. [EST] from the leaf density of the cultivar's photos and the 13–16 mm petiole wing as a
scale

**Thorns.**

- Sweet orange: "Spines confined to trunk, main branches and strong young vegetative shoots." [SP] S14. "Straight
  spines often present in the leaf axils." [SP] S15 [2nd]. 'OLL-8': "Relatively smooth with small thorns or spines …
  On mature branches, average **25 to 50 mm** in length, and **2 to 5 mm** in diameter at the base"; green, grey-green
  tip. [SP] S12
- Tarocco: "thorns straight; prickles absent or not persistent" (S1). Nucellare 57-1E-1 is very thorny ("la
  spinescenza … può causare ferite ai frutti in presenza di forte vento") (S6). No thorn length for Tarocco was found.
- **Model:** one straight axillary thorn at a share of nodes: **5–15 mm** on fruiting twigs of an old-line clone,
  **20–50 mm** on water sprouts, young trees and the Nucellare clone. [EST] from S12, S6

### Roots

- **Depth** depends on rootstock and soil. Mean rooting depth at the drip line of 'Orlando' tangelo trees in Florida
  sand ran from **4.6 m (rough lemon)** to **2.8 m (Rubidoux trifoliate)**; sour and sweet orange were in the middle.
  [SP] S22 [2nd]
- 'Pêra' sweet orange in Brazil: roots "concentrated on the soil surface layers (0–0.40 m), extending horizontally up
  to 1.50 m from the plant". [SP] S22 [2nd]
- **Model:** a few sinker roots to 1.5–2.5 m; most feeder roots in the top 0.6 m; spread to about the drip line, a
  little past it in old trees. No visible taproot after transplanting. [EST] from S22

**Shape for the model.**

- A **broadly obovoid to globose** fruit, L/D **0.95–1.10**. [CV] S1, S2, S3, S4
- At the stem end, a **broad, short neck (the "muso")**, often with 3–6 shallow furrows radiating from the button.
  [CV] S1, S4
- The apex is rounded or slightly flattened, with a small stylar scar. [EST]
- Give each fruit a neck from **none to ~8 mm** high and about **35–50 % of the fruit's diameter** across. Most fruit
  on a Dal Muso or Meli tree get a clear neck; Scirè and Gallo are nearly round with at most a raised base. [EST]: no
  neck dimensions are published; the fractions are from the L/D values (Dal Muso and Meli the longest, S3) and the
  PGI's "long or short muzzle" (S4)

**Rind.**

- Thickness: "medium to medium-thick, moderately tightly adherent, and finely to moderately pebbled" (S1). Measured
  **3.5–6.6 mm** (S3) and 3.8–7.9 mm (S2). The EDIS sheet calls it "a thin peel" (S7); sources disagree (Gaps).
- Surface: "superficie molto liscia" (very smooth) (S4); "smooth or slightly rough" (S1).
- Oil glands: no Tarocco count was found. Sweet orange 'OLL-8' has **176–180 oil glands per cm²**, large ones among
  small ones, smaller toward the shoulder (S12). 'Noah': "larger ones interspersed by smaller ones", moderately
  conspicuous, pitting sparse (S13).
- **Model:** glands 0.3–0.8 mm across at ~1.7 per mm², slightly sunken (fine pebbling). [EST] from S12

**Flesh.**

- "di colore ambrato con screziature rosse più o meno intense secondo la zona di produzione e l'epoca della raccolta"

### Growth by days from bloom

Citrus fruit grows on a sigmoid curve with three stages (Bain 1958) [SP] S16:

- **Phase I** is "an approximately two-month interval of cell division and slow growth including the period between
  anthesis and June drop".
- **Phase II**: "fruit experiences a huge increase in size by cell enlargement and water accumulation during four to
  six months".
- **Phase III** is ripening: "growth is mostly arrested".

|---|---|---|---|---|
| May | 0 | ovary 2.6 mm; 5–8 mm by the petal fall | anthesis, flower drop | [SP] S12; [EST] |
| end of June | ~45 | 12–18 mm | June drop | [EST] |
| end of July | ~75 | 28–35 mm | phase II begins | [EST] |

| end of September | ~135 | 55–62 mm | phase II | [EST] |

| December | ~220 | **78 mm** (peak), height 88 mm, ~190 g; rind 4.0 mm | phase III; early clones (Nucellare, Gallo) picked | [CV] S6 |

- **Bloom to ripe:** **~220 days** (early clones, December) to **~275 days** (mid-season, February) and **~330–360
  days** (Meli, Sant'Alfio). [CV] S6. Using the UCR season of "December to February" at Riverside (S2) gives the same
  range.
- **On the tree after ripeness:** Hodgson: "Loses quality if left on tree much past maturity and drops badly" (S1).
  Scirè has a "buona persistenza … sulla pianta (caratteristica non frequente nei cloni di Tarocco)" (S6). Model ripe
  fruit dropping 4–8 weeks after ripeness, except in Scirè and the late clones. [EST]
- **Development of the forecast.** Sicilian agrometeorology puts Tarocco's ripening start at **3,850 development
  units** from 1 January (threshold 12.8 °C, optimum 24–30 °C, ceiling 37 °C), between Washington Navel (3,800) and
  Valencia (4,200). [CV] S20
- The diameter curve is an estimate. It is a sigmoid fitted to the measured December peak and to the 2-month Phase I
  and the 4–6-month Phase II (S16). No month-by-month Tarocco diameters were found (see Gaps).

## Gaps

These rows have no published Tarocco number. Each is marked [EST] above with how it was estimated.

- **Tree height and width by year.** Only the canopy volume (4.7–10.1 m³) and trunk area (75–139 cm²) of ~6-year-old
  Tarocco Scirè are known, from search snippets of S9. Heights come from an ellipsoid of that volume and from a 7-year
  Florida sweet orange (S12).
- **Fruit diameter by month.** The only Tarocco curve read is November–February (S6). The June–November values are a
  sigmoid fitted to Bain's phases (S16).
- **Neck size.** The neck is published as a trait (S1, S4) with no dimensions.
- **Thorn length on Tarocco.** Only "thorns straight" (S1) and Nucellare's "notevole presenza di spine" (S6) are
  published. The lengths come from 'OLL-8' (S12).
- **Leaf, wing, flower and seed measures.** These are from sweet orange patents (S12, S13) and floras (S14, S15), not
  from Tarocco.
- **Phyllotaxis and internode lengths.** Both are estimated.
- **Graft-union look, stalk size and oil-gland size.** All estimated.
- **Yield by age, years to first crop, and productive life.** Estimated, framed by the PGI caps (S4), the Nucellare
  200 kg (S6) and the Scirè yields (S9 [2nd]).
- **Hypogeal germination, stamen bundles.** Standard *Citrus* botany. No source was read here.
- **Hex colours.** The rind hexes are converted from measured CIELAB (S3). The leaf and bark hexes are approximations
  of the RHS codes.
- **Second-hand values ([2nd]).**
  - S9 (canopy, trunk and yield snippets), S10, S18, S21, S22, S23, S24, S25.
  - The CREA "habitus assurgente" snippet.
  - The NSW DPI factsheets on Tarocco Ippolito, Meli and Rosso: the site refused access (403). The search snippets
    gave Ippolito 290 g, 83 mm, rind 4.9 mm; Rosso 251 g, rind 5.7 mm; Meli 266 g, rind 6.5 mm, a "distinctive neck",
    sets in clusters. These are not used in the tables above.
- **Not reached.**
  - Hodgson's chapter itself (UCR and its mirrors were down); it was read as quoted in full by Citrus ID (S1).
  - The UCR Tarocco description pages other than CRC 3596 and 4032.
  - Spiegel-Roy & Goldschmidt, *Biology of Citrus*.
  - The full text of Continella et al. 2018.

**Where the sources disagree**

- **Vigour and habit.** Hodgson: "moderately vigorous, medium in size … irregular … foliage rather open" (S1). UCR
  Tarocco #7 and the Italian Nucellare: vigorous, and Nucellare very thorny with a large crown (S2, S6).
- **Rind thickness.** EDIS: "a thin peel" (S7). Hodgson: "medium to medium-thick" (S1). Measured 3.5–6.6 mm, clone
  dependent: Dal Muso and Scirè thin, Meli thick (S3).
- **Shape.** Hodgson and the PGI: obovate with a collar or "muso" (S1, S4). EDIS: "globular to round" (S7). The
  measured L/D is 0.92–1.05 in Spain (S3) and 0.91–1.12 in California (S2). The Lentini thesis gives Scirè a height of
  88 mm over a 78 mm diameter in December (L/D 1.13) (S6), against 0.98 in Spain (S3).
- **Fruit mass.** Measured 178–351 g by clone (S3) and 120–293 g in California (S2). Scirè is small: 178 g in Spain,
  187–193 g in Sicily (S3, S6).
- **PGI yield cap.** 25 t/ha in the Italian disciplinare (S4); 30 t/ha in the 1995 EU application (S5).
- **Productivity.** "Only moderately productive" (S1, S2), against Scirè's "ottima produttività" and Nucellare's 200 kg
  (S6).
