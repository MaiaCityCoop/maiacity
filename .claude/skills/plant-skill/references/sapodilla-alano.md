# Manilkara zapota (L.) P.Royen 'Alano': facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for a grafted sapodilla grown stage by stage from seed (the rootstock) to ripe fruit. Built on the
template `datasheet.md` and set out like `banana-dwarf-cavendish.md`.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: said of 'Alano' itself.
- **[SP]**: said of the species, of the genus, or measured on another cultivar (named in the row: 'Kalipatti',
  'Cricket Ball', 'Prolific' and so on).
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with how I made it. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a snippet, a summary page or a review that cites the work, not the original.

Each value has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are metres, kilograms, days and years.
Ages run from planting the grafted tree out unless a row says otherwise. Fruit ages run from flower opening (anthesis)
or from fruit set, as each row says.

**Very little is measured on 'Alano' itself.** The only published numbers for it are a fruit-mass range, a shape, a
skin description and a season (S1, S8, S9). Its tree, leaves, flowers and seeds are not described apart from the
species anywhere I could read. Most rows therefore carry [SP] values, taken from the botanical floras and from Indian
cultivar trials. Those trials report their cultivars side by side, and 'Kalipatti' (also a compact, oval-fruited,
heavy-bearing cultivar) is used here as the stand-in for tree size by age.

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** 'Alano' is a grafted, named clone. It is one of the five "major commercial cultivars" of south
  Florida, with 'Hasyá', 'Morena', 'Prolific' and 'Tikal' (S7). Florida's cultivar table gives it fruit that are "conical
  to round, skin light brown, smooth", "S to M, 115–250 g", pulp "smooth to slightly granular", eating quality "very
  good to excellent", yield rated "good", season **November to June** (S1). [CV]
- **Its marks.** Small to medium fruit, typically "about nine ounces" (~255 g) (S8). The skin is lighter and less scurfy
  than most (S1). The tree "tends to be less brittle and have a much smaller habit than other cultivars" (S8). It is
  "only medium vigor, highly productive and typically multiple cropping", with its main season December to February
  in Florida "but often has fruit in late-summer/fall" (S9, a Florida grower). [CV]
- **Where it came from: correcting the brief.** The brief said 'Alano' was introduced from the Philippines and is the
  leading cultivar of the Philippines. **I found no source for either claim.**
  - UF/IFAS gives its origin as "United States (Hawaii)" (S1). A Florida grower says "Originally from Hawaii" (S9), and a
    nursery says it "was selected in Hawaii" (S10).
  - Fairchild Tropical Botanic Garden calls it "an oval-shaped fruit native to Thailand" (S8), and a nursery repeats
    this (S10).
  - The USDA genebank holds it as two Florida-donated clones (MIA 36707, from R.J. Campbell of Fairchild, 2001; TARS
    17897, from G. Zill, 2001). Neither record gives an origin beyond Florida (S11).
  - PROSEA, written by a Filipino author, says that in the Philippines "the small-fruited, prolific 'Pineras' is most
    common". It names 'Ponderosa', 'Sao Manila' and 'Gonzalez' as the other cultivars there, and does not mention
    'Alano' (S3).
  - So treat 'Alano' as a **Florida commercial cultivar, said to be of Hawaiian selection** (the Thai origin is a minority
    claim). The Florida growers who study the cultivars note "much confusion of their proper identities" (S19 [2nd]).
- **Rules for the model.**
  - A grafted 'Alano' never shows the seedling's tall single leader cleanly. PROSEA warns that "in clonally propagated
    trees the architecture is not so clearly expressed" (S3). Start it as a 0.9–1.2 m nursery tree with a few
    scaffold shoots.
  - All parts bleed **white latex** when cut (S2, S3).
  - Leaves sit only in **rosettes at the shoot tips**. Flowers and fruit hang singly from the leaf axils in and just
    below those rosettes (S3, S6).
  - Fruit of every age can hang on one tree at once: it flowers in several flushes a year and bears over many months
    (S1, S3).

- **Growth rate.** "Fairly slow-growing, long-lived" (S2), and "grow slowly" (S1). For the model, the trunk thickens
  by **~1.2 cm in Ø a year** in the first 15 years (from the 'Kalipatti' series above) [EST].
- **Height to crown width.** About 1 : 1.5 by year 11: the crown is wider than the tree is tall in managed trees (S12).
  Unpruned trees are "distinctly pyramidal when young" (S2), and then "pyramidal to rounded" (S1).

## Gaps

These rows have no published number for 'Alano', and most have none for the species either. Each is marked [EST] above.

- **'Alano' origin.** The sources conflict: Hawaii (S1, S9, S10) or Thailand (S8, S10). I found **no** source for the
  brief's "introduced from the Philippines" or "leading cultivar of the Philippines". PROSEA names 'Pineras' as the
  most common there (S3). No Florida State Horticultural Society paper on 'Alano' turned up.
- **'Alano' fruit dimensions.** Only the mass is published (115–250 g). I computed L × D from an ellipsoid with L/D
  1.15 at 1.0 g/cm³.
- **'Alano' seeds per fruit.** Not published. I set 3–5 from the species' range and the Indian cultivar means (S12).
- **'Alano' flower-to-mature days.** Not published. I set ~200 d (180–240) from the species' range and the Indian and
  Philippine cultivar values.
- **'Alano' bloom months.** Estimated as harvest (S1, S9) minus ~6–7 months.
- **'Alano' first crop.** Estimated from the Florida grafted-tree range of 2–4 years (S1) and its "highly productive"
  habit.
- **Height and trunk by age.** Nothing for 'Alano'. Years 8–17 are measured on 'Kalipatti' (S12; S19 [2nd]). Years
  0–4 are interpolated from the nursery size (S1) to the year-8 value. The trunk Ø at planting is from the 6 mm stock at
  grafting (S2) plus 1–2 years.
- **Scaffold count and angle.** Estimated from photographs of the species' habit and the "horizontal or drooping"
  branches (S1).
- **Phyllotaxis angle and internodes.** Only "spiral" and "clustered" are published; I chose 137.5°.
- **Leaf life.** Not found. I set 12–18 months from the two flushes a year (S18 [2nd]) and the rosette habit.
- **Seed width, thickness and mass.** Only the length is published. The width and thickness are from the shape and a
  mass of ~0.6–1 g. The mass is from S12's "seed weight" column, read as grams, not the printed "mg".
- **Seedling and cotyledons.** No measured hypocotyl, cotyledon size or seedling height. The values are from the family's
  seed structure (leafy cotyledons with endosperm) and the stem thicknesses in S2 and S17.
- **Colours as hex.** All hex values are estimates from the published descriptions (dark glossy green, paler below,
  pinkish flush, cream bell flowers with rusty sepals, light-brown smooth 'Alano' skin, honey-brown flesh, black seeds
  with a white hilum, dark brown fissured bark).
- **Fruit stalk size.** Estimated from the pedicel length (S3, S4).
- **Productive life beyond 40 years.** Extrapolated.
- **Second-hand values ([2nd]).** These were read only as summaries or snippets: S14 (fruit development), S18 (BBCH
  phenology), S19 (Pune, Ghataprabha and 16–17-year trials), S20 ('Kalipatti' maturity), S22 (anthesis hours), S17
  (ITFNet), S4b (Flora de Panamá on WFO), and the 4–8 oz figure in S10.
- **Not reachable.** The CABI Compendium sheet (I could not find or open it). Morton's original page at
  hort.purdue.edu returned 503, so I read Growables' full reproduction instead (S2). No Philippine BPI or PCAARRD chico
  guide was found. The Bonilla, Campbell, Ledesma and Bennett (2012) ASHS cultivar evaluation could not be opened
  (S19d).
