Musa acuminata Colla (AAA Group, Cavendish Subgroup) 'Dwarf Cavendish' — the dwarf dessert banana, grown under glass. A giant herb, not a tree: its "trunk" is a pseudostem of tightly rolled leaf sheaths on an underground corm. Sterile and seedless: planted as a tissue-culture plantlet or a sword sucker.
Pseudostem: 1.5–2.5 m high when bearing, 18–22 cm across at its foot and tapering little, glossy green to yellow-green with irregular dark brown-black blotches, its outer sheaths drying papery brown at the base. Leaves: 10–14 standing at once, packed close at the top (short internodes, a dense rosette), blade 1.3–1.7 m × 0.6–0.75 m, oblong with a blunt tip, bright mid-green above, paler with a whitish waxy bloom below, a stout pale-green midrib, fine parallel veins at right angles to it, the blade torn into strips by wind in places; stalk 30–40 cm, grooved, with blotches; the new leaf comes up rolled as a "cigar"; old leaves hang down brown and dry along the stem.
Inflorescence: shoots from the top 9–12 months after planting and bends over to hang; heavy waxy bracts dark purple-red outside, paler crimson inside, each lifting to free a double row of flowers; the female hands first (fruit), then a long bare stalk ending in the male bud ("bell"), heart-shaped, 25–30 cm, dark purple.
Bunch: 25–40 kg, 8–12 hands, 14–20 fingers a hand, fingers 15–20 cm long, 3.5–4 cm thick, curved upward, five-angled when green, rounding as they fill; dark green, then light green, then bright yellow with a green tip, then yellow with small brown sugar spots; ripe 90–120 days after shooting. Cut across: cream-white flesh in three segments, a central line of tiny brown aborted seed specks.
Corm and roots: corm 25–35 cm across, white inside, with cord roots 5–8 mm thick, white turning brown, spreading 1.5–3 m and mostly in the top 40 cm; follower suckers rise beside the mother plant (a sword sucker has narrow sword leaves, a water sucker broad ones).
