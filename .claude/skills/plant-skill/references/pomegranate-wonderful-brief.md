Punica granatum L. 'Wonderful' (Lythraceae; the Wonderful pomegranate, found at Porterville, California, 1896) — the world's most grown pomegranate: a deciduous, multi-stemmed shrubby tree 3–5 m tall, grown in orchards from hardwood cuttings on its own roots and trained low to a few (3–5) trunks; large deep-red fruit under a tall crown-like calyx, scarlet flowers.

Planted as a hardwood cutting in winter: a straight leafless one-year stem 25–30 cm long and about 1 cm thick (pencil to finger thick), grey-brown, cut square at the foot and slanting at the top, set deep so only two or three buds show above the soil. It roots in spring and breaks into several reddish shoots; by its first autumn it is a bush of several stems from the ground, 0.8–1.2 m. Suckers keep coming up from the base all its life.

Wood and bark: no single straight trunk: three to five stems leave the ground or a short head (0.3–0.6 m), lean outward and branch into a dense, twiggy, rounded-vase crown as wide as it is tall, its outer shoots arching over and down, leafy to near the ground. It is NOT a standard: never one clean trunk with a ball of crown on a bare stem; the trunks are short (they divide within 0.5 m of the ground) and the crown reaches down almost to the soil, like a big fountain or haystack of twigs. This year's shoots are slender, four-angled, smooth, reddish (wine-red to red-brown) when young, greying in the second year; many short spur shoots, some ending in a stiff sharp grey thorn 1–4 cm. Older stems light grey to grey-brown, smooth-ish, then twisted and fluted with long ridges and furrows, the thin bark flaking in small papery strips and patches, showing paler grey-buff beneath. The old trunks are 8–15 cm thick, seldom round.

Leaf: opposite or sub-opposite, or bunched 4–8 together in rosettes on the spurs; simple, entire, narrowly oblong-lanceolate, about 6 cm long and 1.7 cm wide on long shoots (3–5 × 1–1.4 cm on spurs), about four times as long as wide, the tip blunt or slightly notched, the base tapering; short stalk 4–6 mm, reddish. Upper side glossy dark to mid green with a pale or reddish midrib, underside paler and matt; thin, smooth, hairless, side veins faint and looping. New leaves come out bronze-red to coppery in spring, greening in two to three weeks; in autumn every leaf turns clear butter-yellow and falls; the tree is bare in winter.

Flower: on the tips of this season's short shoots, singly or in twos and threes, April–June in waves. The calyx is the flower's body: a fleshy, thick, glossy red-orange tube (the hypanthium) 2–3 cm long, crowned by 5–8 (mostly 6) thick pointed triangular lobes, spreading in flower, erect later on the fruit. Two kinds of flower on one plant: perfect "vase" flowers, the tube long and swollen at its base (the ovary) that set fruit, and functionally male "bell" flowers, short and narrow-based, that drop. Petals 5–8 (mostly 6), scarlet orange-red, thin, crumpled like crepe paper, 2–3 cm long, between the calyx lobes; the open flower 4–5 cm across, the perfect flower about 4 cm long; inside, a dense brush of about 170 stamens, red-orange filaments 5–8 mm with yellow anthers, round one style. Most flowers (most of the bell kind) drop; many lie fallen under the tree. The bud is a red-orange pointed vase.

Fruit: a berry with a leathery rind (a balausta); ripe 'Wonderful' is large, about 10–11 cm across and 9 cm high (height ≈ 0.85–0.9 of its width), 350–550 g, globose and slightly flattened at the ends, with shallow irregular bumps over the chambers, sitting almost stalkless at a shoot tip, crowned by the persistent calyx: a stiff broad tube 2–2.5 cm tall (about a quarter of the fruit's height), its mouth 2.5–3.5 cm, with 6 (5–7) thick triangular pointed lobes, erect or a little spreading, red like the rind, their tips dried brown, a brown brush of old stamens inside. Its rind is smooth, leathery, a 3–4 mm red skin over a cream spongy layer, faintly glossy, with fine brown dots; by age: just set (2 cm, red calyx and withered petals still on), green (5 cm, June–July), colouring (red flushing over green from the sun side, 8 cm, August, about 11 weeks after the petals fall), ripe (deep crimson to purplish-red all over, about #9c1626, a little burnished, late September–October, about 150–165 days after full bloom; now and then a ripe fruit splits). Inside: chambers in two tiers separated by thin cream-yellow papery membranes (septa) and spongy white-cream pith, packed with about 500 (200–1,000) juicy dark ruby-red translucent arils, each angular and faceted like glass, about 11 × 8 mm, one cream hard seed 8 × 3 mm showing faintly inside.

Roots: no taproot (a cutting): several strong roots from the foot of the cutting spreading in the top 60 cm and beyond the crown, many fine fibrous feeders, the bark of old roots red-brown.
