Punica granatum L. 'Wonderful' (Lythraceae; the Wonderful pomegranate, found at Porterville, California, 1896) — the world's most grown pomegranate: a deciduous, multi-stemmed shrubby tree 3–5 m tall, grown in orchards from hardwood cuttings on its own roots and trained low to a few (3–5) trunks; large deep-red fruit under a tall crown-like calyx, scarlet flowers.

Planted as a hardwood cutting in winter: a straight leafless one-year stem 25–30 cm long and about 1 cm thick (pencil to finger thick), grey-brown, cut square at the foot and slanting at the top, set deep so only two or three buds show above the soil. It roots in spring and breaks into several reddish shoots; by its first autumn it is a bush of several stems from the ground, 0.8–1.2 m. Suckers keep coming up from the base all its life.

Wood and bark: no single straight trunk: three to five stems leave the ground or a short head (0.3–0.6 m), lean outward and branch into a dense, twiggy, rounded-vase crown as wide as it is tall, its outer shoots arching over and down, leafy to near the ground. This year's shoots are slender, four-angled, smooth, reddish (wine-red to red-brown) when young, greying in the second year; many short spur shoots, some ending in a sharp grey thorn 1–2 cm. Older stems light grey to grey-brown, smooth-ish, then twisted and fluted with long ridges and furrows, the thin bark flaking in small papery strips and patches, showing paler grey-buff beneath. The old trunks are 8–15 cm thick, seldom round.

Leaf: opposite or sub-opposite, or bunched 3–6 together on the spurs; simple, entire, oblong-lanceolate to narrowly obovate, 3–7 cm long and 1–2 cm wide, the tip blunt or slightly notched, the base tapering; very short stalk 2–5 mm, reddish. Upper side glossy bright to mid green with a pale or reddish midrib, underside paler and matt; thin, smooth, hairless, side veins faint and looping. New leaves come out bronze-red to coppery in spring, greening in two to three weeks; in autumn every leaf turns clear butter-yellow and falls; the tree is bare in winter.

Flower: on the tips of this season's short shoots, singly or in twos and threes, April–June in waves. The calyx is the flower's body: a fleshy, thick, glossy red-orange tube (the hypanthium) 2–3 cm long, crowned by 5–8 (mostly 6) thick pointed triangular lobes, spreading in flower, erect later on the fruit. Two kinds of flower on one plant: perfect "vase" flowers, the tube long and swollen at its base (the ovary) that set fruit, and functionally male "bell" flowers, short and narrow-based, that drop. Petals 5–8 (mostly 6), scarlet orange-red, thin, crumpled like crepe paper, 2–2.5 cm long, between the calyx lobes; the open flower 3–4 cm across; inside, a dense ring of several hundred stamens with yellow anthers round one red style. The bud is a red-orange pointed vase.

Fruit: a berry with a leathery rind (a balausta); ripe 'Wonderful' is large, 8–12 cm across (typically 10), 400–600 g, globose and slightly flattened at the ends, sometimes faintly angular, crowned by the persistent calyx: a stiff tube 1.5–2.5 cm tall with 6 (5–8) erect pointed lobes, dry stamens inside. Its rind is smooth, leathery, 3–6 mm thick, faintly glossy; by age: just set (2 cm, red calyx and withered petals still on), green (5 cm, June–July), colouring (yellow-green flushing red over much of it, 8 cm, August), ripe (deep crimson to purplish-red all over, a little burnished, late September–October, about 160–180 days after bloom; the ripe fruit sometimes splits). Inside: chambers in two tiers separated by thin cream-yellow papery membranes (septa) and spongy white-cream pith, packed with 600–800 juicy deep-red to garnet translucent arils, each angular, 8–10 mm, a small pale woody seed showing through.

Roots: no taproot (a cutting): several strong roots from the foot of the cutting spreading in the top 60 cm and beyond the crown, many fine fibrous feeders, the bark of old roots red-brown.
