# Theobroma cacao, West African Amelonado: facts for a procedural model

Researched for cacao v3 (2026-11-04). The photos named below (`01-bean-01` …) were the session's reference set from

Target: *Theobroma cacao* L., Forastero, Lower Amazon (LA) Amelonado, the West African Amelonado
(Cuatrecasas' subsp. *sphaerocarpum*), from bean to a mature fruiting tree.

**Labels used below**

- **[AM]**: measured on West African Amelonado.
- **[LA]**: said of Lower-Amazon Forastero / Amelonado in general.
- **[SP]**: a value for the species.
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with the reasoning given. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only a search snippet, abstract or a review that cites the work, not the original.

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** Amelonado is the Lower-Amazon Forastero. Its trees are large and come into bearing late, but yield well. Unripe pods are light green and ripen to yellow. The pod is nearly spherical (melon-like) with rounded or very bluntly pointed ends and a generally smooth surface with shallow ridges. The husk is thick and hard, with some lignification. Beans are flat and dark purple, about 40 per pod. [LA] S1
- **How it reached West Africa.** Tetteh Quarshie brought it to Ghana from Fernando Pó (Bioko) in 1878. [AM] S4
- **Uniformity and compatibility.** West African Amelonado is "the same in both countries [Ghana, Nigeria] and well known for its lack of inherent variability". [AM] S3 It is mostly self-compatible. S1, S5
- **Rules for the model.** Keep variation between trees low. Pods are green turning yellow, never red. Cotyledons are purple, never white.

Model notes:

- **Bean in pulp.** Model a pulp-covered bean as about 26 × 16 × 11 mm, a pulp layer of about 2 mm. [EST]
- **Cut cotyledon.** The two cotyledons are convoluted (folded) inside a thin membrane. The cut face is deep violet with a lighter rim (photo 01-bean-01).

- **Hypocotyl.** Use 5–8 cm, about 3–4 mm thick, pale green to reddish. [EST, bounded by S8]

**Flush rhythm on the seedling.** After the first four leaves, new leaves come in flushes about **every 6 weeks**, well spaced in a spiral. S1

**Phyllotaxis on the chupon (orthotropic).**

- Spiral, **3/8**. S5 (CPCRI); a 1951 Purdue abstract also gives the main trunk and chupons 3/8 [2nd].
- Orwa et al. give 5/8 for chupons (S2).
- Use 3/8, a divergence of 135°. 5/8 gives the same angle as 3/8 (225° = −135°).

**Leaves on the chupon vs the fan.**

- **Chupon:** long petiole with a pronounced pulvinus. **Fan:** short petiole with an inconspicuous pulvinus. S5
- Petiole range for the species: **1–10 cm**, "characteristically thickened at both ends". S1
- Use about 6–10 cm on chupons and 1–2.5 cm on fans. [EST, inside S1's range and S5's long/short]

**The two pulvini.**

- Every petiole has two pulvini: "one at the base and the other at the point of insertion of the leaf" (blade). S2
- Basal and apical pulvini are confirmed by the 1951 Purdue abstract [2nd].
- They are swollen, darker green to brown cylinders, roughly 1–1.5 × the petiole diameter and 0.5–1.5 cm long. They are longest on chupon leaves. [EST]
- The apical pulvinus turns the blade toward the light.

**Stipules.** Two, deciduous. S2

## 4. Jorquette and fan (plagiotropic) branches

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Height | **1–1.5 m** (S1); main stem kept at 1–2 m before the first jorquette (CPCRI pruning guide, S10) | |
| Age | about 1 year after field planting, so **≈ 15–18 months from sowing** | S1 |
| How it forms | the apical bud stops. **Five** subterminal axillary buds grow out sideways together. The internodes between them are so short that the fans leave at the same level. | S1 |
| Number of fans | **(3–)5(–6)** (S1); "branches in whorls of 5" (S2); 4–5 (Vikaspedia, via search) | |
| Phyllotaxis on fans | **1/2**, alternate and distichous, leaves in two ranks | S2, S5; 1951 Purdue abstract [2nd] |
| Fan angle | No source found. Fans leave at about 30–50° above horizontal, arch outward and droop to horizontal or below under leaf and pod load at their tips. Fan length 1–2 m by year 3, 2–3 m at maturity. | [EST], from photos 05-jorquette-01, 06-young-tree-02 and 11-green-pods-04 |
| Next tiers | "Some years later an orthotropic shoot or chupon may grow out from below the jorquette joint and, after growing a certain length, form another tier of fan branches. This may be repeated several times." | S1 |
| Practice | Plantations usually keep only the first jorquette. If it formed below 1.5 m, a second one is allowed, and chupons are removed regularly. Unpruned or old trees have 2–3 tiers. | S1, S11 [2nd] |
| Dimorphism | Not absolute. Orthotropic shoots can be induced on mature plagiotropic stems. Fan-derived clones grow bushy, without a trunk. | S1 |

Model notes:

- **Tier spacing.** Make the second tier about 1–1.5 m above the first, so a 3-tier tree is about 4–5 m. [EST]
- **Fan cross-section.** Fan branches are slightly flattened.

### Overall size

- **Height in cultivation:** usually 4–6 m (S1); 3–5 m (S14); 5–10 m (S2).
- **Height wild:** up to 20–25 m (S14 citing Lachenaud).
- Amelonado makes "large trees". [LA] S1
- **Trunk:** short. S2
- **Canopy area:** keeps growing linearly with age after height has plateaued, because the fans keep extending. S12

### Spacing

- 2.5–3 m between rows, 1000–1600 trees/ha (FAO, S9).
- 1100–1200 trees/ha at 4–6-month planting (S1).
- In West African smallholdings the spacing is often irregular, about 3 × 3 m. Use about 3 m, which gives a closed canopy with crowns of about 3–4 m at maturity. [EST]

### Lifespan

- **Economic life:** "over 50 years" when well managed (S15). The Amelonado farm at Agodi, Nigeria was 30–40 years old when studied (S3). A 47-year-old stand was measured in Nigeria (S13).
- **Full maturity:** about 10 years after transplanting. S15

### Roots

- **Taproot:** up to 2 m (S1); measured 1.0–1.3 m in Bahia (S14).
- **Laterals:** a dense mat of feeder roots up to **6 m long** in the **top 20 cm** of soil. S1; also S14: "almost all concentrated in the first 0.2 m".
- **Mycorrhizae:** possible. S1
- **Soil:** needs at least 1.5 m of deep soil. S1, S15

## 6. Bark, trunk and flower cushions

**Bark colour and texture**

- Young stems: green, then light brown, finely hairy at the tips. [SP] S16 [2nd]
- Mature bark: thin, grey-brown to dark brown. It is fairly smooth with fine longitudinal fissures, small lenticels and slight scaliness. S17 (growables, via search) [2nd]
- Old trunks: rougher and flaking, often leaning or crooked (photo 07-mature-tree-03).

**Lichens and moss**

- West African and Indian plantation trunks are typically blotched with **white or pale grey-green crustose lichen** patches. These are large irregular white maps up to 10 cm, densest on the lower 1.5 m. Shaded humid trunks also carry moss.
- Source: observation in photos 07-mature-tree-02, 08-trunk-bark-02, 11-green-pods-03, 11-green-pods-04, 09-flowers-03 and 10-cherelles-05. No literature value found.

**Flower cushions**

- **What they are.** Thickened leaf axils that bear flowers. They are distributed over the whole main stem and the branches. S5
- **Where they come from.** They grow from floral buds in the axils of leaves or cataphylls (scale leaves). S18
- **Where they appear first.** Flowering starts on the oldest trunk nodes and then spreads to the primary branches. S18, citing Swanson et al. 2008 [2nd]
- **What they look like.** Woody knobs about 1–2 cm across, growing larger each year (2–4 cm on old trunks). Cushions sit at old leaf scars, so they follow the trunk's 3/8 spiral and the fans' two ranks. [EST from photos 08-trunk-bark-01 and 09-flowers-04]
- **Flowering episode.** A cushion flowers for about 3+ weeks, up to 167 days at most. S18
- **Harvesting.** Pods must be cut off without damaging the cushion. S1

## 8. Flowers

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Diameter | **1–1.5 cm** (S1); 1–2 cm (S16); up to 2 cm (S19) | |
| Pedicel | 0.5–1.5 cm (S1); 1–4 cm (S2); about 2 cm, hairy (S19). Slender, greenish to reddish. | |
| Sepals | 5, oblong-lanceolate, **5–8 × 1.5–2 mm**, reflexed. White to reddish or pink (S1, S2, S19), fused for a few mm at the base. The Forastero / West African type is mostly whitish-pink. | |
| Petals | 5. Each has a narrow claw, then a concave **cup-shaped hood (cucullate pouch)** that encloses an anther pair, then a narrow strap ending in a spatulate **ligule**. The ligule is pale yellow and reflexed outward. | S1, S2, S19 |
| Guide lines | **dark purple (red-purple) bands/lines** on the inner face of the hood | S2 ("whitish-yellow with dark purple bands adaxially"), S5 ("reddish guidelines") |

| Stamens | 5 fertile, short, filaments reflexed, anthers (2 each) hidden in the hoods. Stamens and staminodes join at the base into a short tube about 1.5–2 mm. | S1, S2, S19 |
| Pistil | superior 5-locular ovary, 5 styles joined at the base | S1, S2 |
| Inflorescence | dichasial cymes, very short and compressed, on the cushions | S2, S5 |
| Flowers per cushion | **average 50** (over a season). Usually 1–10 open at once, among a tuft of round buds. | S5; open count [EST] from photos 09-flowers-04 and 09-flowers-06 |
| Flowers per tree | about 6,000 a year for "only about 20 pods" (S16). Each cushion keeps producing over time. | |
| Flower life | unpollinated flowers drop **24–36 h after anthesis** | S14 |
| Cauliflory | flowers only on the trunk and older (≥ 2–3-year-old, leafless) wood of the main branches, never on the leafy shoot tips | S1, S5, S18 |
| Pollinators | tiny **Forcipomyia** midges (Ceratopogonidae), giving 25–50 % cross-pollination | S1, S15, S16 |
| Pollination / fruit set | pollination efficiency below 10 %. **Pod set 0.5–5 %** of flowers (S14, citing Aneja 1999). About **1 in 500** flowers becomes a mature pod (S1). | |
| Fertilisation | within 36 h of pollination; then sepals, petals and staminodes fall | S2, S15 |
| Age at first flowering | 1.5–5 years after field planting (S1); 24–36 months after transplanting (S15); some hybrids at 18 months, others 3–5 years (S14). **Amelonado is late**: it "began to bear pods 5 years after field planting" in Ghana (S4, citing Abdul-Karimu et al. 2006); "coming into bearing late" (S1). | [AM]/[LA] |
