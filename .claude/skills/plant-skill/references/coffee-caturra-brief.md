Coffea arabica L. 'Caturra' — red Caturra, Caturra Vermelho (Rubiaceae): the dwarf mutation of Bourbon found in Minas Gerais, Brazil; an evergreen shrub with short internodes, compact: 1.7 m tall and 1.55 m across at 3–3.5 years, about 2 m at 11 years, a dense CYLINDER of leaves to the ground, red cherries. Its architecture is Roux's model: one upright main stem bearing opposite pairs of leaves, each node pair crossing the one below (decussate), and from the axils of those leaves a PAIR of horizontal side branches at every node, so the side branches stand out in four directions tier on tier, about 14 pairs a year, a dense green column (cylindrical, not a cone: Caturra's short internodes); the side branches never turn upright: they carry their leaves in two flat rows, in opposite pairs, and bear flowers and fruit in tight clusters at their leaf nodes.
Seed and seedling: the seed is sown in its parchment: a pale straw-beige papery husk, oval, 10.5 × 7.8 × 4.8 mm, flat on one face with a deep straight furrow down the middle, domed on the other; the green bean inside 8.4 × 6.3 × 3.5 mm, plano-convex, grey-green with a thin silvery skin left in its furrow. Germination epigeal: after about 50 days a pinkish-white stem 4–5 cm tall and 2 mm thick, first a hook then straight, holds the parchment up on its tip like a matchstick (the "soldier", fósforo); at 65–80 days the parchment falls and the two seed leaves open flat like a butterfly (chapola), each WIDER THAN LONG, 2.5 cm long × 3.5–4 cm wide, kidney-round, leathery, glossy mid-green with paler veins. Then true leaves in opposite pairs, light green, 5–8 cm at first.
Stem and bark: the main stem green and slightly four-angled when young, a light brown-grey bark from the base up after a year, at 3.5 years about 5 cm thick at the base, grey-brown, finely fissured lengthwise with pale lenticels; side branches thin (5–8 mm), green near their tips, light brown and finely flaking further back; small triangular green stipules between each pair of leaf stalks. Nodes close together: about 4 cm on the main stem, 2.5–4 cm along the side branches.
Leaves: opposite, simple, evergreen, living about 300 days; blade elliptic, 13 × 5.5 cm (10.5–16 × 4.7–7), a slender drip tip 1.3 cm, base wedge-shaped, the margin entire but WAVY (undulate), the blade a little puckered between 9–11 pairs of curved side veins; upper side very glossy deep green, under side paler matt green with small pits (domatia) in the vein angles; petiole short, 10 mm. New leaves at the shoot tips light glossy green (Caturra has green tips, not bronze); old leaves yellow before falling, so the inside of the side branches is bare where they fruited.
Flower: in dense clusters in the leaf axils along the side branches (2–4 short stalked cymes per axil, up to 10–20 flowers at a node), on nodes about one year old, all opening 10–15 days after the first rain and lasting 2–3 days; pure white, star-shaped, 18–20 mm across: a narrow white corolla tube 8–9 mm with 5 spreading pointed lobes 8–10 mm; 5 stamens with long cream anthers sticking out between the lobes; a slender white style ending in two curled stigma branches; the tiny green calyx a rim. Before opening the dormant buds are small and green (4–6 mm), then swell into long white closed "candles" 6–10 mm, crowded at the nodes.
Fruit: the cherry, a drupe in tight clusters of 8–15 at each node pair, hugging the branch on short stalks 3–5 mm, on last year's wood (a band of fruiting nodes, the inner nodes bare); ellipsoid, 15.2 × 11.3 mm when ripe (about 1.6 g), a little flattened on the two sides, the tip with a small round disc (the calyx scar) and a central point; skin smooth and glossy. By age: pinhead green 4 mm (6 weeks); hard green 10–12 mm (3 months); full-size green 15 mm (5–6 months); turning yellow-green then orange-red; ripe glossy bright red to cherry red (225–240 days after flowering); overripe dark wine-red. Cut across: thin red skin over a few mm of sweet pale yellow-orange pulp, slimy mucilage, two beans facing each other flat face to flat face, each in its pale parchment and silvery skin.
Roots: a short stout taproot 30–45 cm, often forked, 4–8 downward roots and many fine laterals, 90 % of the fine roots in the top 20 cm, spreading 1–1.5 m, light brown.
