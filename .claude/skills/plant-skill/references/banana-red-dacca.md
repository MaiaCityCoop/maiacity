## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** A tall triploid dessert banana (AAA), the type of its own **Red subgroup**. "A beautifully pigmented
  plant with deep red trunk, petioles, and midribs. It produces moderately sized bunches of fruit that have a
  reddish-green to deep maroon skin (depending on age and exposure to direct sunlight), and light orange flesh." [CV] S1.
  Queensland's note on it: "Distinctive red petioles, midribs and fruit." [CV] S2
- **Its marks.**
  - Tall and stout: about **5 m** to the throat at harvest in the tropics (S2, S4), **6.1 m** to the bunch in Brazil
    (S3); "large" (S7, S9).
  - **Slow**: about 16 months from harvest to harvest, against 10–12 for Cavendish (S2), and "18 months from planting
    to harvest" (S9).
  - Few, very fat fingers: about **85–92 per bunch**, **~50 mm** across, **~235–250 g** each (S2, S3).
  - The rachis below the hands is **bare**: the male bracts roll back and fall (S4, S5). This is the opposite of
    Dwarf Cavendish, whose dry bracts persist.
- **History.** "Said to be of Indian origin" (S7), now pan-tropical. It reached Hawaiʻi from Mexico and again from
  Puerto Rico in 1904 as 'Colorado' (S7). It was "the red banana of commerce" in Florida in 1922, "the largest sized
  variety of the various red bananas" (S8). Queensland calls it 'Red Dacca'; the ITC genebank holds it as ITC0575 and
  ITC0962, both from QDPI (S4, S13).
- **'Green Red', its green mutant. Do not confuse the two.** "Those in the red series often give rise to a green form
  (the reverse has not been recorded)" (S1). Queensland's 'Green Dacca' is "believed to be a tissue chimera originating
  from Red Dacca" (S2). Its fruit "is more green than red in color until maturity when the green portion turns yellow".
  Its trunks and fruit stems are "striped in some instances and are green on one side and red on the other", and many
  fruits are green on one side and red on the other (S7). See section 9 for its numbers.
- **'Dwarf Red'** (Dwarf Cuban Red, Dwarf Jamaican Red) is a separate short mutant (S1, S10). It is not modelled here.
- **Rules for the model.** Each axis flowers once (hapaxanthic). It is replaced by a follower sucker, so a mat shows
  mother, daughter and granddaughter together. Never give it seeds: the clone is sterile. A Green Red bunch once held
  150 seeds, but only about half a dozen were sound (S7).

- **Use for the model: pseudostem 4.5–5.0 m to the throat, base circumference 1.1–1.2 m, girth at 1 m 0.78–0.82 m.**
  The leaf tips stand 1.0–1.5 m higher, so the whole plant is **5.5–6.5 m**. [EST from S2–S4]
- **Height/girth ratio.** About 4.3 at 1 m (S2 height / S4 girth), so it is slimmer for its height than Dwarf
  Cavendish's 3.0. Still "robust" (S5) and "large and strong" (S7). [EST]
- **Bunch per metre of stem.** Only about **5.4 kg/m** of pseudostem height, against 14–19 for Cavendish. It is a big
  plant for its fruit. [CV] S2, Fig. 2b
- **Older, larger figures.** Pope gives "24 to 28 feet [7.3–8.5 m] tall" for the Red/Green Red, probably to the leaf
  tips. Morton reads this as the Green Red's height (S7, S9). Bananas.org gives 15–18 ft (4.6–5.5 m) pseudostem for
  'Jamaican/Cuban/Tall Red' (S12 [2nd]). Moreira's Brazilian classes put it in "high" (3.5–6 m) to "very high" (S3).

**Sheath (pseudostem) colour and blotches.** This is the cultivar's signature.

- **Exposed outer sheaths: red-purple.** Under the outer sheath: **pink-purple** (S4). "Deep red trunk" (S1).
  Musalogue scores its Comoros accession "green-red, shiny (not waxy)" (S5). Bananas.org: "maroon with some green and
  pink colored pseudo-stems" (S12 [2nd]).
- **Wax.** "Moderately waxy" (S4) to "shiny (not waxy)" (S5). Use a thin bloom that leaves the red showing, with a
  sheen.
- **Blotches at the petiole bases.** "Extensive blotching, black-purple" (S4); "small blotches, brown-black" (S5).
- **Sap.** Milky. [CV] S4, S5
- **Use for the model:**
  - Sun side of the outer sheaths: deep wine to maroon red-purple (≈ #6e1f35 to #8a2b45).
  - Shade side: lighter, with green showing through, an olive-maroon (≈ #6b4a3a).
  - Fresh inner sheaths: pink-purple to rose (≈ #c06a85).
  - Black-purple blotches (≈ #2a1420) clustered near the petiole bases at the top.
  - Old outer sheaths dry to brown-straw with a rusty-red tinge, split, hanging at the base.
  - [EST hex from S1, S4, S5 and photos]

**Growth rhythm.** Continuous, not in flushes: one cigar leaf at a time from the throat. [SP] Rate: see section 3.

**Phyllotaxis.** Spiral, sheaths concentric. Use **~140°** divergence. [SP; EST as for Cavendish]

**Branching (suckers, the mat).**

- Suckers grow **close to the parent, upright**, and reach "more than 3/4 of the height of the parent plant" (S5). So a
  Red mat is a tall, crowded clump of near-equal stems, not one mother over small pups. [CV] S5
- Number: no count found. "Not so exacting in its requirements" (S7). Use **3–6 suckers** per mat, thinned to one
  follower in cultivation. [EST]

## 6. "Bark": pseudostem surface, corm and roots

**Young pseudostem.**

- Glossy red-purple sheaths with a moderate wax bloom; black-purple blotches toward the top. [CV] S4, S5

**Old pseudostem.**

- The outer sheaths dry brown with a rusty-red tinge, split lengthwise and hang.
- The dead leaves form a brown skirt.
- The cut stem is wet and concentric. The inner sheaths are pink-purple (S4), and the core is cream-white [SP].
- Sap milky (S4, S5).

**Corm.** See section 1 ([EST]).

**Roots.**

- 200–300 cord roots in a vigorous Cavendish, about 4.3 mm thick, mostly in the top 15–40 cm. [SP] Cavendish sheet
  S7, S22
- Use 250–400 roots for Red, for its bigger corm. [EST]

## Gaps

These rows have no published number for Red; each is marked [EST] above.

- **Height and girth before shooting, and the month-by-month curve.** Only values at shooting/harvest exist (S2, S3,
  S4, S6). I interpolated from the Cavendish curve, scaled to Red's size and its 396-day shooting.
- **Leaves per cycle and leaf emission rate.** Not found. 40–45 leaves and ~1 per 9–10 days are inferred from the days
  to shooting.
- **Leaf life.** Cavendish value used.
- **Corm size and mass; root number.** Scaled from Cavendish by the base diameter.
- **Sucker number.** Only sucker height and position are published (S5).
- **Young-leaf flush.** The cigar is scored green (S4, S5). A red flush on new leaves is not published, so do not
  paint the blade bronze; colour only the midrib, petiole and margin.
- **Leaf under-face colour and all hex values.** Estimates from the descriptors and photos.
- **Female flower size and colour.** Not measured; set from the ovary pigmentation (S5).
- **Pseudostem girth at 1 m.** S4 and S6 give 0.80 and 0.77 m but do not say at what height. I assume the MusaNet
  standard of 1 m. S3's diameter is at 0.3 m.
- **Readings from bar charts.** S2's Red Dacca values (bunch ~27 kg, ~85 fingers, ~235 mm, ~51 mm, ~5.0 m, ~485 d,
  ~5.7 months) were read by eye from Figs 1–2, and are good to about ±3 % of the axis.
- **Fusarium race 1.** Sources conflict: S2 says susceptible, S10 says resistant in south Florida, and S7 and S9 say

- **Pope's 24–28 ft plant and 12–14 ft blades (S7).** This description sits under the Green Red heading. Its fruit

## Look (what the eye sees, for an image brief)

- **Plantlet / sword sucker (0–3 months).**
  - Narrow upright leaves, mid green.
  - The petioles and the short stem already flushed rose to wine-red.
  - The midrib is a thin red line seen from below.
- **Young plant (3–9 months, 1–3 m).**
  - Glossy wine-red stem, with greener shade sides and a faint bloom.
  - Long red petioles (0.5–0.6 m), with purple-brown winged margins and black-purple blotches at the base.
  - Leaves 1.5–2.5 m long, dark green above, glaucous below with a pink-red midrib stripe, arching.
  - Tall red suckers crowding at the base.
- **Grown plant at shooting (≈ 13 months).**
  - A 4.5–5 m maroon-red column, about 0.37 m thick at the base and 0.25 m thick at 1 m.
  - Black-purple blotches around the crown and a skirt of brown dead leaves.
  - 10–13 leaves, 2.4–2.9 × 0.7–0.9 m, arching to drooping, many torn into strips.
  - Overall 5.5–6.5 m tall, clearly taller than a Cavendish beside it.
- **Inflorescence.**
  - A long purple, finely hairy peduncle (> 0.6 m) arches out of the crown, and the bunch hangs straight down.
  - The bud is ovoid, about 23 × 10 cm, purple-brown with a frosty wax bloom.
  - Bracts lift one at a time and roll back, showing a red inner face that fades pale at the base. They then fall.
  - Beneath the female hands is a **bare, knobbly rachis** with very prominent scars, ending in the bud. There is no
    dry-bract tail.
- **Young bunch.**
  - 5–7 hands of short, fat, blunt fingers in two rows, lying along the rachis.
  - Peel purple-maroon, waxy, with greenish undertone on the shaded side.
  - A dried style at each tip.
- **Full bunch (≈ 5.7 months after shooting).**
  - About 85–95 fingers, 16–21 cm long and 4.5–5 cm thick, nearly straight and round.
  - Deep maroon to purple-brown, dull with bloom. The bunch is about 20–30 kg.
- **Ripe fruit.**
  - Brick red to red-orange, coppery where sun-ripened, yellow-orange in the creases and on the shaded side.
  - Thick peel (about 3.7 mm).
  - Flesh pale salmon-orange to apricot cream, soft, sweet, with a distinct core.
- **Over-ripe.** Dark brown-black flecks on the red; the skin dulls to purple-brown.
- **Mat.** Two or three near-equal tall red stems with a lean, the oldest skirted in dry red-brown sheaths.
