Dacryodes edulis (G.Don) H.J.Lam var. edulis (Burseraceae) — the safou, African pear, bush butter, prune: the cultivated large-fruited variety of Cameroon, Nigeria and the Congo basin, a seedling tree of the home gardens and cocoa farms, a FEMALE (fruiting) tree. A dense evergreen tree 8–12 m tall in cultivation, branched LOW, its crown deep, dense and rounded to broadly ovoid, about as wide as tall.
Seed: no hard stone. One oblong, slightly flattened seed about 4.5 × 2 × 1.6 cm, 12 g, its thin papery pellicle LIGHT GREEN to pinkish, inside a thin brownish corky endocarp; inside, two fleshy light-green cotyledons, each folded and palmately 4–5-lobed. Recalcitrant: sown fresh, lying flat on its side, 2 cm deep, its radicle often already out. Germination EPIGEAL: in 2–3 weeks a green-to-reddish hypocotyl 5–7 cm lifts the two thick green lobed cotyledons above the soil (each about 4 × 2 cm, deeply cut into 4–5 finger-like lobes), and between them the first two leaves come together, small pinnate leaves of 1–3 pairs of leaflets, rose-violet as they unfold. A strong white taproot goes straight down; few laterals in the first year.
Stem and bark: young shoots green, densely covered with rust-brown (ferruginous) star-shaped hairs; each flush starts with a bare stretch of scars where its small scale leaves fell. Young bark smooth, grey-green to light grey with small pale lenticels. Old trunk bark pale yellowish-grey, shallowly scaly, with horizontal folds and lenticels, weeping small drops of translucent whitish aromatic resin that harden in place (the "tears" of Dacryodes); a short straight trunk, slightly fluted, no buttresses, about 20 cm across at 10 years, branching at 0.5–1.5 m into tiers of 3–5 stout ascending limbs (Rauh's model: every axis grows upright in rhythmic flushes, branches in tiers from the top of a flush).
Leaves: alternate, spiral, crowded toward the shoot tips; IMPARIPINNATE, 30–45 cm long: a grooved petiole 5–7 cm, a rachis 25–35 cm, 6–7 pairs of opposite to subopposite leaflets plus one terminal leaflet, each on a short petiolule 0.5–1 cm. Leaflets oblong to oblong-lanceolate, 10–16 × 4–6 cm (smaller toward the base of the leaf), acuminate with a short drip tip, base rounded and lopsided, margin entire; 9–15 pairs of looping side veins; upper side DARK GREEN and VERY GLOSSY, underside paler dull green with a raised midrib. Young flush leaves soft, limp, hanging, ROSE-VIOLET to pinkish-brown, turning bronze-green and then green over 3–4 weeks.
Flowers: in the dry season (January–February), panicles 15–20 cm near the shoot tips, their branched, grooved axes densely rust-brown hairy; on a female tree the panicles are short and inconspicuous. Flowers small, about 5 mm across, in threes: 3 brown hairy sepals, 3 incurved petals brown-hairy outside and CREAM-YELLOW inside, 6 short white stamens (sterile in a female flower) round a 6-lobed yellow-green nectar disc and a green ovoid ovary with a short 2–3-lobed stigma.
Fruit: a drupe like a large olive, ELLIPSOID-CYLINDRICAL with round ends, 7.5 × 3.6 cm (range 5–12 × 3–5 cm), 50 g, on a short stout woody stalk 0.5–1.5 cm, in loose clusters of 3–15 on the woody branched infructescence. Fruit set to ripe about 145 days. Young fruit green tinged pink, then PINK to ROSE-RED while it grows to full length (by 8–15 weeks); in its last 3–5 weeks the skin darkens from the tip and in patches to BLUE-VIOLET, then DEEP BLUE-BLACK to violet-black, thin, waxy and glossy with a faint bloom. Cut lengthwise: a thin skin, 6 mm of PALE GREEN buttery flesh (cream-green), a thin corky brownish endocarp, and the light-green lobed seed filling the middle.
Roots: one strong white-to-tan taproot first, laterals later in the top 30–50 cm.

LAYOUT:
Row 1, the life of the tree, all standing on one thin grey ground line, soil cut away so the seed and upper roots show: the fresh seed lying flat 2 cm under the soil line, its root tip out; germinating at 3 weeks, the hypocotyl lifting the two green finger-lobed cotyledons above the soil, the first pair of small rose-violet pinnate leaves between them, a white taproot down; the seedling at 6 months, 0.5 m, a single stem with 5–7 compound leaves, the newest flush rose-violet at its tip; the young tree at 2 years, 1.5 m, its first tier of 3–4 ascending branches at about 0.6 m; the young tree at 5 years, 4 m tall and 3.5 m wide, low-branched, dense; the grown female tree at 10 years, 7 m tall and 6.5 m wide, a SHORT trunk branching at about 1 m, its crown a dense dark-green rounded dome down to about 1.5 m off the ground, like a big rounded haystack and NOT a ball on a stick, clusters of dark blue-violet fruit at the ends of its shoots.
Row 2, the parts close up, laid flat, photographed straight down: the 6-month seedling's root system washed, the long taproot; three bark swatches side by side (green young shoot with rust-brown hairs, grey-green young bark with lenticels, pale grey scaly old trunk bark with whitish resin drops, each 8 cm); one whole grown compound leaf, upper side, all 13–15 leaflets; one leaflet, underside; a young rose-violet flush leaf, limp; a shoot tip with a female panicle in flower, its rusty axes.
