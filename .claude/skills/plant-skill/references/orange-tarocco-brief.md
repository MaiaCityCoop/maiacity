Citrus × sinensis (L.) Osbeck 'Tarocco' — the Sicilian blood orange of the plain of Catania and the slopes of Etna, a sweet orange whose flesh is flushed red by anthocyanins after cold winter nights; ripe from January to March. An evergreen tree, budded onto a rootstock (sour orange, Citrus × aurantium, or the citrange 'Carrizo'): its seedlings are the rootstock, the Tarocco is the bud grafted on.
Seed (the rootstock's): 9–12 × 5–7 mm, pale cream, flattened ovoid, its coat wrinkled and ridged; polyembryonic, hypogeal: the seed stays in the soil, a slender green shoot rises, its first leaves small, narrow and glossy, their stalks barely winged.
Trunk and bark: young shoots bright green and sharply three-angled for their first months, rounding and turning grey-green, then fine grey-brown bark, smooth with faint longitudinal lines and pale lichen spots on old wood; never deeply fissured. Trunk 12–18 cm across at 8 years, a short clear trunk of 40–60 cm; the bud union 15–25 cm above the soil, a slight step where the scion is a little narrower or wider than the stock. Few short thorns (under 1 cm) in the leaf axils, mostly on vigorous young shoots.
Leaves: unifoliate (a compound leaf of one leaflet), blade 8–12 cm × 4–6 cm, elliptic to ovate, a bluntly pointed tip often slightly notched, the margin entire or faintly crenate, a little wavy; upper side dark glossy green (#2b4a1e to #33561f), underside paler yellow-green and matt with fine translucent oil dots; stalk 1–2 cm with a narrow wing 2–5 mm wide at its top, jointed to the blade. Young flush leaves pale lime green, thin and soft; the crown carries two or three years of leaves.
Flowers: single or in short racemes of 2–6 at the shoot ends of the spring flush, among young leaves or on leafless shoots; buds white, plump, 1–1.5 cm; open flower 3–4 cm across, five thick waxy pure-white petals 1.5–2 cm long, curving back, dotted with oil glands; 20–25 stamens with yellow anthers joined at the base in bundles round a green-yellow style with a yellow knob stigma; a small green cup of five sepals.
Fruit: medium-large, 7–8.5 cm across, 200–280 g, round to slightly obovoid, often a short collar or neck at the stem end; rind 4–6 mm, finely pitted by oil glands, smooth to slightly pebbled; dark glossy green while it grows, turning in winter to deep orange, a red-burgundy blush spreading over part of the cheek in cold weather; a small green star-shaped calyx and a short woody stalk. Cut across: 10–12 segments round a small central core, almost seedless; the flesh orange flecked and streaked with crimson, the red varying from a few streaks to most of it; a white albedo layer 2–3 mm under the orange flavedo.
Roots: a taproot and spreading laterals, the fine feeder roots mostly in the top 60 cm, light brown to orange-brown.
