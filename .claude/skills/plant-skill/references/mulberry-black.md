# Morus nigra L. (black mulberry, 'Chelsea' / 'King James'): facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for a black mulberry tree grown stage by stage, from the seed (or, as it is really raised, a
hardwood cutting) to a gnarled, leaning tree several centuries old in a European garden or a Mediterranean or Anatolian
village. Built on the template `datasheet.md` and set out like `plum-hauszwetschge.md`. Where the way it is grown changes
a number (own roots or grafted onto *M. alba*, UK or Anatolia), the change has its own line.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured on a named clone of *M. nigra* ('Chelsea' = 'King James' = 'King James I', 'Charlton House',
  'Black Persian', 'Jerusalem', 'Wellington', 'Noir of Spain', 'Kaester', 'Martinus', 'Shakespeare'). **None of the
  measured papers below names one of these**, so almost nothing here is [CV]. What the RHS says of 'King James' is marked
  [CV] where it is about that clone, but it is a garden description, not a measurement.
- **[SP]**: said of *Morus nigra* in general, including the measured papers on unnamed Turkish, Iranian and Spanish
  "genotypes" (local trees). This is less of a loss than it looks: 359 *M. nigra* accessions from Anatolia and Iran,
  among them more than 250 ancient trees, were **identical at all 15 SSR loci** (S15), and the cultivars "show little
  genuine variation; differences in fruit size and quality mostly reflect the environment" (S2). The species behaves
  almost like one clone, so a species number is close to a cultivar number.
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with the reasoning given. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a snippet, a search summary or a page that cites the work, not the original.

**Careful with names.** 'Shahtoot' / 'Shah Toot' (the long-fruited "king mulberry" of Pakistan and Iran) is *M. alba*
(often put under *M. macroura*), not *M. nigra*, although ICRAF lists "shah-tut" among the Hindi names of *M. nigra*
(S1). 'Black Persian' and 'Black Beauty' are sold as *M. nigra* but some nursery stock is *M. alba* (S25); many North
American "M. nigra" records are misidentified *M. alba* (S2 context, S15: red/purple-fruited "M. rubra" of Anatolia turned
out to be *M. alba*). Fruit colour does not identify the species (S1). The marks that do: leaf hairy all over beneath and
rough above, base deeply cordate, petiole short, fruit almost stalkless, very large black-brown buds, and leafing out last.

Each number has a source tag such as S1; the references are at the end. Units: millimetres for small organs, centimetres
for leaves and trunks, metres for trees, grams, days and years. Tree ages count from planting a rooted cutting or a
grafted tree unless a row says otherwise.

## 0. The species in one paragraph

- **What it is.** A small, broad, slow, very long-lived deciduous tree of the Moraceae, cultivated so long in the
  Caucasus, northern Iran and Anatolia that its wild range is unknown (S2, S15). Romans grew it; it reached Gaul and
  southern England with them, Iberia in the 8th century, and northern Europe's monastery gardens in the Middle Ages (S2).
  In England, James VI & I's edict of 1607–1609 planted thousands for silk; the survivors (Charlton House c. 1608–11,
  Balliol and Merton colleges c. 1609–11) are still alive and fruiting (S2, S21).
- **The clone 'Chelsea' / 'King James'.** Descended from a tree in the Chelsea Physic Garden planted under James I,
  felled in the Second World War for an air-raid shelter after cuttings were taken; now the most popular UK clone (S2).
  RHS: "crooked and gnarled branches", "self-fertile and crops from a young age", "large, juicy dark red to black
  fruit in late summer", leaves heart-shaped, toothed, yellow in autumn, H6, 8–12 m × >8 m, maturity in 20–50 years,
  Award of Garden Merit (S7). No paper measures it.
- **Its marks for the model.** A short, thick, often leaning trunk, deeply fissured, burred; a dense, low, spreading
  crown wider than tall; crooked, zigzag branches; rough, heart-shaped leaves; fruit like a short fat blackberry, almost
  stalkless, ripening a few at a time over 8–10 weeks, so a branch carries green, red and black fruit at once (S2, S16).
- **The curiosity.** 2n = 22x = **308** chromosomes, the highest count of any seed plant reported in the genus and among
  the highest of all seed plants (S5 citing Ottman 1987; S2; S15).
- **Rules for the model.** One tree, own-rooted (from a cutting or a layer), monoecious, partly parthenocarpic.
  Fruit on the current season's short shoots from one-year wood. Leaves lobed only on seedlings, suckers and very
  vigorous young shoots. In age the trunk splits, leans and lays limbs on the ground, which root.

## 1. Seed and germination

The fruit is a **sorosis**: the whole female catkin becomes the fruit. Each female flower becomes a **drupelet**: a thin
stone (the "seed" sold and sown, botanically a pyrene) inside the swollen, juicy, fleshy calyx (perianth) of that flower
(S5: "the seed should be classified as a drupelet rather than an achene"; "the calyx adheres to the ovary and becomes an
accessory part of the drupelet").

- **Growth tempo.** "Slow-growing" (S3); "the slowest of the mulberry species" (S25). ICRAF's "trees grow very rapidly"
  (S1) is generic and contradicts every specific source. A 75-year-old tree already "looks like a veteran", at least
  twice its age (S21). Ageing is unreliable: rings are irregular and the old trees are hollow (S2, S16).
- **Grafted vs own-rooted.** On *M. alba* seedlings: faster, earlier harvest, but shorter-lived (none older than 30–40 y
  seen, S16).

### Growth rhythm

- **Bud break very late**: one of the last trees to leaf. "Of all cultivated trees, it is the very last to bud, and it
  does not do so until the cold weather is entirely passed", hence "the wisest of trees" (Pliny, *Natural History* XVI,
  as quoted, S26 [2nd]). In Britain buds open in May ("middle or latter end of May", 1778 Britannica, S26 [2nd]; "early
  May", Morus Londinium, S32 [2nd]). Measured in Tokat (north Anatolia, 2017): bud swell **20 April**, bud burst **29
  April**, fruit set **7 May**, against 24–29 March and 10–17 April for *M. rubra* and *M. laevigata* in the same
  orchard: **about 2–3 weeks later** (S9). Tokat 2019–20, other genotypes: bud burst 9–14 April (S10); bud swell 9–20
  April across 31 genotypes (S11). The name *Morus* is sometimes derived from Latin *mora*, delay, for the late buds
  (S1).
- **One main flush**: shoots grow from bud break to about midsummer; their soft tips then die back to the top lateral
  bud (sympodial, no terminal bud) [EST, genus]. Extension of vigorous young shoots 40–80 cm, of old cropping trees 5–20
  cm; fruiting short shoots 3–10 cm [EST].
- **Leaf fall**: leaves yellow and fall in **October–November**: Tokat 10 October (S9), "second and third weeks of
  November" across the species there (S9 abstract); bare from November to April in London (S21).

### Branching and the year-by-year architecture

- **Habit.** "Tree 6–12 m, with a clean bole or branching almost from the base … crown dense and spreading, often wider
  than tall … mature trees often lean, then collapse and layer to produce new upright stems" (S2). "Slender but with
  numerous branches … tends to be a bush if not trained when young" (S1). "Spreading crown, picturesque when old" (S3).
  "Gnarly and zigzagged, spreading with a low canopy" (S25). "Crooked and gnarled branches" ('King James', S7). "Broad,
  rounded, half-open … capricious growing" (S8).
- **Old age.** In Anatolia "when they age, the trees lean over, the trunk splits into several parts that lean apart, or
  the big limbs lie down", so that in orchards centuries old one cannot always tell one tree with many limbs from
  several planted in one pit (2–3 were often planted together, *ocak dikim*) (S16). Old brittle branches need **Y-shaped
  props padded with hessian** (S7). Cuts over 5 cm across do not heal; the tree bleeds latex if pruned in growth (S1).
- **Year by year** [EST, from the above and standard training]:
  - **Year 0–3**: a leader with 3–5 side shoots set alternately, 40–60° from the vertical; zigzag internodes from the
    sympodial growth; lower shoots kept or removed to make a 1–2 m stem.
  - **Year 3–8**: a framework of 8–10 branches (bush, S7). One-year shoots carry **mixed buds**; the short shoots growing
    from them flower and fruit (section 5).
  - **Year 8–30**: crown spreads faster than it rises; the lower limbs arch out nearly horizontal and, near the ground,
    sag; short shoots crowd on the upper side of old limbs; inner shade twigs die.
  - **Year 30–100**: the trunk thickens, fissures deeply and grows burrs; it begins to lean (often towards the light or the
    prevailing lee) 10–30°; a limb or two breaks under fruit or wind, sprouts water shoots, or touches down and roots.
  - **Year 100+**: the heartwood rots; the trunk splits into 2–4 parts, each leaning outward; props; layered limbs give a
    ring of daughter stems; the crown becomes a low mound, wider than ever, and keeps fruiting.

### Phyllotaxis

- Alternate. On strong upright shoots spiral (≈2/5); on the side shoots and fruiting shoots the leaves turn into **two
  ranks** (distichous) [EST, genus: mulberry varieties are recorded with 1/2, 1/3, 2/5 and 3/8 phyllotaxis; not found
  for *M. nigra*].
- Stipules **lanceolate, 2–3 cm, hairy**, falling early (S2).

### Roots

- Deep and spreading; "root damage potential high" (S25); established trees tolerate some drought through deep roots
  but drop fruit unripe if the roots dry (S1). Roots of genus seedlings "rather stout and tough" (S5). No measured
  depths for *M. nigra* [gap]. Model: a few thick, wandering, shallow-ish laterals 1.5–2× the crown radius; a sinker
  zone to 2–3 m [EST].

## Gaps

- **No measured named clone.** Not one paper measures 'Chelsea', 'King James', 'Wellington', 'Black Persian' or
  'Jerusalem'. All measured numbers are of unnamed genotypes in Turkey, Iran and Spain, or single dated trees. Given
  the near-identity of all *M. nigra* (S15), I treat them as true of 'Chelsea', with fruit size from the top of the range
  ("very large fruits", S7) as an estimate.
- **Seed size and weight**: only a forum quote (3–4 mm) and "clearly larger" (S9). Weight estimated by scaling *M.
  alba*.
- **Seedling morphology** (hypocotyl, cotyledon size, when the first lobed leaf comes): estimated from the genus being
  epigeal (S5). Check the single Commons seedling photo.
- **Height, trunk and crown by age**: one 8-year grafted tree (S9), one ~50-year tree (S21), and old trees without a
  firm age (S21, S22, S23). Everything between is interpolated. Height by itself misleads in old trees (S21).
- **Yield per tree**: only an unread Egyptian trial (51 and 96 kg, S30) and the genus (red mulberry 3.7 hl, S5). The
  model values (10–30 kg, 30–80 kg) are estimates.
- **Phyllotaxis, architecture model, roots**: genus analogy, not measured on *M. nigra*.
- **Persistent stigmas on the fruit**: from the long stigmas of the flower (S11) and photos; no flora read says so.
- **Drupelet size, tooth count, vein count, bark colours of old trees**: estimated from photos and fruit volume.
- **Climate**: no chilling requirement, no minimum heat sum, no rainfall range measured.
- **Fruit growth curve**: one genotype, one year, six points, and the fruit weight units in the figure are unclear (S9).
- **Floras not read**: Flora of China (efloras would not load; its numbers came from a mirror snippet, S4 [2nd]),
  Flora Europaea vol. 1 (Tutin et al. 1964), Flora Iberica, Davis's Flora of Turkey vol. 7, Ghafoor's Flora of Pakistan
  171 (1985). **Morton's *Fruits of Warm Climates* (1987), "Mulberry"**, was asked for but the Purdue server returned
  503 every time; not read, not used.
- **Tree registers**: the Tree Register of the British Isles and monumentaltrees.com could not be opened (403); the
  British maxima (≈15 m, >5 m girth) are from S2.
- **Second-hand values ([2nd])**: S4, S12, S13, S14, S15, S17, S19, S20, S23, S24, S26, S27, S28, S29, S30, S31, S32,
  the cited Turkish theses inside S9 and S11 (Güneş & Çekiç 2003, 2004; Erkaleli & Dalkılıç 2016; Koyuncu & Vural 2003),
  and the 'Chelsea'/'Jerusalem' female note from Wikipedia.
