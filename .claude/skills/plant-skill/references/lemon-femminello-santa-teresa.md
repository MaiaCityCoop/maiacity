# Citrus × limon (L.) Osbeck 'Femminello Santa Teresa': facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for a Sicilian lemon grown stage by stage, from seed or from a grafted nursery tree. Built on the
template `datasheet.md` and set out like `banana-dwarf-cavendish.md`. Where a Sicilian orchard practice changes the tree
(pruning, forcing for verdelli), it is given on its own line.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured on 'Femminello Santa Teresa' itself (also written 'F. S. Teresa', 'Femminello Sta. Teresa',
  'Santa Teresa').
- **[SP]**: said of the Femminello group (other Femminello clones, measured), of the lemon species, or of *Citrus*.
  The row says which.
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with the reasoning given. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a snippet, or a review that cites the work, not the original.

Each number has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are metres, kilograms, days and years.
Ages run from planting the grafted nursery tree unless a row says otherwise ("from seed" is said where it applies).

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** A clonal selection of the Femminello group, "reported to be a selection of Femminello Ovale". Its
  mother tree was a healthy old tree found in a Femminello orchard almost destroyed by mal secco
  (*Plenodomus tracheiphilus*, formerly *Phoma tracheiphila*). It was valued for "greater resistance to the mal secco
  disease than any other clone found thus far" (Hodgson in *The Citrus Industry* 1967, quoted by UCR). [CV] S1, S3
- **The group.** Femminello lemons are "the most important lemon group in Italy". They are about three quarters of
  Italian production (Burke 1962, via S3), and the cultivar base of the Siracusa and Etna PGIs. Trees "tend to have few
  thorns and flower and set fruit throughout the year". [SP] S2, S3, S10
- **Its marks against other Femminellos.** The smallest, roundest fruit of 24 lemons in the Catania collection
  (122 g, 62.3 mm across, 67.2 mm long, a short 7 mm nipple). Many seeds (14–15 a fruit). Almost every flower has a
  working pistil (7.7 % pistil abortion, among the lowest). Normal purple pigment in buds, petals and young shoots (not a
  'Zagara Bianca'). [CV] S4, S5, S6
- **Rules for the model.** Evergreen. Remontant: it flowers on every flush, so a grown tree carries flowers, green fruit
  and yellow fruit at once. Shoots grow in flushes and stop by tip abortion, so branching is sympodial. In Sicily the
  tree is a grafted, pruned globe or open vase, kept at about 3.5–4 m.

- **Canopy volume.** 11.3–28.7 m³ in the Femminello clones at about 12 years (S7). [SP]
- **Trunk growth curve.** In the 18-cultivar trial the trunk area grew slowly for 5 years, then fast "starting from the
  sixth year"; the most vigorous clone (Femminello Cerza) gained **976 %** in TCSA over 10 years. [SP] S8
- **Rootstock effect.** Volkameriana gives the tallest trees, Carrizo medium, sour orange medium. [SP] S9
- **Forcing effect.** Summer drought for verdelli cut height and trunk growth (2 y in tubs: 2.29–2.38 m against
  2.60–3.00 m). [SP] S9

**Training in Sicily.** A **globe** ("trained to the globe system", S8) or open vase on 3–4 scaffolds from 0.5–0.8 m.
Pruning in April limits height for hand picking and opens the crown to light (S11). The model should keep the crown at
**3.5–4 m** and clip any vertical water shoots. [SP] S8, S11; height [EST]

**Growth rhythm (flushes).** Three main flushes a year in Sicily: **spring** (February–April, the biggest, bearing the main
bloom), **summer** (June–July) and **autumn** (September–October, the verdelli flush after forcing). Each flush grows for
about **3–5 weeks**, then its tip aborts. Spring shoots **10–25 cm**; summer and autumn shoots longer, **20–50 cm**;
water sprouts **0.5–1.2 m**. [EST: from the lemon flowering calendar in S9 and general citrus habit; S7 measured shoot
growth by period but gives no table]. After re-watering a forced tree, buds break and flower in about **10 days**; late
cultivars take 15–20 days (S9). [SP]

**Shoots and thorns.**

- Young shoots are angular (ridged under each petiole), green, flushed **red-purple** while growing; the pigment fades as
  the shoot hardens (S9). [SP]
- Thorns: the group has "few thorns" (S2); Femminello Ovale is "nearly thornless" (S3). [SP]
- Model: one thorn in perhaps **1 axil in 10** on vigorous shoots, **5–12 mm** long, 2–2.5 mm at the base, straight,
  green, then brown [EST: count from S2/S3; size from the juvenile thorns of 'Yellow Star', a Femminello cross:
  10–15 mm × 2.5 mm, S15]. None on fruiting twigs. Seedlings are thornier.

**Branching and angles.** Sympodial: 2–4 shoots from the buds below an aborted tip. Side shoots at **40–60°** from the
parent; scaffolds at 35–50° from vertical; fruiting twigs droop under fruit [EST].

**Phyllotaxis.** Alternate, spiral. No measured value found. Use **2/5 (144°)** [EST].

**Internodes.** **1.5–3 cm** on flush shoots, 4–6 cm on water sprouts, under 1 cm at a shoot's base and top [EST].

**Roots.** See section 6.

## 6. Bark and roots

**Young shoot.** Green, angular (three ridges below each leaf), glabrous, flushed red-purple at the tip while growing;
dark green ("Brunswick green") under 2 years. [SP] S9; colour from S15

**Thorns.** Few, short, green then brown (section 2).

**Young bark.** Smooth, green-grey, turning grey-brown by 3–4 years. [EST]

**Old bark.** "Grey-brown, smooth" (S9) [SP]. On old trunks: fine shallow cracks and thin flakes, often with grey-green
lichen spots. Scaffolds smooth grey [EST].

**Graft union.** A slight step 20–30 cm above the soil, the sour orange below a little thicker (1–9 %, S7) and darker,
rougher. On sour orange the trunk base may show gum scars from collar rot (*Phytophthora*) [EST].

**Roots.**

- Tap root short; the system is wide and shallow. Most roots lie in the top **0–25 cm** (Syrian rootstock study).
  The main mass is at **0.6–0.9 m**, and roots reach **1.2–1.5 m** in deep soils. [SP] S17 [2nd]
- Sour orange roots deeper than Carrizo or trifoliate (these had no roots below 1 m). On Florida sands sour orange
  reached **2.7 m**. [SP] S17 [2nd]
- Spread: about **1.5×** the crown radius (to 2.5–4 m from the trunk in a grown tree) [EST].
- Fine roots light brown, no root hairs to speak of [EST from the genus].

## Gaps

These rows have no published number for 'Femminello Santa Teresa' (and some none for lemon). Each is marked [EST] above.

- **Architecture model.** No source assigns *Citrus* to a Hallé–Oldeman model in what I could read. Scarrone's is my
  reading of flush growth, tip abortion and terminal flowers.
- **Seed size in mm, cotyledon life, first-leaf arrangement and size.** Only the seed mass (0.14 g) is measured. Size from
  mass and shape; the rest from the genus.
- **Nursery tree caliper and graft height.** Caliper back-calculated from S8; graft height from S8's 30 cm measuring point
  and common practice. Only Interdonato's 50–60 cm is written down (S14).
- **Heights and trunks at 1, 5, 8 and 20 years in the field.** Years 1–2 come from tub-grown Femminello Zagara Bianca (S9),
  year 3 from trunk area (S8), years 10–12 from Femminello clones in Sardinia (S7). The rest is interpolated. Crown widths
  before year 10 are estimates.
- **Flush lengths, dates and internodes; shoot angles; phyllotaxis (2/5).** No measured lemon values read.
- **Thorn frequency and size.** "Few" and "nearly thornless" are published (S2, S3); the size is taken from a Femminello
  cross (S15).
- **Leaf size for Santa Teresa, petiole length and wing width, leaf colours as hex, leaf life.** Species ranges only; leaf
  life from extension anecdotes (15–24 months).
- **Flower diameter, flowers per cluster, locule count, calyx.** From a Femminello cross (S15) and the genus.
- **Fruit set (2–6 %), June drop size, the growth curve by days, colour-break timing.** No Femminello curve found; the
  days are from the PGI harvest windows (S10) and the crop calendar (S9).
- **Stalk and calyx button size.** Estimated.
- **Yield of a grown tree (50–80 kg), full-bearing age, productive life.** From the PGI cap (S10), the trunk-growth jump
  (S8) and general lemon life.
- **Root spread.** Estimated; depths are [2nd].
- **Frost limits for leaves and wood.** Fruit limits are [2nd]; leaf and wood limits estimated.
- **Rind thickness.** Santa Teresa's 3.85 mm is from the California desert on *C. macrophylla* (S6). In Italy the
  Femminello clones measure 5–10 mm (S7, S8), so the model uses 4–6 mm.
- **Second-hand values ([2nd]).** S12 (Flora of China, read in a reprint), S16 (germination), S17 (roots), S18 (frost),
  S19 (leaf life), S20 (tip abortion), S21 and S22 were read only as abstracts or snippets. The Torrisi, Calabrese and
  Crescimanno results are cited by S9 and were not read in the original.
