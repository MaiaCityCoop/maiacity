Persea americana Mill. 'Fuerte' (Lauraceae) — the Fuerte avocado: a natural Mexican × Guatemalan race hybrid found at Atlixco, Puebla, Mexico, in 1911, for decades the avocado of California. A large, spreading evergreen tree, grafted onto a seedling rootstock; flower type B.
Seed (stone): a single large seed, broadly ovoid to round-conical, about 5–5.5 × 4–4.5 cm, 40–50 g, its two thick cream to pale-yellow cotyledons pressed together, wrapped in a thin papery seed coat that is brown and loose. Sown with its broad end down and its pointed top third left out of the soil. Germination hypogeal: the stone splits in two from its base, a thick white taproot goes straight down, and a stout green-to-reddish shoot rises from between the two halves; the cotyledons stay in the stone and feed the seedling for months. The shoot's first nodes carry only small scale leaves; true leaves come in a tuft at its tip, red-bronze and limp when they unfold, greening as they firm.
Stem and bark: young shoots green, smooth, faintly downy, angled; one- and two-year wood olive to grey-brown with pale lenticels; the trunk of a grown tree grey-brown, rough, finely and irregularly furrowed into small corky plates, often with a slight graft step 20–30 cm above the soil. A grown Fuerte is broader than tall: 2.4 m high and 3 m across in its fourth year, 5–6.5 m high and 7–8 m across at 10 years, 8–10 m and 12 m across at 20; a short trunk (about 16 cm across at 10 years, 21 cm at 20) branching low, at about 1–1.5 m, into 4–7 heavy spreading limbs, the crown a dense dome whose outer skirt reaches down nearly to the ground.
Leaves: alternate, spirally set and crowded toward the shoot tips; blade elliptic to oblong, 14–20 × 6–9 cm, its tip drawn out to a point, wedge-shaped base, entire margin slightly wavy, smooth, leathery; upper side dark glossy green with a pale yellow-green midrib and 6–9 pairs of fine curving lateral veins, underside paler, matt and glaucous blue-green; petiole long and slender, 4.5–6.5 cm. Young flush leaves bronze-red to reddish-brown, soft and hanging, greening over about a month; old leaves yellow before they fall, many at once in spring flowering. Crushed leaves smell of anise (Mexican race).
Flowers: in late winter to spring, many-flowered panicles (each 10–20 cm) at the ends of last year's shoots, the shoot growing on from the panicle's tip; flowers small, 1–1.3 cm across, six greenish-yellow to cream tepals in two whorls of three, nine stamens in three whorls with orange nectar glands at the base of the inner three, and a single slender pistil with a white stigma. Type B: each flower opens first as female in the afternoon (tepals spread, stigma white and receptive, stamens bent outward and closed), closes overnight, and reopens next morning as male (stamens erect round the pistil, anthers opening their little flaps, pollen yellow). Of a tree's hundreds of thousands of flowers only one in a thousand or fewer sets fruit; most of the young fruit drop.
Fruit: a pear-shaped drupe, 10.5–13 cm long and 6.5–7.7 cm across (typically 11.5 × 7 cm), 210–350 g (typically 280 g), with a distinct neck and a slightly slanted, lopsided broad end, on a stalk that thickens toward the fruit and joins it a little off its axis; the skin smooth, thin, peeling easily, medium green with a satin sheen and many small yellow dots, and it stays green when ripe. From set it takes 260–290 days to mature on the tree. Cut lengthwise: flesh pale buttery yellow, shading to bright green just under the skin, smooth and oily; one medium seed (about 16 % of the fruit) in a seed cavity, its brown papery coat clinging to the flesh. Young fruit just after set are pea-sized, dark green and glossy on the panicle.
Roots: shallow; a taproot from the seed, then most feeder roots in the top 50–60 cm, brown and thick, with few root hairs.
