Dioscorea rotundata Poir. 'Pona' — the white Guinea yam of Ghana and Nigeria, the prized early yam of the New Yam festivals. A twining herbaceous vine grown each year from a piece of its own tuber: planted as a sett (a cut piece of tuber, the "head" piece with its crown, about 200–500 g) 10–15 cm deep in the top of a heaped earth mound about 0.6 m high and 1.0–1.2 m across at its base (a rounded cone with a flattish top), beside one straight rough bush pole 3–5 cm thick standing about 2.2 m above the mound.

Sett: a chunk of tuber about 10–12 cm long and 7–9 cm across, rough brown corky skin with fine rootlet stubs, its cut faces dried white-cream with a thin pinkish-brown callus, ash-dusted.

Sprouting: after 5–7 weeks one shoot breaks from the sett's head end (near the old crown): a stiff leafless spear 5–8 mm thick at the soil, purple-green, with small brown-green triangular scale leaves (3–10 mm) at its first nodes and short hard hooked prickles; thin white roots come out from the base of the new shoot (not from the sett).

The vine: ONE main stem, round in section (not winged or square), 6–10 mm thick at the base, 3–5 mm at 2 m, green with a faint glaucous bloom, flushed purplish-brown near the foot and at the nodes; its lower 0.5 m bears short hard downward-hooked prickles 2–5 mm long, brown, about 5–15 per 10 cm at the foot thinning to none; above that smooth. It twines to the RIGHT (a right-handed Z helix: on the side of the stake facing the viewer each turn rises from lower left to upper right; anticlockwise seen from above), wrapping the stake in loose turns of about 10–15 cm pitch, internodes 8–15 cm; it reaches the top of the stake about 2 months after it comes up, its end then hanging over and down, and 4–10 side shoots from the upper leaf axils tangle over the stake's top.

Leaves: simple, on petioles 4–8 cm long (twisted at both ends, often with a pair of small prickles at the base); petiole 5–8 cm, pale green, purple at both ends, twisted; blade broadly heart-shaped (broadly ovate-cordate) 9–14 cm long and 6–10 cm wide, an open notch 1–2 cm deep at the base with rounded lobes, the tip drawn out into a narrow point 1–2 cm long, slightly down-turned; margin entire, smooth. 7 main veins from the base (the middle three running to the tip and meeting there, the outer ones curving into the margin), a fine net between. Upper side dark green with a satin sheen, waxy; underside paler greyish green, the veins raised and lighter. Alternate on the lowest metre of stem, opposite above. Young leaves lighter, glossier and bronze- to purple-tinged; the leaves yellow from the base of the vine up 6–7 months after emergence, then brown, curl and fall.

Flowers: 'Pona' does not flower (none drawn).

The tuber: one large tuber per plant, grown straight down from the base of the vine under the sett, starting at about 2–3 months; at harvest (7–8 months) cylindrical, the top narrowing to the head, the bottom bluntly rounded and slightly broader, about 32 cm long and 13 cm across, 2 kg; skin mid to dark brown, matt, finely rough and slightly scaly with fine shallow cracks and darker patches, scattered fine short wiry rootlets (1–5 cm, about 1 mm thick, dry brown), densest on the upper third; at the top the "head", a small hard knob 3–5 cm across where the vine and the fibrous roots join. Flesh white with a faint cream tint, firm and moist, a thin cream layer under the skin. The tuber hangs straight down from the head, its top 10–20 cm under the mound's surface, its tip 45–55 cm down. By age: 2 months 8 × 3 cm, pale cream-brown, smooth, carrot-like; 3 months 15 × 6 cm; 4 months 25 × 9 cm, browning; harvest 32 × 13 cm.

Roots: a mass of thin wiry unbranched fibrous roots 1–2 mm thick, white when young then cream to brown, radiating from the head just under the mound's surface, mostly in the top 30 cm, spreading 1 m and more sideways (cut off at the mound's edge on the plate).
