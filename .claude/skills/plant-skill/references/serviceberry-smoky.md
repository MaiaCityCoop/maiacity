# Amelanchier alnifolia (Nutt.) Nutt. ex M.Roem. 'Smoky': facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for a saskatoon (serviceberry) grown stage by stage, from the seed and the planted cutting to a
20-year-old unpruned clump, on the Canadian prairies (Peace River, Alberta, to Saskatoon, Saskatchewan). Built on the
template `datasheet.md` and set out like `plum-hauszwetschge.md`. Where pruning or the orchard changes a number, the
change has its own line.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured or described on 'Smoky' itself (also written 'Smokey'; tested as Selection no. 9 and B.E.F. 3502;
  GRIN PI 559414, CAME 30).
- **[SP]**: said of *Amelanchier alnifolia* in general (or of var. *alnifolia*, the prairie variety 'Smoky' belongs to),
  or of the genus where marked.
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with the reasoning given. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a snippet, or a page that cites the work, not the original.

Each number has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are millimetres for small organs,
metres for shrubs, grams, days and years. Shrub ages count from planting a one-year-old cutting or plug unless a row
says otherwise. "DAFB" means days after full bloom.

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** A wild saskatoon found on the Beaverlodge Research Station, Alberta (55° N, in the Smoky River drainage
  basin), transplanted in 1918 by W.D. Albright; tested as Selection no. 9 (1928) and B.E.F. 3502 (1935), re-selected
  1950 and introduced in **1952** by J.A. Wallace, Agriculture Canada, Beaverlodge (S1, S14). It is a selection from the
  wild, not bred (S1). "The cultivar that enabled commercial production to start in the prairies; in 1990 it occupied
  about 85 % of the hectarage in Canada" (S1). Still one of the top three cultivars on the prairies with Northline and
  Thiessen (S2).
- **Its marks.** A freely suckering, multi-stemmed shrub, "initially upright to arching-spreading", 4.5 m high and 6 m
  across at maturity, "crown expands indefinitely; crown long-lived, 70+ years" (S1, S14). Medium-sized round blue-black
  fruit with bloom, 7–11 a cluster, ripening relatively unevenly; the sweetest cultivar, with the highest sugar:acid
  ratio, mild, "fleshy", with relatively large and many seeds (S1, S2). Very productive; alternate bearing is
  "significant" in Smoky (S1). Its fruit-laden branches hang over (S1, harvest section).
- **"Large fruit"?** The manuals call it large ("up to 14 mm", "average 14 mm", S1, S2, S3), but in the only replicated
  trials it was **medium**: 11.2–12.8 mm, about 1 g, smaller than Thiessen, Martin and Northline (S4, S5, S7). Model it as
  medium.
- **Rules for the model.** One clone, own-rooted (cutting or tissue culture), grown either as an unpruned clump (the
  4.5 × 6 m end state) or as an orchard hedgerow kept to 2–2.5 m (S1, S2). Fruit is borne in terminal racemes on short
  shoots and on the tips of last year's long shoots (S1, S2).

## 3. Growth and form

### Height, spread and stems by age

Measured anchors: shrub height grows **23–25 cm a year** on average (S1); Smoky's current-season shoot was **42.9 cm**
in its 3rd season (1994; among the longest of 15 cultivars, S4 Table 1) and **13.3 cm** a year at maturity (8–12 y,
S5 [2nd]); Smoky and Pembina were "an ideal height, ranging from 1.5 to 2.5 m" in an orchard in 1997 (about the 7th
year, S1); unpruned Smoky reaches **4.5 m × 6 m** (S1, S14) or 4.5 × 4.5 m (S2); wild saskatoons "naturally mature
in 8 to 10 years to a height of 3 to 6 m", then stop making extension growth (S2).

- **Stems, the species.** Stems 1–50 a plant, solitary or colonial (species), 1–20, often colonial (var. *alnifolia*)
  [SP] S9; the genus 1–150 [SP] S9. Wild Idaho plants before fire averaged **3.78 m** high with a crown **1.25 m** across
  (S11 [SP]). A 9-year-old wild plant had a root crown **10.5 cm across and 20.7 cm long** (S11, Bradley [SP]).
- **Stem age.** In wild Montana 61 % of 470 plants were 6–20 years old (mean 17.9), the oldest **85** (S11 [SP]);
  "the average age apparently may be closer to 20 years" (S10). The largest fruit is borne on **2–4-year-old shoots** (S1).
- **Pruned orchard hedgerow (the commercial Smoky)** [SP] S1, S2: kept **2–2.5 m** high, narrow at the base (30–45 cm),
  vase-shaped; stems thicker than **2.5 cm at ground level** cut out every spring; from year 3–6, **¼ to ⅓** of the oldest
  stems removed each year, so every stem is renewed on a **3–4-year cycle**; a stem is "usually too high or the base
  circumference too large" by its 5th–6th year. Neglected orchards may last under 12 years (S1).
- **Seedlings, by contrast,** show visible height differences in a row (S2 fig. 7).

### Growth rhythm

- One main flush: shoot expansion begins in **late April or early May** and continues to the **end of May or early
  June**, sometimes into midsummer (S1) [SP].
- Flower buds for next year are initiated on shoots in the **last week of June to first week of July**; the cluster's
  pattern is laid down by mid-July (S1) [SP].
- After petal fall comes a "secondary bud break": vegetative shoots grow from the axillary buds at the base of the basal
  leaves of the fruiting inflorescence (S2) [SP].
- When fully mature, wild plants stop making new extension growth and fruit only from basal buds on the spur-like
  stubs of earlier inflorescences (S2) [SP].

### Branching and the year-by-year architecture

- **Long shoots and short shoots.** Twigs are long shoots or short shoots; flower clusters are usually, but not
  always, on short shoots; a drawn branch has one long shoot and 3 short shoots, the terminal short shoot carrying a
  fruit cluster (S1, fig. 2, after St-Pierre & Steeves 1990) [SP].
- **Mixed buds.** Fruit comes from mixed buds (a flower cluster plus two or three leaves at its base) on last season's
  wood extensions, and some from 2- and 3-year-old wood "on spur-like projections (not true spurs)" (S2) [SP]. New
  stems from the crown do not flower in their first year (S2).
- **Wild habit.** "Wild Saskatoon berry plants produce a strong central leader and narrow, weak secondary branching.
  Some varieties sucker profusely from lateral rhizomes while others produce few suckers and develop a more tree-like
  structure" (S2). Smoky is of the first kind (S1, S2, S3).
- **Smoky's form.** "Upright to spreading" (S1 table), "initially upright to arching-spreading" (S1, S14), "upright,
  spreading structure; suckers profusely" (S2), "upright and spreading" (S3); bush habit "similar to Northline" (S2).
- **For the growth driver** [EST]: stems rise from the crown at **5–20° from vertical**, the outer ones leaning out to
  **30–45°** and arching to near horizontal at the tips under crop after year 6; laterals at **40–60°** from their
  stem; short shoots 1–5 cm, each ending in a raceme; long shoots 10–45 cm a year (S1, S4, S5 measured lengths).

### Phyllotaxis

- Alternate, simple leaves (S1, S12) [SP]. The Maleae spiral is **2/5 (144°)** [EST, by analogy with apple]; use
  137.5–144° with ±5° jitter. Twig pith is **five-pointed** (S12), which fits a 2/5 spiral.

## Gaps

These rows have no published number for 'Smoky' (nor, sometimes, for the species). Each is marked [EST] above with how
it was estimated.

- **Height, spread, stem count and stem diameter by age.** Only the end state (4.5 m × 6 m, S1), the yearly height
  gain (23–25 cm, S1), shoot lengths (S4, S5), the 1.5–2.5 m orchard height at about 7 years (S1) and the pruning rule
  (stems over 2.5 cm cut out, a 3–4-year renewal cycle, S1) are published. The table in section 3 is fitted to these.
- **Leaf size of Smoky.** None measured; the var. *alnifolia* ranges (S9) are used. The tooth count per side (8–12)
  is chosen inside S18's 2–20 and S9's counts; colours as hex are estimates.
- **Flower diameter.** S2's "8 to 12 mm across" contradicts the petal lengths (9–14 mm, S9); the model takes ~25 mm from
  the petals.
- **Seed width and thickness, seedling and cotyledon sizes.** Only seed length (S19), mass (S7, S13) and epigeal
  germination (S13) are published.
- **Bark lenticels, lichens, one-year twig colour in winter.** No source describes lenticels; colours are from S1, S12.
- **Leaf-fall and autumn-colour dates on the prairies.** Only Montana dates (S11) were found.
- **Clusters per shoot, branch angles, sucker distance.** From the branch drawing (S1), form words and the hedge-closing
  time (S16); the "within 1.5 m" figure is from a snippet only.
- **Hallé–Oldeman model.** No source places *Amelanchier*; Scarrone's model is assigned by analogy with apple.
- **Fruit mass of Smoky** is read off a bar chart (S7 fig. 3, ≈0.97 g) and confirmed by S15 (0.9 g) and S22 [2nd].
- **Second-hand values ([2nd]).** S5 (12.1 mm, 4.58 kg, 13.3 cm shoots), S6 (pH 4.18, Brix range), S22 (Poland:
  95.4 g per 100 fruit, 17.39 °Brix), S23 (maturity classes) and the 1.5 m sucker distance were read only as abstracts
  or search snippets.
