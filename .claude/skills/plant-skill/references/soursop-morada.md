# Annona muricata L. 'Morada': facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for a grafted soursop (guanábana, graviola) grown stage by stage from seed (the rootstock) to ripe
fruit. Built on the template `datasheet.md` and set out like `sapodilla-alano.md`.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured or said of 'Morada' itself.
- **[SP]**: said of the species or the genus, or measured on another soursop type (named in the row: 'Elita', 'Lisa',
  'Blanca', 'FAO II', unnamed seedlings and so on).
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with how I made it. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a snippet, a summary page or a review that cites the work, not the original.

Each value has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are metres, kilograms, days and years.
Ages run from planting the tree out unless a row says otherwise. Fruit ages run from flower opening (anthesis) or from
fruit set, as each row says.

**'Morada' is measured better than most cultivars, but only in Brazil.** Five Embrapa trials and two university
studies weigh and measure its fruit, and one Embrapa trial measures its trees at 9 and 44 months (S2, S3, S4, S6, S7,
S10). All of them are Brazilian. I found **no Colombian publication that uses the name 'Morada'** (see section 0).
Its leaves, flowers and seeds are not described apart from the species anywhere I could read, so those rows carry [SP]
values from the floras (Flora of China, Flora of Pakistan), PROSEA, Morton and the ICUC monograph (S1).

---

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** 'Morada' is a soursop **type (ecotype)**, not a registered clone. Embrapa introduced it, with 'Lisa'
  and 'Blanca', **from Colombia into Brazil in 1980–1981** (Embrapa Cerrados, Brasília) (S1, S3, S5). The ICUC monograph
  calls it "an ecotype introduced into Brazil from Colombia" that "produces large fruits with an average weight of
  3.8 kg, but its fruits have very acid pulp" (S1). It belongs to "the acid group", "has shown high yield and good
  tolerance to trunk and fruit borers" (S1, S5, S30). 'Gigante de Alagoas' is a clone selected from a Morada seedling
  (S1). Most Brazilian Morada trees are still raised from seed; some orchards graft it (S3). [CV]
- **Correcting the brief.** The brief calls it "the Colombian soursop" and asks whether it is Colombia's main
  commercial cultivar. **I found no source for that.** Every source that names 'Morada' is Brazilian, and they say only
  that it came from Colombia (S1, S3, S4, S5). Colombian literature I could read names **'Elita'** (Valle del Cauca;
  S19) and the 2024 AGROSAVIA cultivars **'Agrosavia Tesoro'** and **'Agrosavia Fecunda'** (S28 [2nd]), and otherwise
  sorts Colombian guanábana as acid, semi-sweet or sweet types (S1). The name is not explained anywhere; *morada* means
  "purple" in Spanish and "dwelling" in Portuguese. **No purple trait is reported**, so do not colour anything purple.
  Treat 'Morada' as a **Colombian-origin, Brazilian-named type** and model it from the Brazilian measurements.
- **Its marks** (against 'Lisa', 'Blanca', 'Comum'/'Crioula' and 'FAO II').
  - **Big, broad, heart-shaped fruit**: 3.21 kg, 24.7 × 15.8 cm, length/diameter **1.56**, "cordiforme". The local
    'Comum' is longer and narrower (L/D 1.94) (S3). Another trial has Morada the longest and widest of three types
    (28.2 × 16.7 cm) (S10).
  - **Prominent spines** at harvest. 'Lisa' ("smooth") has only incipient ones (S3).
  - **Yellowish skin** colour: "frutos com coloração amarelada" (S4, S5, S30).
  - **Many seeds**: 259 a fruit against 90–162 in the other types (S2). Acid pulp (S1).
  - **Borer-tolerant and productive**: 18 fruits a tree at 3 years, the most of five types at Manaus (S2).
- **Rules for the model.**
  - The tree is **slender and erect**, branching low with near-vertical limbs (S11; "upturned limbs", S13), and is
    pruned to an **open vase ("taça") about 2 m tall** in Brazil (S4, S5) or a free canopy **kept at 2.5–3.5 m** in
    Colombia (Torres & Sánchez 1992 in S1).
  - Leaves are **two-ranked** on the branches (S27), glossy dark green, with a short stalk (S14).
  - **Flowers and fruit hang singly or in twos and threes from the trunk, old limbs and twigs** (S11, S13, S29).
  - Fruit of every age can hang on one tree at once: it flowers nearly all year in flushes (S1, S9, S12).
  - A **poorly pollinated fruit is lopsided or curved**, because unfertilised carpels stay small (S1, S11, S17).

- **Shape.** "Erect growth habit with a high canopy height-to-diameter ratio" (S1); "porte ereto e esguio, pouco
  encopada, com ramificações baixas quase verticais" (upright and slender, small-crowned, with low, almost vertical
  branches) (S11). In the measured 'Morada', height : crown width is **1.8 : 1 at 9 months** and **1.1 : 1 at 44
  months** (S6), so the crown fills out from an upright cone to a round head (as S15 describes).
- **Growth rate.** About **1 m of height a year** in the first 4 years and **~4.5 cm of trunk Ø a year** (S6, 9 to 44
  months: +2.5 m and +12.8 cm in 35 months) [EST from S6].

### Form, rhythm and roots

| Row | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Growth rhythm | **no distinct flushes**: extension "at any time of year, fairly steadily"; a dry season synchronises shoot growth and flowering, giving a harvest peak (S12). After the harvest, Brazilian trees renew much of their foliage over ~3 months (S9) | [SP] S9, S12 |
| Branching (wild form) | low-branching, bushy, "upturned limbs" (S13, S17); low branches almost vertical (S11); sylleptic laterals (S12). Branches ascending at first, spreading later (S15) | [SP] |
| Branching (pruned orchard tree) | **Embrapa**: top the stem at **~0.6 m**, keep **3–4 well spaced shoots in its last 20 cm**; cut each branch back to 50 cm whenever it passes 60 cm; build in 50 cm steps to **2.0 m** and hold there, with an **open centre ("taça", vase)** (S4, S5). **Colombia** (Torres & Sánchez 1992): either a central axis with horizontal laterals or forked branches, the first branches at **0.6–0.8 m**, **3–4 scaffolds at ~30° to the trunk**, height kept at **3.5 m**, branches above 2.5 m removed (S1). Model the 'Morada' orchard tree as **3–4 scaffolds from 0.6–0.8 m at 30–45° from vertical, each relaying Troll-fashion, arching outward with crop** | [SP] S1, S4, S5, S17; angle range [EST] |
| Phyllotaxis | Annonaceae leaves are "simple, alternate, usually distichous" (S27); "held in one plane in two rows" (Flora of Costa Rica in S27). | Internodes | none published. Use **2–4 cm** on leafy shoots, longer (4–8 cm) on vigorous water shoots | [EST] from the 8–16 cm leaf and the two-ranked spacing in photos |

At harvest **3–4 spines per 2.5 × 2.5 cm** (S22 [2nd]), i.e. ~13 mm apart, so a 3.2 kg fruit (surface ~1,090 cm²) carries **~550–700 spines** [EST]. They break off easily when ripe (S4, S5, S13) | [CV] S3; [SP]; count [EST] |
| Skin | thin, inedible (S11, S13). Skin is **7.9 %** of a Morada fruit's mass (S3), 10.3 % (S2, S7). From 7.9 % of 3.21 kg over ~1,090 cm², the skin with its spines averages **~2–3 mm** thick [EST] | [CV] S2, S3; thickness [EST] |
| Skin colour as seen | unripe **dark green**, glossy (S1, S12, S13) ≈ **#2f4a22**. At harvest **light, bright green** in 'Morada' (S3), "slightly yellowish-green" (S1, S13, S17); 'Morada' is called **"amarelada"** (yellowish) (S4, S5, S30). Measured harvest-stage skin: L* 42.5, b* 25.7, h 100.4 (RHS Yellow-Green 144A) (S22 [2nd]) ≈ **#6b6539**. | Flesh, colour as seen | **white**, "cottony-fibrous, juicy" (S1), "snow-white, fibrous, juicy segments" (S13), each segment wrapping one seed or none. **'Morada' is acid** (S1). Pulp is **83.6–83.7 %** of the fruit (S2, S3), central axis (core) 2.4–3.5 % (S3, S7). Hex ≈ **#f4f1e6**, the core cream ≈ #e9e0c4 | [CV] S1, S2, S3; [EST hex] |
| Sugar | **'Morada' 16.0 °Brix** (Manaus) (S2); 13.1 mean of three types in Bahia, acidity 0.94 g/100 g, pH 3.46 (S3) | [CV] S2; S3 |
| Growth by days | fruitlets start to swell **2–6 weeks after pollination** (S4, S5); before that the young fruit is the "escova" (brush) of styles (S9). | Days from flower to mature | **5–6 months after pollination** (S1, S4, S5); "around 6 months" (Embrapa Cerrados, S1). 'Elita' (Colombia, 1,070 m): physiological maturity **16 weeks after fruit set** (S19). Mexico ~114 days from set (BBCH study [2nd]); harvested 16–20 weeks after anthesis (Lem et al. [2nd]). Use **~150 days** from anthesis (115–180) | [SP]; [EST] for Morada |
| Harvest signs | dark green turns **light, bright green or yellowish-green**; the gloss goes; **spines spread apart**, soften and snap off easily; carpel units spread; the fruit looks "bloated" with smoother segment margins; slightly soft under the thumb (S1, S4, S5, S12, S17). Picked hard, it ripens in **4–6 days** (S1, S17) | [SP] |
| Drop | most flowers fall unset (S9, S20). Seed-borer and fruit-borer attack drops young fruit (S5). A ripe fruit left on the tree "falls" (S1) or is eaten by bats (S12) | [SP] |
| Stalk | the stout, woody pedicel (1.5–2 cm, S14b) thickens under the fruit. Use **2–4 cm long, 1.5–2.5 cm thick**, green-brown, the fruit hanging straight down | [SP] S14b; size [EST] for a 3 kg fruit |
| Fruits per tree | **'Morada' 18 ± 8** a tree in the 3rd year (Manaus) (S2); 16 a tree in the Cerrado (Pinto & Silva 1994 in S2). Species: 15–20 once stable in the 5th–6th year (S5); 12–24 (S12, S13). Keep **one fruit per branch** (S4, S5) | [CV] S2; [SP] |
| Yield | **'Morada'** at 204 trees/ha in central Brazil: **2, 4, 5, 7, 9 and 10 t/ha in years 2–7**, i.e. ~10, 20, 25, 34, 44 and **49 kg a tree**; "attains its mature yield of **50–60 kg** of fruit a plant in the seventh year" (S1, Table 12-1). ## Gaps

These rows have no published number for 'Morada', and most have none for the species either. Each is marked [EST] above.

- **'Morada' in Colombia.** I found no Colombian source that names 'Morada'. Its Colombian origin rests on the Brazilian
  statements that it was introduced from Colombia in 1980–1981 (S1, S3, S5). The brief's "Colombia's main commercial
  cultivar" is unsupported; I could not open Torres & Sánchez (1992, ICA), Escobar Torres (1992, ICA) or the 2024
  AGROSAVIA 'Tesoro'/'Fecunda' booklet, which are the places to check.
- **Height and trunk by age.** Measured on seed-raised, unpruned, irrigated 'Morada' at 9 and 44 months (S6) and on
  trunk Ø and crown at ~3–4 y (S2). Years 1, 2, 5 and 8 are interpolated or extrapolated; the nursery-tree trunk Ø is
  from the 1 cm stock at grafting (S4). S6 does not say at what height its girth was measured; I took it as near the base.
  The mature trunk Ø is chosen within S11's 15–30 cm. The Gurguéia trial (S25) gives 'Morada' a stem value of 32.06 cm
  (probably a girth, Ø ~10 cm) but neither the age nor which height bar is Morada's, so I did not use it.
- **Pruned orchard height and scaffold angle.** The 30° insertion is Torres & Sánchez's (S1); the 30–45° range is my
  allowance for crop weight.
- **Internodes.** Not published; set at 2–4 cm from the leaf size.
- **Seedling phyllotaxis, hypocotyl, cotyledon size and fall.** Not measured. S11 gives only the timing (emergence from
  day 10, leaves on day 23–30). The germination type conflicts: epigeal (S1, S11, first-hand) against hypogeal in a
  morphology paper I read only as an abstract (S21).
- **Young flush colour and leaf life.** Not described. The flush colour is a guess (pale yellow-green); the leaf life
  (~12 months) is from the yearly post-harvest leaf change (S9).
- **Stamen and carpel counts.** Not published. Carpels are estimated from the spine density at harvest (S22, read as 3–4
  per 2.5 × 2.5 cm) times the surface of a 3.2 kg ellipsoidal fruit; stamens from the receptacle.
- **Bud size.** From the flower length (S1, S14); only the 1 cm stage is measured (S20).
- **Bloom months in Colombia.** Harvest months (S1) minus ~5 months.
- **Fruit mass by days.** Only the double-sigmoid shape (S22, Franco-Mora [2nd]) and the total time (S1, S4, S5, S19)
  are published; the day-by-day masses are mine.
- **Morada flower-to-harvest days.** Not measured on 'Morada'; ~150 d from the species' 5–6 months and 'Elita''s 16
  weeks from set.
- **Skin thickness, spine count, fruit-stalk size.** Skin thickness from the skin share of mass (S3) and the ellipsoid
  surface; spine count from S22; stalk from the pedicel (S14b) scaled to a 3 kg fruit.
- **Colours as hex.** All are estimates from the descriptions, except the harvest-stage skin, converted from the CIELAB
  values of S22 (#6b6539). Mexican skin readings (S23: L* 30–46, C* 9–21, h 152–165, i.e. about #394b43 to #50755b)
  are bluer than any description, so I did not use them.
- **Productive life beyond 24 years.** Extrapolated.
- **Second-hand values ([2nd]).** S20's citation of Guzmán (1991) for natural fruit set; S21 (seedling type); S22
  (growth curve, harvest colour, spine density); Franco-Mora et al. (2001); the Mexican BBCH study (~114 d); Lem et
  al. (ISHS, 16–20 weeks); Cavalcante (2000, anther opening at 18:00); Janick & Paull (27–35 d bud); S27 (Annonaceae
  phyllotaxis); S28 (AGROSAVIA cultivars); the Colombian regions (press).
- **Not reachable.** Morton's original page at hort.purdue.edu returned 503 (I read Growables' reproduction, S13);
  CORE (core.ac.uk) blocked downloads, so the Lima-Brito et al. (2006) seedling paper stayed unread; Flora Neotropica has
  no *Annona* treatment I could open; no UF/IFAS soursop fact sheet by Crane & Balerdi turned up (UF/IFAS covers soursop
  only on a sweetsop/atemoya page).
