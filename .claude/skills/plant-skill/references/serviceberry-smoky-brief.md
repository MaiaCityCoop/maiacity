Amelanchier alnifolia (Nutt.) Nutt. ex M.Roem. 'Smoky' (Saskatoon berry, Saskatoon serviceberry, western serviceberry, juneberry) — the Saskatoon selected for fruit in Alberta and released in 1952, the most planted Saskatoon of the Canadian prairie orchards. A deciduous SHRUB of the rose family (Rosaceae, Maloideae): MANY upright stems from one crown at the ground and from suckers round it, never a tree on one trunk.

Seed and seedling: the seed is tiny, 4–5 mm long, 2–2.5 mm wide and 1.5 mm thick, a flattened ovoid with a curved back and a short beak, dark brown and leathery; it germinates after a winter's cold in the soil; germination is epigeal: two small oval seed leaves (5–7 × 3–4 mm) come up on a thin reddish hypocotyl 1.5–3 cm tall, then the first true leaves, small, round and toothed at the tip.

Habit and stems: a multi-stemmed suckering shrub, upright to arching-spreading and vase-shaped: in its eighth year about 2.5 m tall and 2.3 m wide (4–4.5 m and wider when old), 15–25 stems of every age rising from the crown at 5–20° from upright and from suckers that come up 20–80 cm round it, the old ones leaning outward and arching under fruit, the young ones straight up; each stem 1–4 cm thick at its foot, branching from about knee height into many twiggy side shoots, so the bush is dense and rounded at the top and open and leggy at the bottom. This year's shoots are slender, smooth, red-brown to purplish-brown, with narrow pointed buds (2–8 mm, reddish-brown, twisted, pressed against the twig; the flower buds plumper at the shoot tips); two- to five-year stems are smooth and grey-brown with small pale lenticels; old stems (6–12 years) are smooth light grey with darker vertical lines in a slightly twisted pattern, only the base of the oldest shallowly fissured and scaly; the oldest are cut out by the grower and renewed from suckers.

Leaf: alternate, simple, OVAL TO ALMOST ROUND, about 4 cm long and 3 cm wide (3.3–4.7 × 2.4–3.6 cm; smaller under the racemes, up to 6 cm on suckers), the tip rounded or squarely cut (truncate), the base rounded; the margin COARSELY TOOTHED ONLY ON THE UPPER HALF to two-thirds of the blade, the lower part smooth-edged; 8–13 pairs of straight, parallel side veins running from the midrib into the teeth; upper side dull blue-green to dark green, underside paler grey-green; the stalk 1.2–1.8 cm, slender. Young leaves come out folded along the midrib, pale green tinged bronze, finely woolly below; in autumn yellow, orange and orange-red.

Flower: in May, with the young leaves just unfolding, in short dense upright racemes 3–4 cm long at the ends of short shoots on last year's wood (two or three leaves at the base of each), 6–11 flowers each on slender stalks 0.8–1.6 cm; each flower about 2.5 cm across with FIVE NARROW STRAP-SHAPED WHITE PETALS (9–14 mm long, 4–5 mm wide) spread like a loose star, a green bell-shaped cup, five small triangular sepals (3 mm) bent back, about 20 stamens with pale yellow anthers, five styles joined at the base in the cup. Bloom is a white cloud over the whole bush.

Fruit: a small pome (a "berry", a tiny apple) about 12 mm across and 1 g, round, crowned at its tip by the five dry dark sepals round a small cup; in loose grape-like clusters of 7–11 along the raceme (3.5 cm in fruit), each on its own stalk of about 1 cm. By age: just set (3–4 mm, green, the petals gone, sepals still erect), green (8–10 mm, mid June), colouring (red to pinkish red, then red-purple; the berries of a cluster colour unevenly), ripe in mid July, about 56–63 days after bloom (deep purple-black to blue-black with a fine pale waxy bloom, soft and juicy). Cut across: deep purple-red flesh, about three small brown seeds in the core.

Roots: fibrous and spreading in the top 50 cm, with horizontal rhizomes (underground stems) running out from the crown and sending up suckers; a taproot only in the seedling.
