Amelanchier alnifolia (Nutt.) Nutt. ex M.Roem. 'Smoky' (Saskatoon berry, Saskatoon serviceberry, western serviceberry, juneberry) — the Saskatoon selected for fruit in Alberta and released in 1952, the most planted Saskatoon of the Canadian prairie orchards. A deciduous SHRUB of the rose family (Rosaceae, Maloideae): MANY upright stems from one crown at the ground and from suckers round it, never a tree on one trunk.

Seed and seedling: the seed is tiny, 4–5 mm long and 2–3 mm wide, flattened, curved like a comma, dark brown and smooth; it germinates after a winter's cold in the soil; germination is epigeal: two small oval seed leaves (5–8 mm) come up on a thin reddish hypocotyl, then the first true leaves, small, round and toothed at the tip.

Habit and stems: a multi-stemmed suckering shrub 3–4 m tall and 2.5–3.5 m wide when grown (eighth year on), upright and vase-shaped: 15–30 stems of every age rise from the crown and from suckers that come up 10–40 cm round it, the old ones leaning outward, the young ones straight up; each stem 2–5 cm thick at its foot, branching from about knee height into many twiggy side shoots, so the bush is dense and rounded at the top and open and leggy at the bottom. This year's shoots are slender, smooth, red-brown to purplish-brown, with long narrow pointed buds (6–10 mm, reddish-brown, the scales hairy at their tips); two- to five-year stems are smooth and grey-brown with small pale lenticels; old stems (6–12 years) are smooth to finely streaked silver-grey, never deeply fissured; the oldest are cut out by the grower and renewed from suckers.

Leaf: alternate, simple, OVAL TO ALMOST ROUND, 2.5–5 cm long and 2–4 cm wide, the tip rounded or squarely cut (truncate), the base rounded; the margin COARSELY TOOTHED ONLY ON THE UPPER HALF to two-thirds of the blade, the lower part smooth-edged; 7–11 pairs of straight, parallel side veins running from the midrib into the teeth; upper side dull blue-green to dark green, underside paler grey-green; the stalk 1–2 cm, slender. Young leaves come out folded along the midrib, pale green tinged bronze, finely woolly below; in autumn yellow, orange and orange-red.

Flower: in May, with the young leaves just unfolding, in short upright to spreading racemes 3–5 cm long at the ends of last year's shoots and spurs, 6–15 flowers each on slender stalks 0.5–2 cm; each flower 2.5–3.5 cm across with FIVE NARROW STRAP-SHAPED WHITE PETALS (10–15 mm long, 3–5 mm wide) spread like a loose star, five small triangular green-brown sepals bent back, about 20 stamens with pale yellow anthers, five styles joined at the base in the cup. Bloom is a white cloud over the whole bush.

Fruit: a small pome (a "berry") 12–15 mm across ('Smoky' is the large one; about 1–1.3 g), nearly round, a little broader than long, crowned at its tip by the five dry dark sepals and a small cup; in grape-like clusters of 6–12 along the raceme, each on its own stalk. By age: just set (3–4 mm, green, the petals gone, sepals still erect), green (8–10 mm, mid June), colouring (red to pinkish red, then red-purple; the berries of a cluster colour unevenly), ripe in mid July, about 50–60 days after bloom (deep purple-black to blue-black with a fine pale waxy bloom, soft and juicy). Cut across: deep purple-red flesh, a few small brown seeds in the core (5–10 chambers, 2–5 seeds developed).

Roots: fibrous and spreading in the top 50 cm, with horizontal rhizomes (underground stems) running out from the crown and sending up suckers; a taproot only in the seedling.
