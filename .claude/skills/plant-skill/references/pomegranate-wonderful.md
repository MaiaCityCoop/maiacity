# Punica granatum L. 'Wonderful': facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for a pomegranate grown stage by stage, from the seed to a 15-year-old orchard tree in full crop
and on to an old shrub, as it stands in a San Joaquin Valley (California) orchard. Built on the template `datasheet.md`
and set out like `plum-hauszwetschge.md`. Where the training (one trunk, three trunks or a free bush) changes a number,
the change has its own line.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured on 'Wonderful' itself, in California, Israel, Italy, Iran, Egypt or Greece. Its sports ('Red Jay',
  'Early Wonderful') are not [CV]; where a sport's patent is used because it is "similar to 'Wonderful'", the row says
  so and the tag is [EST].
- **[SP]**: said of *Punica granatum* in general, or measured on another cultivar.
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with the reasoning given. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a snippet, a talk's slides or a page that cites the work, not the original.

Each number has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are millimetres for small organs,
centimetres for leaves and trunks, metres for trees, grams, days and years. Tree ages count from planting a rooted
hardwood cutting in the orchard unless a row says otherwise. "DAFB" means days after full bloom; "DAF" days after
flowering.

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** The pomegranate of California and of most export orchards: a vigorous, thorny, own-rooted shrub-tree
  with large, deep red, slightly flattened fruit, a long crown, a thin-to-medium rind, dark red tart-sweet arils and
  medium-hard seeds. Said to have been found **about 1896 in Porterville, California, among cuttings received from
  Florida** (UC fact sheet, S37 [2nd]); UGA calls the origin disputed (one story: found in Florida and brought west;
  the other: found in a shipment of cuttings or seedlings in California, S13). UF says it "originated in Florida"
  (S12). It accounts for about **90–95 %** of US production (S38 [2nd]) and is the main cultivar of Israel's ~3,000 ha
  (S27 [2nd]).
- **Genetically one plant.** Every 'Wonderful' is a clone from cuttings: germplasm studies find "pronounced genetic
  uniformity consistent with their widespread clonal propagation in global commercial orchards" (S32). Its genome
  (accession PG-100-1, Newe Ya'ar) is ~351 Mb in 8 pseudochromosomes, 2n = 16 (S31). So one model is true of it.
- **Its marks.** UPOV uses 'Wonderful' as the example variety for: medium vigour, long and narrow leaf blades with a
  moderately acute apex and a medium petiole, a broad calyx of orange-red colour, a medium orange corolla, a long and
  broad fruit with a **long crown**, round cross-section, thin skin, medium acidity, low juiciness, medium aril width,
  **dark red arils**, medium seed width and **medium seed hardness**, and medium flowering and ripening time (S4).
- **Rules for the model.** One clone. Fruit from hermaphrodite flowers at the tips of short new shoots borne on spurs and
  one-year wood, mostly on the outer shell of the crown. Most flowers are male and fall. The tree is trained in
  California as a short single trunk (30–45 cm) with 4–6 scaffolds in a vase, or as 3–6 trunks from the ground; left
  alone it is a suckering bush.

## 1. Seed and germination

What is eaten as an "aril" is the **seed with its juicy outer seed coat (sarcotesta)**; the hard inner part is the
sclerotised mesotesta and the embryo. Pomegranate is botanically exarillate (S29). Below, "aril" means the whole juicy
seed as sold, "seed" the hard inner kernel.

## 2. How it is propagated and planted

| Way | What it is | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Hardwood cuttings (the commercial way)** | One-year wood taken in late fall or winter: **~25 cm (10 in)** long, **6–13 mm (¼–½ in)** thick, "about the same width as a pencil" (UGA); 25–50 cm (Morton); 15–20 cm (UF). Stuck vertically with 2–3 top nodes (5–8 cm) above ground; 30 cm apart in a nursery row; grown one season (1–2 in Florida) | [SP] S13, S14, S12 |
| **Own-rooted** | **Yes. 'Wonderful' is grown on its own roots; no rootstocks are used or needed in California** (Morton 1987, cited by UC). Grafting is rarely done | [SP] S38 [2nd], S14 |
| Planting stock | rooted cuttings set out bare-root in late winter or early spring (UGA; UC cost study: "bareroot trees") | [SP] S13, S24 [2nd] |
| Heading | cut back to **60–75 cm** at planting; 3–5 scaffolds chosen, the lowest **20–25 cm** above ground (UF); UC Master Gardeners: when ~60 cm, cut to 30 cm and keep 4–5 shoots | [SP] S12, S34 |
| Training (California) | **single trunk**: a short **30–45 cm (12–18 in)** trunk with **5–6 main branches** forming a **vase**, "popular in California"; or **multi-trunk**: **3–6** strong stems straight from the ground; or a compromise **triple trunk** (UGA). "Typically free-form multiple trunks, although single trunk and trellised production is being used" (UC) | [SP] S13; S38 [2nd] |
| Suckers | "Trees tend toward a bushy habit with many suckers arising from the root and crown area" (UF); removed in summer each year in orchards. The 'Red Jay' sport of 'Wonderful' "produces suckers", "similar" to 'Wonderful' | [SP] S12, S13; S18 |
| Other ways | air layering and transplanted suckers work (Morton) | [SP] S14 |

- **Vigour.** UPOV: 'Wonderful' is the example of **medium** vigour (S4); Ferrara et al. call it a "vigorous cultivar" whose
  vegetative buds give "primary shoots often with abundant secondary shoots having a terminal thorn" (S22); Morton:
  "vigorous and productive" (S14). 'Red Jay' is "slightly less vigorous" than 'Wonderful' (S18). [CV]
- **Maturity.** Trees "reach maturity in five to seven years" (UGA, S13). [SP]

### Growth rhythm

- Bud break in **March** (San Joaquin Valley; "dormancy in December, new spring foliage in March" in north Florida)
  [EST from S33 [2nd]]. A strong spring flush with the flowers on its tips, then more flushes into summer; pomegranates
  "can have shoot flushes that bear flowers throughout the warmer parts of the year" (S13). [SP]
- Extension shoots on young vigorous trees **50–120 cm** a year; on cropping trees 10–40 cm; spurs grow under 1 cm a
  year [EST]. Shoot classes from mixed buds: spurs **< 1 cm**, short **2–5 cm**, medium **5–10 cm**, long **> 10 cm**
  (S22). [SP, measured on four cultivars incl. 'Wonderful']

### Branching, thorns and spurs

- **Buds.** Small (0.1–0.3 cm), brownish-green; typically **two buds per node at 90°** to the pair below, sometimes one,
  sometimes three (S22). [SP incl. 'Wonderful']
- **Thorns.** Young branches are 4-angled and "often terminating as indurate spines" (FOC, S15). In 'Wonderful' many
  secondary shoots end in a thorn (S22). Thorn lengths in related cultivars: 3–5 mm (EMEK), 3–8 mm (KAMEL) (S19);
  ~3 cm ('Red Jay', sport of 'Wonderful', S18); from 3 mm to 9 cm, in opposite pairs about 5 cm apart on second-year wood
  ('Smith', S20). For 'Wonderful' use **1–4 cm, stiff, straight, red-brown to grey**, at the tips of short side shoots
  and in pairs at nodes [EST]. The Cal Poly thesis on 'Wonderful' is titled "Fruit, Flower, and Thorn" (S10).
- **Spurs and fruit wood.** "Short fruiting spurs appear primarily on two- or three-year-old wood, and are found growing
  mostly on the outer perimeter of the canopy" (UGA, S13). On mature 'Wonderful' (S10 [2nd]): one-year wood bore **70 %**
  of the flowers (5 % hermaphrodite) and **57 %** of marketable fruit; two-year wood 19 % (10 % hermaphrodite); older
  wood 10 % (20 % hermaphrodite). "Flowering rarely occurred in the interior of the canopy". [CV]
- **Crop load.** "Fruit bearing branches will bend or break unless fruit thinning is practiced" (UF); in California fruit
  is not thinned (S9), so the outer limbs hang down with fruit near the ground and are propped or wired. [SP; CV]
- **Year by year (for the growth driver)** [EST from the above]:
  - **Year 0–1**: a whip headed at 60–75 cm; 3–6 scaffold shoots in decussate pairs, 40–60° from vertical; suckers at the
    base.
  - **Year 1–3**: scaffolds lengthen 0.5–1.2 m a year; many short side shoots, the weaker ending in thorns; first spurs;
    crown upright-fountain.
  - **Year 3–6**: dense twiggy shell; spurs on 2–3-year wood; fruit at the tips of short shoots on the outer metre;
    limbs begin to arch under crop.
  - **Year 6–15**: full crop; outer limbs arch to horizontal or below, renewed by upright shoots from their bends; the
    interior bare and shaded; trunk thickens and twists.
  - **Old (25 y +)**: trunks fluted and twisted, hollowing; suckers take over if not removed.

### Phyllotaxis

- **Opposite and decussate** on growing shoots (UF: "opposed and pairs alternately crossing at right angles"), sometimes
  in **whorls of 3**; **clustered** on spurs and short shoots, "in whorls of 5 or 6" (Morton) because the internodes of a
  spur are nil (S12, S14, S22). UPOV records the predominant number of leaves per node on young shoots as two, three
  or more than three (S4). Model: decussate pairs on long shoots, ±10° jitter; rosettes of **4–8 leaves** on spurs.
  [SP; counts EST]

## For the brief

- A **shrubby small tree, 3.5–4.5 m tall and as wide**: a short, twisted, grey, flaking trunk (30–45 cm to the first
  limbs) or **3–6 stems from the ground**, opening into a **vase/fountain** of arching limbs; never a lollipop standard.
- Twigs **fine, dense and thorny**: many short side shoots end in a **stiff 1–4 cm spine**; young shoots are **square in
  section and red-tinged**; suckers rise around the base.
- Leaves **small, narrow and glossy**: lanceolate, about **6 × 1.7 cm** on long shoots, smaller on spurs, **opposite in
  crossed pairs** and **clustered in rosettes of 4–8** on spurs; dark shiny green above, paler below, on 4–6 mm reddish
  stalks. Spring flush **bronze-red**, autumn **butter-yellow**.
- Flowers at the **tips of short shoots**, singly or 2–5 together: a **thick, waxy, orange-red calyx tube** with **6 (5–7)
  pointed lobes**, and from its mouth **6 (5–7) crumpled, tissue-thin, vivid scarlet-orange petals** (flower 4–5 cm
  across), filled by a **brush of ~170 red filaments with yellow anthers**.
- **Two flower shapes on the same twig**: fruit flowers with a **fat urn-shaped base** (the ovary, ~1.5 cm wide), and the
  more numerous male flowers with a **slim tapering base**, many lying fallen under the tree.
- Fruit **round, slightly flattened**, about **9.5–11 cm wide and 8.5–9 cm high**, 300–550 g, sitting almost stalkless at
  a shoot tip, often weighing the branch down.
- The **crown is big**: a **2–2.5 cm tall, broad calyx** with **6 triangular, pointed lobes**, red like the rind, the tips
  dried brown, a brown brush of old stamens inside.
- Rind **leathery, deep crimson-red all over when ripe** (≈ #9c1626), slightly glossy, the shaded side a little browner,
  with fine brown dots, wind-scar russet and on the sun side sometimes a bleached sunburn patch; green → red-blushed
  green (colour break ~11 weeks after petals fall) → deep red by ~22 weeks.
- Cut open: a **3–4 mm red skin over a cream-yellow spongy layer**, **cream papery septa** dividing the inside into
  irregular chambers in **two layers**, packed with **~500 (200–1,000) juicy arils**.
- Arils **angular, ~11 × 8 mm, dark ruby-red and translucent like glass**, each with **one cream, hard seed (~8 × 3 mm)**
  showing faintly inside.
- On the tree in October a few fruit are **split open**, a jagged crack showing the arils; birds peck them.
- The orchard: hedgerows of dense vases at about **5.5 × 3.7–5.5 m**, the bare floor between, fruit hanging in the outer
  metre of the crown, none inside.

## Visual pitfalls

What image models (and people) usually get wrong about pomegranates, and what is true of 'Wonderful':

- **Too many petals, or rose-like double flowers.** 'Wonderful' has **5–7 single petals** (5.7 on average); the double
  "ornamental" forms have ~50 (S22). Petals are crumpled tissue, not smooth or cupped.
- **Petals the wrong colour.** It is a vivid **orange-red/scarlet**, not pink, magenta or deep crimson; the calyx is a
  more orange, waxy red.
- **No fleshy calyx.** The flower is not a flat five-petalled star: it is a **thick red tube with pointed lobes** that the
  petals spill out of, and the same tube becomes the fruit's crown.
- **The crown too small or missing.** On 'Wonderful' it is **long and broad** (UPOV), about a quarter of the fruit's
  height, with 6 pointed lobes; models often draw a tiny nub or a smooth dimple.
- **A lollipop standard tree.** It is a **multi-stemmed shrub or a short-trunked vase**, wider than a ball-headed tree,
  twiggy and thorny, with suckers at the base; not a tall clean trunk under a round crown.
- **Big apple- or citrus-like leaves.** The leaves are **small (≈ 6 × 1.7 cm), narrow, glossy and opposite/clustered**;
  broad or serrated leaves are wrong. No thorns is also wrong.
- **Fruit hanging on long stalks like apples.** It sits **almost stalkless at a shoot tip**, the bent shoot does the
  hanging.
- **Fruit drawn as a perfect sphere or a pear.** It is **round but slightly flattened** (height/width ≈ 0.9), with
  shallow irregular bumps over the chambers, and the crown on top (pointing away from the stalk).
- **"Arils as seeds" (and seeds as round berries).** The juicy part is the seed's own coat; each aril is **angular,
  faceted, translucent dark red with a pale hard seed inside**, packed tight in chambers, not loose round red beads, not
  a solid red interior.
- **No septa and no white pith.** Inside, **cream papery membranes and a pale spongy layer** frame the arils; models often
  fill the fruit uniformly red to the skin.
- **Too few arils.** A 'Wonderful' holds **~500 (to ~1,000)**, not a few dozen.
- **Rind too thick or too thin.** About **3–4 mm red skin plus the pale layer**, a centimetre in all, not a paper skin and
  not a melon rind.
- **Uniform colours.** Ripe fruit are deep red, but young fruit are green, then green with a red blush; a tree in
  September shows a mix. Split fruit, sunburn patches and russet are normal.
- **Fruit and flowers on the wrong wood.** Flowers and fruit are at the **tips of short new shoots on the outside of the
  crown**, not along bare old branches (no cauliflory) and not in the shaded interior.
- **Evergreen in winter.** 'Wonderful' is **deciduous** in California: bare and twiggy from December to March, yellow in
  November.
