# Prunus domestica L. subsp. domestica 'Hauszwetschge': facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for a plum tree grown stage by stage, from the stone to a 30-year-old standard tree, in a
middle-European orchard meadow (Streuobstwiese). Built on the template `datasheet.md` and set out like
`banana-dwarf-cavendish.md`. Where the rootstock or the way it is grown changes a number, the change has its own line.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured on 'Hauszwetschge' itself, under any of its names: Hauszwetsche, Hauszwetschke (AT), Deutsche
  Hauszwetsche, Bauernpflaume, Hauspflaume, Basler Zwetsche, Quetsche, **Požegača / Pozegaca** (Serbia, Bosnia; the
  Bundessortenamt lists it as a synonym, S3), **Domácí švestka / "Domestic Prune"** (Czech trials, S6, S7), or one of its
  selected clones ('Schüfer', 'Meschenmoser', 'Wolff', 'Etscheid', 'Gunser', 'Schraderhof 100', 'zum Felde').
- **[SP]**: said of *Prunus domestica* in general, of European plums, or of the genus.
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with the reasoning given. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a snippet, or a page that cites the work, not the original.

Each number has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are millimetres for small organs,
centimetres for leaves and trunks, metres for trees, grams, days and years. Tree ages count from planting a one-year
grafted tree unless a row says otherwise. "DAFB" means days after the start of full bloom.

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** A very old, late, self-fertile blue prune with relatively small fruit. It is grown in Germany since
  the Middle Ages (S3) and spread more widely from the 17th century; stones like its own were found in Roman wells at
  Aalen from the 2nd–3rd century AD (S2). It is "a population presenting itself in many forms", with better and worse
  types in yield, fruit size and taste (S1). Hartmann watched more than 170 types in 1975–1980: fruit weight ran from
  **11 to 32 g** and ripening spread over about **3 weeks** (S4 [2nd]). The clones sold today come from that selection
  (S5 [2nd]).
- **Its marks.** An upright tree with steep leaders and a dense crown, whose fine fruit wood hangs under the crop (S1,
  S3). Small to medium oval, dark blue fruit under a heavy pale blue bloom, the suture hardly visible, firm yellow-green
  freestone flesh, high sugar and acid (S1, S3, S12). Late flowering (S3, S7).
- **Why it is hard to grow now.** It is very susceptible to sharka (plum pox virus): fruit "very strongly susceptible
  (strong to very strong symptoms); strong leaf symptoms" (S3); **62 %** of its fruits showed typical PPV symptoms in a
  Czech trial (S9 [2nd]).
- **Rules for the model.** Model one clone-like tree (the variation inside the population is real, but keep it to fruit
  size ±25 % and ripening ±10 days between trees). Fruit is borne on spurs and on the base of one-year shoots. The tree
  is a standard (Hochstamm) on a seedling rootstock unless a world asks for an orchard spindle.

- **Rootstock effect on the final size.** St. Julien A: about **3–4.5 m** high and wide; Wangenheim: about **2.5–3.5 m**
  (nursery guidance, S31 [2nd]). Myrobalan seedling gives the full 6–8 m standard. Own-rooted trees on suckers are less
  vigorous than on Myrobalan (S19 [2nd]).
- **Vigour of the cultivar.** "strong-growing (depending on site), upright, dense crown" (S3); "medium-strong to strong,
  crown typically tall-going, leaders steeply erect" (S1); "very vigorous, growth type 1" (S12); "medium strong to
  strong, at mature stage widely spreading" (S11); "very strong growth" (S23). [CV]

### Growth rhythm

- One main flush a year: shoots grow from bud break (early–mid April) to about **late June–July**, then set a terminal
  bud; a short second flush in wet summers on young trees. [EST, typical of temperate *Prunus*]
- One-year extension shoots: **40–80 cm** on young vigorous trees, **10–30 cm** on old cropping trees. [EST]

### Branching and the year-by-year architecture

- **Form.** Crown "typically tall-going, the leaders steeply erect; through the often heavy crop the fine fruit wood
  mostly hangs" (S1). Upright with a dense crown (S3). Mature trees "widely spreading" (S11). Round, slightly irregular,
  semi-open (S20). [CV]
- **Measured branch traits** on 13-year spindles [CV] S7 (scores 1–9):
  - canopy density **3.0** (1 = very dense, 9 = very thin): among the two densest of nine cultivars;
  - spurring **7.0** (9 = abundant spurs): spurs well;
  - branch angle from the vertical at the most bent-down point **70–83°** (69.9° on Wangenheim, 83° on Myrobalan);
  - bare branch area **7.0–7.4** (9 = no bare area): little bare wood.
- **Year by year (for the growth driver)** [EST, from the above and standard plum training]:
  - **Year 0–1**: a leader and 3–4 scaffold limbs at **30–45° from the vertical**, spaced around the stem on the 2/5
    spiral, each 15–25 cm apart in height.
  - **Year 2–4**: leader and scaffolds lengthen 40–80 cm a year; one-year side shoots at **45–60°** from their parent;
    many short shoots (1–5 cm) form on two-year wood.
  - **Year 4–8**: the short shoots become **spurs ("Fruchtspieße")**: short, stiff, 2–10 cm, with crowded leaf scars and
    clusters of flower buds, living **3–5 years** [EST]. Flowers also on the basal buds of one-year shoots. Crop weight
    starts to bend the outer limbs.
  - **Year 8 on**: fine fruit wood hangs at 90–120° from the vertical under crop (S1 "hangend"); new upright water shoots
    from the bent limbs replace them; the crown broadens to 4–6 m.
  - **Old tree**: inner wood bare, crop in the outer shell; dead twigs inside; some limbs break under heavy crops
    (alternate bearing, S3, S11).
- **Thorns.** Seedling and sucker trees and young vigorous shoots may carry short thorny spur shoots; Hartmann counted
  thorns on Hauszwetsche shoots as a sign of juvenility (S5 [2nd]). Grafted clones: few or none. [EST]

### Phyllotaxis

- Alternate, spiral **2/5 (144°)**: measured at about **143°** in sweet cherry (MRI of a trunk, S18 [2nd]). Use 137.5–144°
  with ±5° jitter. [SP genus]

## Gaps

These rows have no published number for 'Hauszwetschge' (nor, sometimes, for the species). Each is marked [EST] above
with how it was estimated.

- **Height, trunk diameter and crown width of a standard tree at 1–15 years.** Only the mature size (6–8 m × 4–6 m,
  S20) and trunk areas of pruned orchard spindles (S6, S7, S10) were found. I scaled the spindle diameters by about 1.3×
  for an unpruned standard and fitted heights between the young nursery tree and the mature size. The 30-year trunk
  diameter of 25–35 cm is an estimate with no measured anchor.
- **Leaf blade length and width.** Only the area (17–20 cm², S8) and the shape words (S1) are measured on the cultivar.
  I derived 7.0 × 3.9 cm with L/W 1.8 and an area factor of 0.68.
- **Leaf colours as hex, autumn colour, bud break and leaf-fall dates.** No measured values; from the species
  descriptions and the flowering dates.
- **Flower diameter and stamen count.** Species values only (1–1.5 cm in S14; petals 7–14 mm). The stamen count of 20–30
  is the usual *Prunus* number, not a count.
- **Fruit growth curve stages (days).** Only the bloom-to-harvest span is measured on the cultivar (134–135 d, S7). The
  stage boundaries are scaled from prune 'd'Agen' (S24 [2nd]).
- **Fruit colour timing and hex values.** The colour sequence is published (S1); the days and hex codes are estimates.
- **Kernel size and stone colour.** Derived from the stone size (S1).
- **Germination type (hypogeal), seedling height, first leaves and when the cotyledons rot.** Genus-level only (S16);
  no *P. domestica* seedling description found.
- **Hallé–Oldeman model.** Rauh's model is assigned to cherry, peach and almond (S17); its use for plum is an estimate.
- **Spur life, shoot lengths, branch angles of an unpruned standard.** Angles were measured only on trained spindles
  (S7); the rest are estimates.
- **Bark of young and old trees, lichens, trunk form.** No measured or published description of the cultivar's bark; the
  usual look of old *Prunus domestica* in orchard meadows.
- **Root spread and sucker numbers of a standard.** Root depth and density measured on another cultivar (S25).
- **Yield of a full-grown standard (60–100 kg).** Only the 50-year Bavarian average (40 kg, S26 [2nd]) and orchard
  spindle yields are published.
- **Productive life.** Only garden pages, which disagree (30–50 to over 100 years).
- **Second-hand values ([2nd]).** S4, S5 (Hartmann's clone selection), S9 (Czech PPV and fruit weight), S12 (20.4 °Brix,
  citing Balmer 2011), S18 (2/5 phyllotaxis in cherry), S19 (propagation of Požegača on its own suckers), S24 (prune
  growth curve), S26 (40 kg average), S27 and S31 (crown and stem sizes from a parish sheet and a nursery), S28 (frost),
  S29 (seedlings of Hauszwetsche) and S30 (Bosnian Požegača types) were read only as abstracts or snippets.
