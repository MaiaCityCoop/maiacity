Manihot esculenta Crantz 'TME 419' (TMEB419, NICASS 20; IITA, Nigeria) — the leading improved cassava of Africa, a "sweet" white-fleshed clone for gari and fufu, resistant to cassava mosaic disease. A woody shrub of the spurge family grown for one season from a stem cutting; its crop is the cluster of brown-skinned, white-fleshed storage roots at the cutting's base. It is a TALL, ERECT, STRAIGHT CANE that hardly branches (it flowers late and little; cassava forks only where it flowers): most plants are one or two straight unbranched canes with a crown of leaves on top, a few fork once, late and high, into three.

The cutting: a 25 cm piece of mature stem, 2.5 cm thick, light brown, with 6 nodes (internodes about 4 cm), each node a raised crescent-shaped leaf scar 5–8 mm wide with a small bud just above it; both ends cut clean, the cut face showing white pith inside a thin green cortex under the brown skin. It is pushed into a soil ridge at about 45° (or upright), two thirds of it buried, buds pointing up.
Sprouting: at 1–2 weeks two or three buds above the soil break into short green shoots with tiny folded pale leaves; white adventitious roots, thin as string (1–2 mm), come out of the buried cut end and the buried nodes.
The stem (cane): straight, round, 0.7 cm thick at one month, 2 cm at six months, 2.5–3 cm at its foot at twelve; the top 30 cm under the crown green and soft, then grey-green, then light brown, slightly corky and dull; the bare lower cane densely knobbly with prominent pale-brown leaf scars spiralled up it (2/5 phyllotaxis), a scar every 2–4 cm; the base curving a little where the cutting lay slanted. Leaves only in the top 0.5–0.8 m of each cane: the lower leaves yellow and fall after 2–4 months, so the bare scarred cane is most of the plant's height.
The leaf: palmate, divided almost to the petiole into 7 lanceolate lobes on the big mid-season leaves (5 on the upper ones, 3 on the first small leaves and near a flower); the central lobe 15–18 cm long and 3.5–4.5 cm wide, pointed, the side lobes shorter outward, the outermost pair a third of the central one's length, the lobes spreading like fingers of a hand; upper side smooth, dark green, a little glossy, a pale midrib down each lobe; underside paler grey-green and glaucous, midribs raised and cream-green. Petiole long, 18–25 cm (longer than the lobes), slender, GREEN (not red), standing up at 30–45° on young leaves, horizontal then drooping on old ones. Unexpanded leaves at the shoot tip pale yellowish-green, folded; the first expanded leaf bright green; old leaves turn yellow before they drop. No hairs.
Flower (rare on TME 419): at a fork, a small terminal panicle 6–10 cm; a few female flowers at its base (1 cm, five yellowish-green tepals streaked purple-red, a 6-ridged green ovary, white stigmas), many smaller male flowers above (bell-shaped, pale yellow with purplish streaks, orange disc and stamens).
Fruit (rare): a green capsule 1.5 × 1.3 cm with 6 narrow wings running pole to pole, drying brown and splitting in 6; three seeds, 8–10 mm, glossy, mottled grey with dark brown speckles, a pale caruncle at one end.
Storage roots (the crop): 8–10 per plant at twelve months, radiating outward and downward (20–45° below horizontal) from the base of the buried cutting like the spokes of a wheel, in a cluster about 0.8 m across lying in the top 35 cm of the ridge; each root 40–48 cm long and 5–7 cm thick, conical to conical-cylindrical (fullest a third of the way from the neck, then tapering into a long thin tail that ends in a fibrous root), joined to the cutting by a SHORT neck of 1–3 cm, about 0.5 kg each, 4.9 kg a plant. By age: thin, stiff, slightly darker fibrous roots at 5 weeks; pencil-thick (1–2 cm, 20 cm long) at 2 months; 3 cm at 3 months; 4.5 cm at 4 months; 5 cm at 6 months; 6 cm at 12 months. Skin: thin, rough, corky, BROWN, flaking off in small patches. Cut across: a thin brown skin, then a 2–3 mm cream to light-yellow peel (cortex) that comes off as a ring, then pure white starchy flesh with faint radiating lines and a thin fibrous core at the centre. Fine fibrous roots everywhere, pale cream-brown.
