# Moringa oleifera Lam. 'PKM-1' (Periyakulam 1): facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for an annual (seed-raised) drumstick moringa grown stage by stage from seed to dry, split pod.
Built on the template `datasheet.md` and set out like `sapodilla-alano.md`.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured on 'PKM-1' itself, or said of it by TNAU, which bred it.
- **[SP]**: said of the species, or measured on another named cultivar (named in the row: 'PKM-2', 'Dwarf moringa',
  Jaffna and so on).
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with how I made it. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a snippet, a summary page or a review that cites the work, not the original.

Each value has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are metres, kilograms, days and years.
**Ages run from sowing** unless a row says otherwise. Many trials count "days after transplanting" (DAT) or "after
planting" (DAP); the row then says so, and the Timeline turns them into days from sowing. Pod ages run from flower
opening (DAA, days after anthesis).

**PKM-1 is unusually well measured for a vegetable tree.** Five field trials report it (S4, S5, S6, S7, S8), four of
them at its home station in Periyakulam. They measure plant height, trunk girth, branches, panicles, flowers per panicle,
fruit set, days to flowering, pod length, girth, mass, seeds per pod and yield. Its leaves, flowers, seeds and seedlings
are not described apart from the species anywhere I could read, so those rows carry [SP] values from the floras.

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** 'PKM-1' is a "seed moringa": a seed-propagated, annual-managed drumstick bred at the Horticultural
  Research Station (now the Horticultural College and Research Institute, HC&RI) of Tamil Nadu Agricultural University at
  Periyakulam, Theni district (S2). It was "released during the year 1989" as a **pure-line selection from the
  'Eppodumvendran' local type** (S3 [2nd]; the name means roughly "always winning"). With 'PKM-2' it is one of the
  two varieties TNAU recommends for annual moringa (S1). [CV]
- **Its marks (TNAU's own description).** "Plants grow to a height of 4–6 m and come to flower in 90–100 days after
  planting. The first harvest starts 160–170 days after planting and on an average each tree bears 200–225 fruits/year.
  Pods are 65–70 cm long with 6.3 cm girth and 150 g weight. Fruits are green coloured and highly pulpy" (S2). The HC&RI
  listing gives pods of ~75 cm and ~150 g and harvest in 7–8 months (S3 [2nd]). ECHO calls it "a bushy variety that is
  grown for both leaf and pod production" (S21). In a Florida trial it made the widest, most branched canopy of three
  cultivars (S10 [2nd]). [CV]
- **Managed as an annual, but a tree.** It is sown in pits, pinched at ~75 cm to make it branch, cropped in its first
  year, then **cut back to 0.9–1.0 m** after each harvest and re-cropped as a ratoon "for 3 years" (S1, S2). Left alone it
  is the species: a soft-wooded, deciduous tree to 10–12 m (S11, S12). A model should therefore grow a **pinched,
  vase-shaped tree of 3–5 m** with 5–10 main branches, not a single-stemmed tree.
- **Correcting the brief.**
  - *Pod length.* The brief's "65–70 cm?" is TNAU's catalogue figure (S2). Field trials measure **61–80 cm**: 61.0 cm in
    the main crop and 75.4 cm in the first ratoon at Periyakulam (S4), 68.6 cm (S5), 75.8 cm in Gujarat (S7). Use
    **~70 cm**, with the first crop a little shorter.
  - *Pod mass.* The "~150 g" is TNAU's catalogue figure (S2, S3). The trials weigh less: **118 g** (main crop) to 119 g
    (ratoons) at the recommended spacing (S4), and **145.7 g** in Gujarat (S7). Use **~130 g** (110–150 g).
  - *Pod girth.* 6.3 cm (S2) is confirmed in range: **5.7–6.9 cm** measured (S5, S7).
  - *Pinching.* TNAU gives a height, not a day: "Pinch off the seedlings when they are about 75 cm in height" (S1, S2).
    The "80th day" pinch is TNAU's advice for **'PKM-2'**, not 'PKM-1' (S1). I estimate ~75 cm is reached at
    **~45–60 days** from sowing (see §2).
  - *Height kept at 3–4 m.* Not a TNAU rule. Measured heights at harvest are **2.9–3.6 m** in the main crop and
    **3.6–5.0 m** in the ratoons (S4, S7); TNAU's description says 4–6 m (S2).
  - *Germination type.* **Hypogeal** (cryptocotyledonary): the cotyledons stay inside the seed coat at sowing depth
    (S17, S19). One seed leaflet says "epigeal" (S16); I follow the two sources that describe and draw the seedling.
  - *First leaves.* They are **not simple or trifoliolate**: "First pair of true leaves bipinnate" (S19).
  - *Leaflet size.* The brief's 1–2 × 0.5–1.2 cm is Flora of China's species range (S12). On 'PKM-1' the leaflet of the
    third leaf from the top measured **2.75 × 1.65 cm** (S9).
  - *Petal spots.* I found no source for a "yellow-spotted base" on the petals. Flora of Pakistan puts **"prominent
    yellow streaks"** on the reflexed **sepals**, and gives the filaments as yellow (S14).
  - *Reddish pods.* No source gives 'PKM-1' a red tinge: its pods are "green coloured" (S2). Red-tipped pods belong to
    the perennial Kerala type 'Chemmuringa' (S2).
  - *Yield.* "220 pods/tree/year" and "50–55 t/ha" are TNAU's figures (S1). Its own trial at the recommended
    2.5 × 2.5 m spacing gave **150 pods, 17.7 kg a tree and 28.3 t/ha** in the main crop (S4). Gujarat gave 314.7 pods
    and 36.7 kg a tree at 5 × 3 m (S7).
  - *Chromosomes.* Teaching notes print 2n = 22 (S2). PROSEA gives **2n = 28** (S11), the accepted count.

- **Growth rate for the model.** Slow for the first 3–4 weeks while the seed feeds it (S18), then about **2–3 cm a
  day** to pinching, and **3–4 cm a day** from 2 to 5 months (from 1.05 m to 2.04 m to 4.71 m, S5) [EST]. Height
  plateaus after the first crop because the tree is cut back to 0.9–1.0 m after each harvest (S1, S2).
- **Trunk.** Ø grows from ~1 cm at pinching to ~9 cm at pod set in the first ~5 months (S5) [EST stage ages]. Use
  **~1.5 cm a month** in Ø in the first crop, then ~0.5–1 cm a month in the ratoons [EST]. The bole is short, crooked,
  "often forked from near the base" (S15), and soft (wood density 0.5–0.7, S15).

## Gaps

These rows have no published number for 'PKM-1', and some have none for the species either. Each is marked [EST] above.

- **Ages of the growth stages in S5.** The trial gives height and girth at a "vegetative", "flowering" and
  "pod-setting" stage without the days. I set them at ~60–75, ~90–110 and ~140–170 days from sowing, from TNAU's 90–100 d
  to flowering (S2) and 160–170 d to first harvest (S2).
- **The 1.79 cm "girth" at the vegetative stage (S5).** As a girth it gives Ø 0.6 cm for a 1.05 m plant, which is too
  thin. It is probably a diameter. I used Ø ~1.5–2 cm.
- **Age at pinching.** TNAU gives only a height (75 cm). I estimated 45–60 days from the transplant height of 20–30 cm at
  one month (S20) and 1.05 m at the vegetative stage (S5).
- **Height before one month.** Estimated from the 25-day leaf stage (S17) and the 20–30 cm at one month (S20).
- **Crown width.** Only one measure (1.68 m in a pruned Florida tree at ~1 year, S10 [2nd]). The rest is from the
  spacing (the rows close at 2.5 m).
- **Architecture model.** No published Hallé–Oldeman model for moringa. Rauh's is my reading of an orthotropic tree with
  lateral (axillary) flowering on new growth.
- **Branch angle, phyllotaxis angle and internodes.** Only "alternate" and "crowded towards the twig-ends" are published.
  I chose 137.5°, 35–55° from vertical, and internodes from the growth rate.
- **Leaf length on 'PKM-1'.** Not published. 45 cm is the middle of the species' 25–60 cm.
- **Leaf complexity by node.** Only the first leaves and the tenth leaf (both bipinnate, S19) and the adult leaf
  (2–3-pinnate) are described. The counts in between are interpolated.
- **The 'PKM-1' leaflet (S9).** The paper says "leaf length" and "leaf breadth" of "the third rachis from the top". At
  2.75 × 1.65 cm these can only be leaflets, so I read them as such.
- **Leaf life.** Not measured. 60–90 days is from the 40-day leaf regrowth cycle (S8) and the leaves crowded at the tips.
- **Gland size, rachis colour, young-leaf colour.** From the descriptions, not measured.
- **Root sizes.** Only "thick", "swollen and carrot-like" and "tuberous when young" are published. Every root dimension is
  estimated from the shoot size by age.
- **Epicotyl length.** Estimated from the drawings' scale in S17.
- **Pod growth curve and picking day.** Only for other cultivars (Trinidad, 32 DAA, S22 [2nd]) and the species (55–70 d,
  S11, S16). For 'PKM-1' the 65–75 d is TNAU's first harvest minus its first flowering (S2).
- **Pod tapers and stalk.** From the species' "dagger-shaped", "tapering to a point" and "apex beaked".
- **Seed mass.** The 0.288 g of S2 is for annual drumstick in TNAU's teaching notes, with no cultivar named. The PKM-1
  seed count per ounce is a seller's (S25 [2nd]).
- **Gum ageing and bark lenticels.** From general experience of the species' bark, not a source.
- **Colours as hex.** All hex values are estimates from the published descriptions (dark green leaflets paler below,
  cream zygomorphic flowers with yellow-streaked sepals and yellow anthers, green pods drying light brown and silvery
  inside, dark brown seeds with straw wings, whitish-grey corky bark).
- **Second-hand values ([2nd]).** S3 (HC&RI variety listing: the 1989 release, the 'Eppodumvendran' parent, 75 cm, 7–8
  months; page returned 503), S10 (ECHO Florida trial, abstract only), S22 (Trinidad maturity, abstract), S23 (seed
  development, abstract), S24 (Morton 1991, abstract), S25 (seed sellers).
- **Not reachable or not read.** Ramachandran, Peter & Gopalakrishnan (1980) in *Economic Botany* 34: 276–283 (only its
  metadata turned up). Morton (1991) in *Economic Botany* 45: 318–333 (abstract only). The ECHO technical note on
  'PKM-1' (HTTP 403). Kew POWO and Flora of Tropical East Africa (not needed beyond the four floras read). No IIHR or ICAR
  bulletin with 'PKM-1' numbers turned up. The Nicaraguan nursery thesis with weekly seedling and root growth
  (repositorio.una.edu.ni) could not be downloaded; it is the best source for the root and seedling gaps.
