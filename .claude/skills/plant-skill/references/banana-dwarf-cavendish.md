# Musa acuminata (AAA Group, Cavendish Subgroup) 'Dwarf Cavendish': facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-04 for a banana grown stage by stage under glass in a dome in middle Europe. Built on the template
`datasheet.md` and set out like `cacao-amelonado.md`. Where greenhouse values differ from the open field or the tropics,
they are given on their own line.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured on 'Dwarf Cavendish' itself (also under its synonyms: 'Pequeña Enana', 'Dwarf Chinese', 'Chinese',
  'Basrai', *M. cavendishii* Lamb.).
- **[SP]**: said of the Cavendish subgroup, of dessert bananas or of the genus.
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with the reasoning given. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a snippet, or a review that cites the work, not the original.

Each number has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are metres, kilograms and days.
Ages run from planting the plantlet or sucker unless a row says otherwise.

---

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** 'Dwarf Cavendish' is the shortest commercial clone of the Cavendish subgroup. It is "the most widely
  distributed clone of edible banana worldwide" and "short and compact, it is relatively cold-tolerant". It is very
  prone to **chokethroat** (the bunch fails to push out of the throat) "where temperatures below 15 °C (59 °F) occur for
  extended periods". [CV] S2
- **Its marks.** The male bracts and dead male flowers stay on the rachis, so the rachis reaches nearly to the ground,
  clothed in dry bracts. [CV] S3. Its leaves are broad on short, stout petioles. [CV] S3, S10. Its bunch weight per metre
  of pseudostem height is the highest of the Cavendish clones. [CV] S4
- **History, and why it suits a glasshouse.** This is the banana of the hothouse. It went from southern China to
  Mauritius in 1826, and then to England in 1829, "grown in a hothouse". At Chatsworth (Paxton, 1836) it was named
  *M. cavendishii* Lambert. "The Chatsworth gardens were the original source of all the Cavendish bananas for commercial
  cultivation." S3. It was the Canary Islands' only cultivar until about 1990, grown in the open and under plastic. S4
- **Rules for the model.** Each axis flowers once (hapaxanthic). It is replaced by its best sucker (the follower), so
  a clump (the "mat") shows mother, daughter and granddaughter together. Never give it seeds. Plants in a clone vary
  little, apart from what age and season do.

Model notes:

- **Juvenile leaves.** A young sucker's or plantlet's first leaves are scale leaves, then narrow, lanceolate "sword"
  leaves (S6). Young Dwarf Cavendish-type plants often show **maroon to purple blotches** on the blade that fade with
  age (seen on 'Super Dwarf Cavendish', S10 [2nd]). Give leaves 1–10 blotches and a narrow L/W of about 3–4 [EST].
- **Tissue-culture start for the game.** Use a plantlet 10–15 cm tall with 4 leaves, then about 45 cm with 6 leaves
  at 3 months [EST from S17].

- **Rule of thumb.** "Excluding the juvenile period, the circumference at the base in inches was slightly smaller than
  the number of leaves, whilst the height was approximately twice the circumference." [CV] S7
- **Height/girth ratio at shooting.** 3.0 for Dwarf Cavendish, against 4.1–4.3 for Grande Naine and Williams, so it is
  a stout, squat stem. [CV] S4
- **Whole plant.** The leaf tips stand about 0.5–1 m above the throat.
  - 2.5–4 m in cultivation (RHS, S11).
  - 1–3 m (NParks, S10).
  - "8 to 12 feet [2.4–3.7 m] high; trunks erect or slightly reclining, 4 to 8 feet [1.2–2.4 m] long, 8 to 14 inches
    [0.20–0.36 m] in diameter at base" (Hawaiʻi 'Chinese', S3).
  - 8–10 ft mature (S18).
- **Greenhouse.** Under plastic in the Canaries, Dwarf Cavendish grew **taller and wider** than in the open, most of all
  in the southern greenhouse. [CV] S8 [2nd]. Under plastic in Turkey, leaf number, pseudostem circumference and height
  were all higher than in the open field. [CV] S9 [2nd]. **Model it 10–20 % taller and thicker under glass.** [EST]

**Sheath (pseudostem) colour and blotches.**

- No measured descriptor for this cultivar could be read; the MGIS record ITC0002 lists the descriptors without values
  (S20).
- Use for the model: outer sheaths **yellow-green to mid-green, with large irregular dark brown to blackish blotches**,
  densest near the petiole bases and the top. Inner, fresh sheaths are pale green to cream. Old outer sheaths dry to
  straw-brown, fibrous and split, hanging in strips at the base. A light **whitish wax bloom** on young sheaths.
  [EST from the Cavendish-subgroup look and S3 ("glaucous")]
- Petiole margins are **green with pink or red margins**. [CV] S3

**Growth rhythm.**

- Growth is continuous, not in flushes. Leaves emerge one at a time from the throat as a rolled "cigar", which unrolls
  in about 7 days in good conditions and 15–20 days in poor ones. [SP] S6
- The emergence rate follows temperature (section 3).

**Phyllotaxis.**

- Spiral, the "spiral arrangement of petioles distinct" ([CV] S3), with the sheaths concentric.
- Banana leaves follow a spiral of about 1/3 to 2/5 divergence in the literature, but no Dwarf Cavendish value was
  found. Use **~140°** divergence (close to the 2/5 and Fibonacci angle) [EST].
- Successive petioles are "successively longer", each leaf's petiole stretching just enough for the blade to clear the
  throat. [CV] S7

**Branching (suckers, the mat).**

- Suckers rise from buds on the rhizome next to the mother: peepers, then sword suckers. One follower is kept per mat.
  S1, S6
- In the Canaries the follower kept was the best sucker with 13+1 leaves by 1 August (for the 2nd cycle) and 16+1 for
  the 3rd. [CV] S4
- A 'Double' mutant of Dwarf Chinese makes 2–7 bunches per plant. [CV] S2 — do not use it for the base model.

**Greenhouse (middle Europe).**

- Under plastic in the Canaries, the total leaves per cycle were the same as in the open air, but warmer winters raised
  winter leaf emission and so shortened the cycle. [CV] S8 [2nd]
- Protected cultivation cut radiation by about 20 % but raised productivity by 20–30 %. [CV] S5 citing S8
- At 18/14 °C (day/night), leaf output of Cavendish fell by **51 %** against 30/25 °C. Low light at 18 °C caused
  photoinhibition. [SP] S21 [2nd]
- **For a dome in middle Europe:** in dull winters (low light, short days) model about 1 leaf a fortnight, even when
  heated. From May to September, model about 1 leaf a week. [EST from S7, S21]

## 6. "Bark": pseudostem surface, corm and roots

**Young pseudostem.**

- Smooth green sheaths with a waxy bloom.
- Brown-black blotches appear on the outer sheaths toward the top. [EST, as in section 2]

**Old pseudostem.**

- The outer sheaths dry to brown and fibrous, split lengthwise, and hang.
- The dead leaves form a brown skirt.
- The cut stem is wet, white and concentric. [SP] S1, S6

**Corm (rhizome).**

- Underground. Once the flower is initiated, the corm "elongates vertically to form an aerial stalk". [CV] S7
- Mass and size: see section 1.

**Roots.**

- Number: **200–300** in a vigorous plant [CV] S7; 200–500 [SP] S1.
- Diameter: primary (cord) roots about **4.3 mm** in dessert bananas (S22 [2nd]). Fleshy, white when young, browning
  with age.
- Depth and spread:
  - Most roots lie in the top **15 cm**, none deeper than 80 cm (Nigeria) (S22 [2nd]).
  - Effective root depth 0.3–0.4 m (Robinson, via S5).
  - In deep soils down to 1.5 m and up to 4.9 m sideways (S1).
  - A mat's roots spread 6–10 ft (1.8–3 m) (S1).
- Branching: secondary roots 0.09–0.52 mm, tertiary roots 0.06–0.27 mm. [SP] S5 citing Lecompte et al. 2005

| 10–15 months | **shooting**: the purple bud rises from the throat, bends over and hangs; the hands are exposed bract by bract over ~1–2 weeks | S1, S4 |
| shooting + ~2 weeks | all female hands open; the male rachis keeps growing toward the ground, bracts persistent | S3 |
| shooting + 90–180 days | bunch full (90 days in summer, 180 in winter; about 115 days under glass in summer) | S7, S9 |

## Gaps

These rows have no published number for Dwarf Cavendish. Each is marked [EST] above with how it was estimated.

- **Petiole length.** Only "short, stout" is published. I set 0.25–0.40 m from the cultivar's look.
- **Male bud size.** I set 20–25 × 10–12 cm from photos and the size classes used by the descriptor list.
- **Inner bract colour.** No measured value. The outer colour is published (dark purple with bloom).
- **Pseudostem colour and blotches.** The MGIS/ITC0002 descriptor values exist but could not be read (S20). The colours
  are taken from the Cavendish-subgroup look and S3's "glaucous".
- **Leaf colours as hex.** Estimates. "Glaucous" (waxy) on both faces is published (S3).
- **Corm diameter.** Estimated from the corm mass (S7) and the base diameter (S3).
- **Height and girth before 4 months, and the month-by-month curve.** Summerville gives height and girth by the number
  of leaves (Table 2), not by date. I converted leaves to months with the emission rates in S7 and S12.
- **Months to shooting under glass in middle Europe.** I set 12–18 months for a heated dome. It rests on the Canarian
  plastic-house results (S8) and the 18 °C slowdown (S21). There is no measured Central European number. The Bristol
  5-year case and Iceland's "almost two years" are anecdotes.
- **Fingers per hand.** One Canarian value (25.6 on the 2nd hand) and one Indian value (14.3). The spread from top hand
  to bottom hand is estimated.
- **Phyllotaxis angle.** Estimated.
- **Second-hand values ([2nd]).** S8 (Canarian greenhouse vs open air), S9 (Turkish greenhouse), S12 (organic Canary
  plants), S14 (Basrai, India), S17 (plantlet size), S21 (18 °C trial) and S22 (root diameter) were read only as
  abstracts or snippets.
