# Persea americana Mill. 'Fuerte': facts for a procedural model

Researched 2026-11-05 for an avocado grown stage by stage from seed to ripe fruit. Built on the template `datasheet.md`
and set out like `banana-dwarf-cavendish.md`. 'Fuerte' is a grafted clone, so the tree in the model is two plants: a
seedling rootstock (the seed stage) and the 'Fuerte' scion budded onto it in the nursery. Section 1 says which numbers
belong to which.

**Labels used below**

- **[CV]**: measured on 'Fuerte' itself (also in reports that call it "Fuerte No. 15" or the "parent Fuerte tree").
- **[SP]**: said of *Persea americana*, of the Mexican or Guatemalan race, or of avocado cultivars in general.
- **[EST]**: my estimate for modelling, with the reasoning given. It is not a published value.
- **[2nd]**: I read only an abstract, a snippet, a blog or a review that cites the work, not the original.

Each number has a source tag such as S1. The full references are at the end. Units are metres, kilograms, days and
years. Ages run **from sowing the rootstock seed** unless a row says otherwise ("from planting" = from setting the
nursery tree in the field, about 1–1.5 y after sowing).

## 0. The cultivar in one paragraph

- **What it is.** A chance seedling in the garden of Alejandro Le Blanc at Atlixco, Puebla, Mexico (about 1,830 m).
  It was first noticed in 1908, soon after it began to bear. In 1911 Carl B. Schmidt, collecting for F. O. Popenoe's
  West India Gardens at Altadena, California, cut budwood from it as his "No. 15". [CV] S1, S29
- **Its name.** Popenoe named it "Fuerte" (strong). One account says the buds "nearly all grew, and grew so strongly"
  (S29). Another says the young trees came through the 1913 freeze better than other varieties (S1, S29). Which came
  first is disputed (S26 [2nd]).
- **Its race.** A natural Mexican × Guatemalan hybrid (S3), about 50/50 (S14). The Mexican half gives the anise smell
  of the leaves, the thin skin, the cold hardiness and the early winter crop.
- **Its marks.**
  - The tree is broad and spreading, wider than tall. [CV] S3, S5, S6, S9
  - The limbs are crooked and "bent", with a strong tendency to throw long "strong arms". [CV] S1, S22
  - The fruit is pear-shaped with a neck and a slanted, asymmetric stylar end. [CV] S5, S12, S26
  - The skin is thin and green, with many small yellow dots. It stays green when ripe. [CV] S3, S4, S5
  - It is a **B-type** flower. [CV] S4, S5, S13
  - It bears biennially (alternate bearing) and erratically ("drone" trees). [CV] S1, S3, S6, S33
- **History.** It was California's leading avocado for decades: more than 75 % of the trees in 1936 (S1), more than
  80 % of 16,000 acres in 1948 (S29), and 61 % of all fruit shipped (S3). It was then displaced by 'Hass'. By 2001–02
  it was down to 1,452 bearing acres (S14).
- **Rules for the model.**
  - Rauh's model (section 2), grown flush by flush.
  - One seed per fruit.
  - A grafted tree: a short seedling trunk with a bud union about 0.1–0.3 m above the soil.
  - Every 'Fuerte' scion is the same clone, so the scion varies only with age, site and crop load. The rootstock
    varies (S33).

## 1. Seed, rootstock and the nursery tree

A 'Fuerte' tree is **never grown from its own seed**. A seed of a 'Fuerte' fruit gives a variable seedling, not a
'Fuerte'. Nurseries sow seed of Mexican-race rootstock trees ('Topa Topa', 'Mexicola', 'Ganter', 'Duke', 'Zutano' and
others, S16) and bud or graft 'Fuerte' onto the seedling. For the model, the seed stage can show a 'Fuerte' seed (it
looks much like a rootstock seed). The tree above the bud union is 'Fuerte'.

- Show the **bud union** as a slight swelling or step in the bark, 0.1–0.3 m above the soil. The rootstock below it can

- **Habit.** "Broad" (S3). "Spreading" on every rootstock tried in Cyprus, against 'Ettinger''s upright form (S6, S7).
  UPOV gives "spreading" with 'Fuerte' as the example (S5). Limbs are "upright, peculiarly crooked or 'bent'" (S1).
  The variety has a "sprawling habit of growth … often stressed tendency to throw so-called strong arms"; "the parent
  tree in Mexico had a very pronounced strong arm" (S22). [CV]
- **Lateral beats vertical with age.** "Observations made in mature Fuerte groves show a decrease in the amount of
  vertical growth as compared to the lateral growth." [CV] S21. So let H/D fall from ~0.8 when young to ~0.6–0.7 when
  mature [EST].
- **Unchecked avocados in general** reach 15–20 m tall and 12–15 m wide within 15–20 years in Australia [SP] S15. In
  Florida the species is "usually to 30 ft (9 m) but sometimes to 60 ft (18 m)", with a trunk 30–60 cm across [SP] S3.
  'Fuerte' is at the broad end of this range.
- **Cultivated form.** The head (the first scaffold limbs) is trained at **0.9–1.2 m** (3–4 ft), with 3–4 vase-shaped
  leaders [CV] S22. The parent tree branched at 1.47 m (S1).

### Growth rhythm, branching, phyllotaxis

| Row | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Growth rhythm | **Flushes.** Generally **one reproductive (spring) flush and two vegetative (summer, autumn) flushes a year** in the subtropics (S9). Each flush lasts **3–5 weeks** (S19). Branches that are shaded or carrying fruit make only one flush a year, during flowering (S19). In Florida and Cuba up to 4 flushes; fewer in colder climates; "the first flush is usually the strongest, and is associated with flowering" (S18). Root flushes alternate with shoot flushes, two a year: spring and late summer/autumn (S15) | [SP] |
| Shoot growth per year ('Fuerte', 4–5 y old, South Australia) | annual growth sections on major limbs **0.29–0.77 m** a year (means of 475, 502, 773, 765 and 289 mm for 1986–1990). Major limbs 2.1 m long at 4 years from grafting | [CV] S9 (Tables 3.1, 3.2) |
| Nodes per flush | primary axes of a **reproductive (spring) flush: 17.7 ± 2.3 nodes** in 'Fuerte' (14 for vegetative flushes in the genus, 21 for reproductive). Axillary proleptic shoots ~12 nodes; sylleptic shoots ~6. Current annual module: **57 ± 3 nodes** (shoots + buds) | [CV] S9 (ch. 4, Table 4.3; ch. 3, Table 3.4); [SP] S9 |
| Branching | **Sylleptic** shoots (grow at once, without a resting bud, bulbous base, long first internode, no bud-scar ring) and **proleptic** shoots (from resting buds, with a ring of bud-scale scars at the base). On 'Fuerte' the current annual module of a major limb carried **29.0 ± 3.6 sylleptic and 2.0 ± 2.0 proleptic shoots and 27 resting buds**: the most sylleptic shoots of all five cultivars. **45.8 branches per major limb**, 80.7 % under 0.5 m, 9.5 % 0.5–1 m, 9.8 % over 1 m. **3.7 ± 0.33 major limbs** (> 35 mm across) at 4 years, at about 30–60° from vertical (Fig. 3.4). "Weak apical dominance … weak acrotony, producing few major limbs, relatively short axillary branching". Each flush starts and ends with bract-like leaves on short internodes, leaving a **bud-scar ring** (compressed nodes) between flushes | [CV] S9; [SP] S18, S19; angle [EST] from S9 Fig. 3.4 |
| Phyllotaxis | **spiral**, one leaf and one axillary bud per node (S18). No measured angle was found: use **2/5 (144°)**, ~137.5° in the model | [SP] S18; angle [EST] |
| Internodes | "intermediate" length (UPOV, Fuerte and Hass) (S5). With 0.3–0.8 m of axis per year and ~40–57 nodes per year, use **1.5–3 cm on full-leaf internodes, 2–5 mm at the bud-scar rings** | [CV] S5; length [EST] from S9 |
| Roots | **Shallow and hairless.** **80–90 % of the feeder-root length lies in the upper 8–10 in (20–25 cm)** under the canopy (S15). The feeder roots have "very few root hairs" (S15); none were found at all, in soil or in water culture (S20). New feeder roots are white to pale yellow, then darken and turn woody. A seedling **taproot** goes straight down: through a 30 cm nursery ball "and partially into the ground below" (S17b). In the field it reaches **1–1.5 m** where drainage allows; the water table must stay below 0.9 m (S3). Lateral roots reach to the drip line and a little beyond | [SP] S15, S20, S17b, S3; taproot depth and spread [EST] |

## 4. Flowers

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Where borne | **panicles (thyrses) clustered near the shoot tips**, on the outer, sunlit surface of the crown, from buds formed on the summer flush. Two kinds: **determinate** (the shoot ends in a panicle) and **indeterminate** (a leafy spring shoot grows on from the panicle's tip). In 'Hass' 63 % are indeterminate: 6 basal bud scales, then **6 axillary panicles in the axils of bracts, then up to 9 true leaves**. A tree carries hundreds of panicles | [SP] S13, S9 (ch. 4) |
| Panicle size | axis length **"medium"** (UPOV, Fuerte). Use **8–15 cm**, much branched, the axes yellowish-green to cream and finely hairy | [CV] S5; cm [EST] |
| Flowers per panicle and per tree | ~**150 per panicle** ('Hass'). **"Up to a million flowers"** a year on a grown tree | [SP] S13 |
| Size, stalk | about **1 cm across** when open. Pedicel **~5 mm**, hairy | [SP] S25 |
| Perianth | **6 tepals in two whorls of 3**, pale green to yellow-green or cream, densely hairy. Outer tepals ~3 mm, inner ~4 mm (the outer slightly smaller). Tepals persist briefly on the young fruit | [SP] S3, S25 |
| Stamens | **9 fertile stamens in 3 whorls**. Filaments hairy, ~1 mm (species). The 3 inner stamens carry **2 orange nectar glands** each at the base. Anthers open by 4 flaps (valves). Then **3 staminodes** (a 4th whorl) that secrete nectar in the female stage | [SP] S3, S13, S25 |
| Pistil | 1 carpel: a hairy ovary, a **straight** style (UPOV, Fuerte) ~1.5 mm, and a papillate stigma ~0.5 mm, **white when receptive, brown and shrivelled after**. Nectary **stalked** (UPOV, Fuerte) | [CV] S5; [SP] S13, S25 |
| Dichogamy (type B) | each flower opens twice. **Day 1 afternoon: female** (stamens bent out at ~90°, stigma white and receptive, open 2–3 h), then it closes overnight. **Day 2 morning: male** (outer 6 stamens fold in to 30–40°, inner 3 stand erect beside the pistil, the anthers shed pollen 1–2 h after opening), then it closes for good. With cool weather the cycle slips. At 18–21 °C days and 7–12 °C nights, **'Fuerte' flowers failed to open as females at all** | [CV] S4, S5, S13 (citing Leslie & Bringhurst 1951) |
| First flowering | grafted trees usually begin to flower **in the 3rd year after planting** (~4 y from sowing). Ungrafted seedlings take 4–5 y (S3) or 10–15 y (S13) | [SP] S13, S3 |
| Season (California) | beginning of flowering **"medium"** (UPOV: 'Duke' early, 'Fuerte' medium, 'Hass' late). Fruit sets **15 February to 15 May** at La Habra Heights. Blooms can start as early as October and last into June. Main bloom **January–April**, peaking about March; "both the A- and B-type cultivars bloom continuously for about 2 months". 'Fuerte' flowered under both 9 h and 15 h days | [CV] S5, S2, S26 [2nd], S8, S13 |
| Pollination | bees (honeybees in California) carry pollen from male-stage to female-stage flowers. Planting with an A-type cultivar helps. Pollen tubes reach the ovule in 2–4 h | [SP] S13 |
| Set | 'Fuerte' trees matured **0.031, 0.023, 0.001 and 0.008 %** of their flowers (Cameron, Mueller & Wallace 1952). "Usually sets less than 0.02 per cent mature fruits" (Chandler 1958). At most ~0.1 % with heavy bloom (Bergh 1967). 0.15 % under natural conditions (other cultivars). A good crop is 100–200 fruit from about a million flowers | [CV] S24 (citing the primary works); [SP] S27, S13 |

## Where it grows (for worlds)

| Row | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Climate | subtropical, Mediterranean or highland tropical. **Home:** Atlixco, 1,830 m; mean annual ~15.5 °C (60 °F), max ~32 °C, min ~1.5 °C; ~890 mm (35 in) of rain May–October, irrigated in the dry season. **Grown in** coastal southern California, Israel, Cyprus (420 mm rain + irrigation), southern Turkey, Spain, South Africa, Australia, New Zealand, Chile, Mexico. It flowers poorly in the lowland tropics (S13). Needs warm days (> ~21 °C) and nights > ~10–12 °C in bloom for a good set. Wind dehydrates flowers and drops fruit, so windbreaks help | [CV] S1, S6, S13; [SP] S3 |
| Cold | **intermediate**, "the standard measure of frost resistance". Mature trees are damaged at **−2.2 °C (28 °F)** (Mexican types −3.9 °C; Guatemalan −1.7 °C). The fruit freezes if held **4 h at −3.3 to −2.8 °C (26–27 °F)**. A 'Fuerte' came through **−3.9 °C (25 °F)** better than 'Hass', 'Reed' and 'Lamb' in one garden. In the 1937 freeze 'Fuerte' was intermediate between the pure Mexican types and the tender ones. Young trees are more tender. **Model: leaves scorched below −2 °C, defoliation and twig dieback below −4 °C, killed to the graft at about −6 °C** | [CV] S23, S14, S26 [2nd], S23b [2nd]; thresholds [EST] |
| Heat, water | needs perfect drainage; does not stand waterlogging. pH 6–7 is best; chlorosis on calcareous soil (Cyprus pH 8.2 needs Fe chelate). Salt-sensitive (Mexican roots) | [SP] S3, S14; [CV] S6 |
| Shade | **full sun**. Flowers and fruit form on the sunlit outer shell of the crown. Shaded lower limbs lose vigour and die when trees crowd ("crowding immediately shades and causes the shaded limbs to lose vitality"). Seedlings are raised in light shade | [CV] S22; [SP] S13, S3 |
| Spacing | Cyprus trials **6 × 6 m** (278 /ha). California **20 × 20 ft (6.1 m)**, thinned from 7 years; mature trees on deep soil want **12–18 m** (40–60 ft) squares. Generally 7.5–10.7 m | [CV] S6, S21, S22; [SP] S3 |
| Productive life | 50+ years (S3); the parent tree was still bearing ~500 fruits at an estimated 60+ years (S1). California 'Fuerte' orchards from 1914–1920 lasted many decades | [CV] S1; [SP] S3 |

## Gaps

These rows have no published number for 'Fuerte'. Each is marked [EST] above with how it was estimated.

- **Seed size and mass.** Only the seed share of the fruit (15–18.8 %) is published for 'Fuerte'. I set ~50–55 × 40–45
  mm and 40–50 g from that share × the fruit mass. The sources also disagree on the size: "large" (S4) against "small,
  tight" (S3).
- **Days to radicle and to shoot.** Only "about 30 days" to germination (S16) and 4–6 weeks (S3) are published. The
  split into root first (~2–3 weeks), then shoot (~4–5 weeks) is my estimate.
- **Seedling height and leaf count at 6 and 12 months.** These are interpolated from the nursery timings (budding size
  at 6–8 months, 25 cm at 3 months under heat, 0.9 m at 14 months) and Thorp's ~14 nodes per flush.
- **Height by age.** Measured only at the 4th year from grafting (2.4 m, S9) and for the ~60–80-year-old parent tree
  (13.7 m, S1). Heights at 6, 10 and 20 y are estimated from crown widths (S21, S22) with H/D 0.8 falling to 0.7.
- **Trunk diameter before 16 years and in good soils.** It is measured in Cyprus at 16 and 20 y (18.9 and 21.3 cm, a
  dry, calcareous, pruned site) and for the parent tree (42 cm). The Orange County remark (> 42 cm by ~17 y) shows that
  deep California soils give much thicker trunks. Values at 2–10 y are interpolated.
- **Leaf blade and petiole in cm.** Only the UPOV classes are published for 'Fuerte' (length and width "medium",
  petiole "very long", apex acuminate). The cm values are scaled on 'Hass'-type patent measurements read in snippets
  (S30). The secondary vein count (6–9 pairs) and the leaf outline are estimates.
- **Leaf life.** Measured on 'Hass' (7.8–12.5 months), not 'Fuerte'.
- **Flower and panicle sizes.** The flower size is a species measurement (S25); the panicle length in cm is an estimate
  (the UPOV class only); the flowers per panicle (~150) are a 'Hass' value.
- **Skin thickness in mm.** Only "moderately thin" is published (S5). I set 0.8–1.0 mm against 'Hass' 1.6 mm.
- **Pedicel and peduncle lengths in cm.** Only the UPOV class "medium" is published.
- **Fruit size by day before October.** Marsh's monthly curve (S2) is published as a graph that could not be read; only
  the text values and Schroeder's half-length at 55–60 mm were used. The early points of the model curve are fitted
  estimates, and the 80-day width (54.4 mm, Hatay) is [2nd].
- **Phyllotaxis angle.** "Spiral" is published; 2/5 is estimated.
- **Bark colours, young flush colours and hex values.** These are from species descriptions and descriptions of the
  cultivar's look, not measurements.
- **Cold thresholds for the model.** UC's 28 °F (−2.2 °C) critical temperature for the hybrids and the fruit freezing
  at 26–27 °F are published. The −4 °C and −6 °C steps are estimates.
- **Second-hand values ([2nd]).** S26 (Alder's profile: bloom from October, leaf colour, 25 °F), S28 (leaf life),
  S30 (patent leaf sizes), S31 (germination 33 d; seedling height at 8 months; Ozdemir fruit widths) and S32 (bark)
  were read only as snippets or blog summaries.
