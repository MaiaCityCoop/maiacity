Ananas comosus (L.) Merr. 'Queen Victoria' (Bromeliaceae, Queen group) — the "ananas Victoria" of Réunion and Mauritius: a small, compact, very spiny pineapple whose small golden fruit (0.7–0.95 kg) is the sweetest of the trade pineapples. A rosette herb, not a tree: a short club-shaped stem hidden in a dense spiral of stiff, channelled, sword-shaped leaves, which ends in one inflorescence that becomes one fruit topped by a leafy crown; the plant is then relayed by suckers from its leaf axils. Seedless; planted as a sucker, in double rows on ridges under black plastic mulch, in full sun.
Planting material: a sucker of 250 g, 30–40 cm long, about 27 short stiff spiny leaves in a tight fan, its base curved where it grew out sideways from its mother's stem, a few brown roots already at its foot, its outer leaves browning at the tips.
Stem: club-shaped, 20–30 cm long, 3 cm thick at its foot and 5 cm under the fruit, its lowest 5–10 cm buried; wholly hidden by the leaf bases; brown aerial roots wind round it between the broad leaf sheaths.
Leaves: sessile, in a dense spiral (about 137° from one to the next), each with a broad, thick, pale cream to pale green sheath clasping two thirds of the stem, then a stiff sword-shaped blade, U-channelled in section, tapering evenly to a hard sharp point. The longest (the D leaves, the youngest grown ones) 55–65 cm × 4.5–5 cm (about 12 : 1), standing at about 45°; the old outer ones shorter, spreading flatter, the inner young ones short and upright; the rosette is a fountain about 0.6 m high and 0.9 m across at forcing, with about 50 leaves. Upper face grey-green to mid green with a faint glaucous bloom; underside silvery-grey from a dense coat of tiny scales, in fine lengthwise stripes. Queen marks: hooked spines along the WHOLE margin of every leaf, 1–2 mm long, 3–6 mm apart, curving toward the leaf tip, green-cream at the base of the leaf and reddish-brown at their tips; the outer leaves flushed red-bronze to purplish along their margins and tips in the sun; the oldest outer leaves yellowing and browning from the tip, drying and lying on the mulch.
Inflorescence and flowers: two months after flower induction the heart of the rosette turns red ("red heart"): a small red cone ringed by 5–7 short red-orange bracts shows deep in the centre, the young heart leaves round it flushed red. It rises on a short stout peduncle (finally 8–15 cm, 2 cm thick, green with small red-tinged bracts) and flowers from the bottom up over about two weeks, 5–10 flowers a day: the cone about 6 cm tall at first flower, 75–86 flowers in spirals, each in the axil of a thick pointed red-pink bract; each flower tubular, about 2 cm, three violet-blue petals, white at their base, opening for one day, with short fleshy sepals coloured like the bract.
Fruit: the flowers and bracts fuse into one fruit of 75–86 eyes arranged in two sets of crossing spirals, 8 one way and 13 the other. Ripe: 13–15 cm tall × 10 cm across (without the crown), a short cylinder tapering to a cone in its top third; Queen's eyes are DEEP and protruding, each a six-sided raised shield about 2.2–2.5 cm across, wider than tall, with a small pointed bract tip at its foot and a little beak of dried sepals at its centre, deep grooves between the eyes. Its colour by age: red-pink with violet flowers; dull green with dry flowers at its eyes (8 × 5 cm); dark grey-green as it fills (most of 5 months); then yellowing from the base up eye by eye, picked from half yellow to all golden yellow-orange, the bract tips staying bronze-brown. Crown: a compact rosette of about 30–50 short stiff spiny grey-green leaves, 15–20 cm tall, 12–16 cm across. Cut lengthwise: deep golden-yellow crisp flesh, a small paler fibrous core 1.5–2 cm wide, the eyes as darker cavities under the skin, no seeds.
Suckers: by harvest one or two leafy suckers (30–45 cm) grow out from the leaf axils of the stem, half way up, curving out and up beside the fruit; no slips under the fruit on this cultivar.
Roots: brown, fine, wiry adventitious roots from the stem's base, mostly in the top 15–30 cm, spreading 0.5–1 m.
