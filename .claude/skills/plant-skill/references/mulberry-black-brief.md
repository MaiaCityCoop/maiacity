Morus nigra L. (Moraceae), the black mulberry — the classic European garden mulberry of old English lawns and college gardens (the 'Chelsea' / 'King James' tree), own-rooted, grown from a hardwood cutting. A small, slow, very long-lived deciduous tree: a short thick trunk, often leaning, forking low into crooked, zigzag, gnarled limbs, the crown a dense low dome much wider than tall.

Planting and the young plant: a hardwood cutting of last year's shoot, 25–30 cm long and 1.2–1.8 cm thick, grey-brown, set upright in the soil in winter with only its top two dark buds above ground. In spring white roots break from its buried base and two or three shoots from its top buds; their leaves are large and LOBED (2–5 deep rounded lobes, 12–20 cm), as on every vigorous young shoot and sucker; the stems are green and densely hairy. In its second year a single crooked stem 1.0–1.5 m with zigzag side shoots; by its fifth year a small tree 2–3 m, a trunk 4–6 cm thick forking at about 1 m into 3–5 spreading limbs, its first few fruit.

Wood and bark: this year's shoots green to grey-green, densely downy, with pale lenticels; buds large, ovoid, pointed, dark brown to almost black, scaly, set alternately on a zigzag twig; two- to ten-year wood grey-brown, smooth then finely cracked, orange-brown showing in the cracks; the old trunk thick, deeply and irregularly furrowed, its ridges grey-brown and the fissures rust to orange-brown, with large rounded burrs low on the trunk sprouting clusters of small twigs. Inside the wood is rich yellow. Every wound bleeds white milky latex.

The grown tree (about 25 years old): 7 m tall and 8 m across, the trunk 25 cm thick, SHORT (forking at 1.2–1.5 m), leaning 10–15° to one side; 4–6 heavy crooked limbs spreading out and arching down at their ends, the lowest nearly horizontal and sagging toward the ground; the crown a dense, low, broad dome, its leaves down to about 1 m above the grass all round. NOT a lollipop on a stem, NOT an upright oval: a broad, low, heavy mound like an old apple tree but wider and denser.

Leaf: alternate, two-ranked on side shoots; broadly heart-shaped (ovate-cordate), 10–14 cm long and 9–12 cm wide (length to width about 1.1 : 1), the base deeply notched (a sinus 1.5–2 cm deep) where the stalk joins, the tip short-pointed, the margin coarsely and regularly toothed with blunt teeth 3–5 mm wide, unlobed on the bearing tree. Upper side dark green, dull to slightly glossy, ROUGH like fine sandpaper, the veins sunken (3–5 main veins fanning from the base, then 4–6 pairs); underside paler grey-green, softly and evenly downy, the veins raised. Stalk short and stout, 1.5–2.5 cm, hairy. New leaves pale yellow-green and soft, unfolding late (mid to late May, the last tree in the garden); in autumn clear yellow.

Catkins: they come with the leaves on short new shoots (3–10 cm) from last year's wood, one in each of the lowest leaf axils. Male catkins cylindrical, hanging, 2.5–4 cm long and 6–8 mm thick, pale green to cream, each tiny flower with 4 stamens with yellow anthers. Female catkins short and plump, ovoid, 1–1.5 cm long, green, bristling with white hairy two-lobed stigmas like a small brush, on a stalk of only 2–5 mm.

Fruit: a multiple fruit (sorosis) like a short fat blackberry, ovoid to short-oblong, blunt at both ends, 23–27 mm long and 16–18 mm across, 3.5–5 g, almost stalkless (stalk 3–6 mm), sitting tight among the leaves at the base of the short shoot. It is made of only 18–28 LARGE rounded glossy drupelets, each 4–6 mm across, packed in a loose spiral, each still tipped by its dried dark stigma as a short bristle. By age: just set (1 cm, green, white stigmas still on it); green (1.5–2 cm, firm, lumpy); turning (pale green-white, flushed pink on the sunny side); red (bright red to crimson, some drupelets already darker); ripe (very dark purple-black, glossy, soft and juicy). Ripening is very uneven: one branch carries green, red and black fruit at the same time over 8–10 weeks. Cut lengthwise: a pale greenish-white core, flesh and juice deep purple-red that stains everything; a few small seeds.

Seeds: 3–4 mm, lens-shaped, smooth, pale yellow-brown.

Roots: of the cutting fine and white, then brown; of the grown tree thick, wandering, orange-brown roots, mostly shallow and spreading wider than the crown.
