Prunus persica (L.) Batsch var. platycarpa (Decne.) L.H.Bailey 'Saturn' — the flat "doughnut" peach (Rutgers NJF2, 1985; syn. 'Stark Saturn', 'Saturne', 'Donut'): a peach whose fruit is pressed flat top to bottom, white-fleshed, highly blushed red, with pink showy flowers. A deciduous fruit tree, grafted low on a peach seedling rootstock and trained as an open vase.
Stone and seedling: the stone (endocarp) small and flattened like the fruit: a round disc about 22 mm across and 19 mm along the fruit's axis, tan with pink tones, deeply and irregularly furrowed with sharp ridges and a keel round its edge. It germinates in spring after a cold winter; the seed leaves stay inside the stone underground (hypogeal); the shoot comes up bent in a hook; the seedling's stem green flushed red, its first leaves narrow and serrate.
Trunk and bark: a short trunk (about 50–60 cm to the first limbs), 10–14 cm across at five years; this year's shoots smooth, red on the sunny upper side and light green below, tiny pale lenticels; one-year shoots in winter wine-red to red-brown; two- to four-year wood red-brown greying, with tan horizontal lenticels (short dashes across the stem, 1 × 3 mm); the trunk grey-brown, smooth and faintly glossy when young, older rough, scaly and darker grey with horizontal bands of lenticels; a bead of amber gum at a wound.
Leaves: narrowly lanceolate, 15–18 cm long and 3.5–4 cm wide (about 4.5 : 1), widest a little below the middle, drawn out into a long fine point, the base wedge-shaped, the margin finely serrate; stalk 10–15 mm with two or three small dark red kidney-shaped glands where it meets the blade. Upper side glossy medium to dark green, underside paler and matt with a raised pale yellow-green midrib. Folded upward along the midrib into a shallow trough and curving down along their length so the tip hangs. Young leaves at the shoot tips yellow-green with a bronze-red flush; autumn leaves yellow. Alternate, crowded along the shoots, 3–4 cm apart.
Flowers: in early spring before the leaves, one or two beside each leaf bud along last year's shoots, nearly stalkless; showy (rosaceous) type, about 30 mm across: five round, slightly cupped medium rose-pink petals deepening toward the centre, a red-brown to purplish cup and five small sepals, about 25–30 stamens with pale pink filaments and yellow anthers, one green style. Winter flower buds fat, grey-silver and downy, in pairs beside a slim pointed leaf bud; swelling pink. In bloom every shoot is lined with pink.
Fruit: flat, like a doughnut or a small round cheese: about 6–6.5 cm across and only 3 cm high (twice as wide as high), round seen from above. The stalk end is broad and flat with a small indented cavity (about 13 mm wide, 6 mm deep) and an almost nonexistent stalk (2–3 mm): the fruit sits tight on the shoot. The opposite end (the eye) is a broad shallow dimple with a pale, dry, slightly cracked scar 10–20 mm across in its middle, closed (flesh covers the stone). The suture is very shallow. The skin is covered in fine short silvery down (a soft velvet sheen); its ground colour creamy white when ripe (greenish-white before), 70–85 % covered with a deep pinkish red (Nopal red) blush, darkest on the sunny side, freckled and streaked at its edge, the stalk cavity staying whitish green. Young fruit: just set, bright green and grey-velvety, small and rounder; then mid green and flat at half size; then pale green-white with the first red flush; then ripe. Cut across through its equator: white melting flesh, a thin red tinge round the stone, the small flat stone in the middle (freestone).
Roots: a peach seedling's roots: a short taproot soon branched, strong spreading reddish-brown laterals, mostly in the top 60 cm.
