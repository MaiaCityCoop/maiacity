# The episode format

What a maiaCITY film is, every time: **half devlog, half a life based on a true story.** We are building a city — a
better blueprint for how people can live — and every episode shows the building (the platform, the worlds, the coops,
the numbers that move) inside a cinematic story of the man building it. Samuel speaks in two voices, mixed: the voice
over the pictures, and talking straight to the camera. He is the character the viewer follows: a leader in the
middle of an *Aufbruch* — the energy of a movement setting out (Martin Luther King's "I have a dream", said by someone
who is already building it), never the lonely genius, never the guru.

Two sources set the two halves. Their transcripts are in `blog/day-00-test-story/sources/`.

## The meta story: the beginning of a new civilization (Samuel, 2026-11-03)

Every maiaCITY story is one step of one big story, and it is told inside it:

- **We are exploring a new way of living:** how humans will feed themselves healthily in the future, and with it a
  completely new way of civilization (self-sufficient, local, healthy, built and owned by the people who live in it).
- **First the game, then for real.** We build it all in a game first; then we build everything in the real world.
- **We are at the beginning.** These are the first days of the journey, and the viewer joins at the start and
  watches it grow. Say it as a beginning ("this is where it starts", "bare ground beside the first dome"), never as a
  list of what doesn't exist yet. **Never name a day** (Samuel, 2026-11-03): no "Day 1", no day number in a line, a
  title or on a card; it is the beginning, not one particular day.
- **Every subject is placed in the big picture.** A papaya is not a papaya: it is the first food of a food forest that
  will feed a city we are only starting to build. Name where the piece sits (the beginning, the game, the real world to come) and
  what it is for (how people will eat, live and thrive), in a line or two, early.
- **Epic and intimate, always** (`emotion.md`, "The series' signature journey"): a fresh start for how humans live,
  told through moments a person relates to closely: a morning, a meal, a child, a home.
- **The register is vision, not dread** (`arc.md`): the old way of eating and living is the *from*, shown clearly; the
  new civilization is the *to*, being built, step by step, in the open.
- **Assume zero background knowledge** (Samuel, 2026-11-03): every episode is someone's first. Say who we are and
  what we are doing in plain words early, and every term as what it does.
- **Never talk about AI, nor about code** (`SKILL.md`, law 7): the work is told as *we* and *I*; a mistake is what
  the viewer sees and what the real thing taught us, never a line that was wrong.

## Half one: the devlog (Homegrown, a farming game, Hamburg)

A solo developer's weekly devlog (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ld9qvxoaB6Y), built on a few devices that work
every week:

| Device | In the devlog | Why it holds |
|---|---|---|
| **The week's one goal, in the first breath** | "Start of a new week here in rainy Hamburg … today, finally, I'm going to be working on some gameplay": the farmers' market | One arching question ("will the market work?") opened in 15 seconds, with a place and a mood |
| **Plan → build → show** | Every segment: a bit of planning, the work, then the result on screen (test spheres that spawn on Sundays → real bunting) | Progress in pieces (`arc.md`), each piece seen working — the proof is the picture, not the claim |
| **The clock as the spine** | "It's a few days later", "8:00 on Tuesday morning", "half past two", "20 to 6, I'm getting some dinner" | Time stamps are free structure: the viewer always knows where they are, and the days move the story forward |
| **A deadline** | "Only two more days before I need to start editing … on Friday I'm going to the Alps" | A ticking clock turns a to-do list into tension (`retention.md`) |
| **Life between the work** | A run while the sun is out, a salad from the balcony (lettuce, tomatoes, spring onions, mint), the laptop on the sofa | Breath beats, and the person behind the work: the viewer likes *him*, not only the feature |
| **The reasons, said aloud** | Removing the clock's time (no day-night cycle, "3 am" looks strange); no stock-market graphs ("a casual game"); balance so the decisions matter | The viewer is let into the decisions: they think along, and they learn the craft |
| **Honest scope** | "The absolute bare bones this week", "next iteration", the upgrade "will have to wait" | Honesty earns trust, and every unfinished part is the next episode's hook |
| **The demo at the end** | The whole feature in one run, start to finish | The payoff: everything the episode built, seen working together |
| **The community** | "I'd love your feedback", the patrons thanked by name | The viewers are part of it — for us: the founders, by name |

## Half two: the cinematic vlog (LifeOfRiza, "how I make my videos")

Kariza's own account of how a LifeOfRiza video is made (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kza94XqkQQ8), adding to her
three rules in `scenes.md`:

| Principle | In her words | For us |
|---|---|---|
| **Write every idea down** | "Every time something pops into my head" — then pre-production only once it is strong enough | The Idea step: one pad per story, links and fragments, nothing lost (the Stories board) |
| **Pre-production is the hard part** | "If I do my pre-production well, filming goes smoothly and the editing is way easier" — an outline, bullet points or a script; a shot list; drawn shots on days of 30 | Journey → Hook → Writing before a frame is shot; the shot list and storyboard (`director`) |
| **Choose stories that carry a full arc** | Relevant to her now, a compelling case, "a full story arc in it"; a marathon or a transformation has one built in | Every episode is a step of a transformation already under way: the city being built |
| **Make people care: plead your case** | "Assume that nobody knows anything about you … sometimes making someone care is as simple as providing context" — her marathon film made the audience know her people first | Every episode stands alone: who Samuel is, what maiaCITY is, why it matters, said again in new words each time (context, `arc.md`) |
| **Style = inspiration × constraints** | Filming alone on a tripod, she shot "a ton of angles" — now her signature | Our constraints are our style: one man, one room, the 3D worlds as his second camera crew |
| **Intentional camera** | Not overshooting; "when I move the camera, there's always a reason for it" | Motivated moves only (`cinematographer`) |
| **Most of it is in camera** | Light, set, wardrobe colour, the palette — "all the little creative choices combined" | Art-direct the room and the worlds (`production-designer`, `gaffer`) |
| **Light from references** | Mood boards (Frame Set, Pinterest), one key light and a foam-board bounce for every A-roll | The CB60 as the key, a bounce, references per scene (`gaffer`, `neewer-cb60`) |
| **Effortless on the surface** | "I never want to take people out of the video. I just want them to live in the story." | No visible machinery: the technique serves the story |
| **Stepping stones** | "The stepping stones make the biggest difference, versus the huge leaps every once in a while" | One episode a day, each a little better — the journey itself is the content |

## The two together: the maiaCITY episode

| | Devlog half | Life half |
|---|---|---|
| What we see | The real work: code, the studio, the worlds, the coops, numbers that move, demos | Samuel's day: the room, Munich, the Isar, the run, the food, the people — re-enacted, shot like a film |
| Who speaks | Voice-over over screens and worlds; to the camera at the desk when he decides something | Voice-over over the life; to the camera while doing something else (Kariza's improv rule, `scenes.md`) |
| What it gives | Proof: it is really being built | Care: we want this man to make it |
| Its feeling | Momentum, competence, "look, it works" | Warmth, longing, the dream, the cost |

**The fixed order** (Samuel, 2026-11-03; `hook-writer`, "The fixed trailer"): the topic hook, then the fixed trailer
spoken by Samuel, *"This is the story of how one million humans build a hundred times better way of living: their own solarpunk city, from the ground up. First in a game, then for real."*, then the story, which keeps the hook's promise.

**Every story teaches** (Samuel, 2026-11-03; `SKILL.md`, law 6b): one clear insight from permaculture or food forest
design (how the plant lives, how the forest is planted and works), plus surprising true facts, each one a step of the
story and checked against sources.

**Only our own assets on screen** (Samuel, 2026-11-03): storyboards and films show only what we really have: the
domes and other buildings, the sandbox and asset-viewer UI, the plants, the biomes, the things, machines and actors in
play's library, and Samuel himself where he speaks. No props, places or people we haven't built; what the world can't
show is said, never faked (`movie-producer`, "Never promise what the world can't show").

**The shape of an episode** (scale by `structure.md`):

1. **Cold open, the topic hook (0–8 s), always two things:** the title (at most 7–8 words, on screen) and the hook
   intro (one or two spoken sentences about the topic, its question and promise), over the strongest true picture
   (`hook-writer`).
2. **The fixed trailer (about 10 s):** Samuel's line, the same every time, over the world (the sandbox, the domes,
   the city in its garden).
3. **The story begins:** where we stand (the beginning, bare ground) and the one goal that keeps the hook's promise.
3. **The work, in pieces:** plan → build → show, each piece seen working; the clock and the deadline as the spine;
   each piece a step, linked by *but* and *therefore* (`scenes.md`).
4. **Life between:** a breath beat of his day every few pieces — never filler, each one turns something (a doubt, a
   decision, a person).
5. **The low:** something fails or costs more than planned — honest scope (the devlog's "next iteration").
6. **The turn and the demo:** the day's result running, in the worlds, as one seen whole.
7. **The vision, said with conviction:** where this goes — the movement's voice, warm and certain, inviting.
8. **The founders:** thanked by name; what comes next (the next episode's open loop).

## The character

- **A leader setting out, not a lone hero.** "We are building a city" — the "we" includes the viewer. Every episode
  ends with an open door.
- **Positive, never naive.** The pain is real and named; the answer is being built, not hoped for (`SKILL.md`, full
  conviction).
- **A man you would follow because he is human.** His doubts, his food, his run, his room — the life half earns the
  right to the vision half.
- **The movement's cadence when the vision is spoken:** short lines, repetition, the dream in concrete pictures, a
  rise in the voice (`sound-designer`, voice takes).

## Rules this adds

1. Every episode is half devlog, half life — and the two halves cut into each other, never two blocks.
2. Every episode pleads the case again: no viewer knows us yet.
3. The clock is shown (time stamps, the days), and a deadline gives the tension.
4. Every piece of work is seen working; "I built X" without the picture of X is cut.
5. Every episode ends on the vision, the founders, and the next open loop.
